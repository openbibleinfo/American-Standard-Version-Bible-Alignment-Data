# Jeremiah 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A relative the ASV supplies where the Hebrew writes none is placed three ways in this chapter, and what the source writes decides which. An articleless attributive takes it with its own English and any copula: אֶרֶץ לֹא זְרוּעָה -> 'a land that was not sown' (2.2, rule 41). A headless clause takes it together with the supplied antecedent, headed on the verb: לֹא יוֹעִלוּ -> 'things that do not profit' (2.8), לוֹא יוֹעִיל -> 'that which doth not profit' (2.11), and the noun מִבְטַחַיִךְ recast as 'those in whom thou trustest' (2.37), all on rule 46's reasoning. But a finite clause merely juxtaposed to an explicit Hebrew noun licenses nothing, so the relative is added: 'a land that none passed through' (2.6), 'a wild ass that snuffeth up the wind' (2.24). A competing reading of 2.6 puts that 'that' on the resumptive suffix of בָּהּ, which 2.24 has no counterpart for; the chapter took added in both, and rule 55's instruction not to fold a supplied subordinator into a neighbouring rendering is why.
- The interrogative particle (הֲ / הַ, tagged Ti) is unrepresented at all five occurrences: 2.11, 2.17, 2.31 and 2.32 mark the question by inversion alone, so the fronted auxiliary — 'Hath', 'Hast', 'Have', 'Can' — belongs to the verb it inflects under rule 17, and 2.14's 'Is' is the copula rule 20 gives the verbless clause's predicate. The particle would take English only where the ASV spends a word on the question itself, as the corpus's four הֲ -> 'should' units do. All five BSB disagreements tagged Ti follow from this and are convention differences. Promoting this would want a corpus query on LEMMA=הַ MORPH=Ti to show how the unrepresented majority is treated.
- מִן compounded with a second particle and rendered by one English word is a single rule 30 unit headed on מִן: מִבְּלִי -> 'without' (2.15), מֵעַל -> 'from' (2.5), מֵאֵת -> 'From' (2.37). The last two were first aligned with מִן taking the word and the second preposition unrepresented, and were corrected in reconciliation. That a member's literal sense ('upon', 'with') is absorbed does not defeat fusion — rule 30's own בְּיַד -> 'by' and מִפְּנֵי -> 'because of' absorb theirs — while a second English word spent on one morpheme does, which is why מֵעוֹלָם -> 'of old time' (2.20) and בַּמַּחְתֶּרֶת -> 'breaking in' (2.34) are split and בְּעֵת -> 'when' (2.17) is not.
- Where an infinitive construct stands with no preposition before it and the ASV supplies a subordinator for the clause it heads, that word is added and is not folded onto the infinitive or its suffix: 'how' at 2.2, 'in that' as one unit at 2.17, 'that' at 2.19. Rules 48 and 52 hand a complementizer to a preposition that is actually present, and this chapter's 2.7 shows the counting rule at work in the other direction — two waws in the clause chain and two connectives written ('but', 'when'), so each waw takes one and nothing is added.
