# Luke 18 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where an indefinite τις or τινι stands, it carries the English indefinite determiner and the noun stands bare — Κριτής τις → *a* and *judge* and ἔν τινι πόλει → *a* and *city* at 18:2, *a certain* at 18:9, 18:18 and 18:35 — including where the ASV compresses *a certain* to a bare *a*. Where no indefinite pronoun stands, the supplied determiner is the noun's own rule 51 helper, as χήρα → *a widow* 18:3, Φαρισαῖος → *a Pharisee* 18:10 and ὄχλου → *a multitude* 18:36 have it. Giving the bare *a* to the noun and leaving τις unrepresented is the competing reading and is refused throughout the chapter.
- Where the ASV demotes a Greek finite verb to a subordinate English clause and no Greek conjunction stands to introduce it, the supplied introducer is folded onto that verb as a rule 7 helper rather than marked `added` under rule 86: κληρονομήσω → *to inherit* at 18:18, where the ASV promotes the participle ποιήσας to the matrix verb, and ποιήσω → *that I should do* at 18:41, a deliberative subjunctive. Rule 86 is read as scoped to a clause the ASV subordinates whole, not to one whose finite verb it demotes; marking such an introducer `added` remains defensible and both units say so.
- ἀναβλέπω heads on *sight* in all three of its units — *I may receive my sight* 18:41, *Receive thy sight* 18:42, *he received his sight* 18:43 — the English being a support-verb rendering whose complement glosses the -βλεπ- root under rules 17 and 91, with the supplied possessive riding along under rules 7 and 11. Its ἀνα- preverb has no separate English particle, so rule 106 splits nothing off. ἐγγίζω heads the same way on the word glossing its own root, *nigh* at 18:35 and *near* at 18:40, and not on the English light verb *drew* or *come*.
