# Deuteronomy 14 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Two-word English animal and bird names in this chapter's clean-and-unclean lists are headed by rule 31's tie-break, not by its subtraction test alone. Where the second word is a class noun the ASV also writes for another Hebrew word of that class, the modifier is the ASV's specifier and the head stays on the class noun: *wild goat* (v.5) on *goat*, which v.4 writes alone for `עֵז`; *gier eagle* (v.12) on *eagle*, written alone for `נֶשֶׁר` in the same verse; *night hawk* (v.15) on *hawk*, written alone for `נֵץ` in the same verse; and *little owl*, *great owl*, *horned owl* (v.16) all on *owl*, which no verse writes bare but which stands as the class noun across three coordinate members for three distinct Hebrew words, on the JOB.4.10-11 lion pattern. Where the second word is instead a generic the ASV would write for anything, the head is the modifier: *abominable thing* (v.3) on *abominable*, *creeping things* (v.19) on *creeping*, *strong drink* (v.26) on *strong*. *sea mew* (v.15) heads on *mew* by the plain subtraction test, *sea* being the specifier on a word that already names the bird. Heading each of the three owls on its modifier was the live alternative and is named in v.16's note; the chapter took the class-noun reading uniformly.
