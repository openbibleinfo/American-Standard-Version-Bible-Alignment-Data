# Judges 17 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Relative recastings in this chapter: the relative goes to the morpheme it actually realizes, and to the clause's waw only when no morpheme does. A pronominal suffix takes whose (17.1); an explicit subject pronoun whose referent is the antecedent takes who (17.7); the waw takes who where nothing in the clause points at the antecedent (17.4, 17.5) and takes which in 17.2, where אַתְּ holds the thou written for it and the relative points at the silver. A waw whose relative another morpheme has taken is unrepresented (17.1, 17.7).
- היה or a verbless clause with dative לְ + suffix and a לְכֹהֵן predicate occurs three times, and the suffix takes the possessive the ASV writes on priest each time: his in 17.5 and 17.12, my in 17.13, where the same suffix is also realized as the I of I have and both words are its one discontinuous unit headed on I (rule 18). The dative לְ is unrepresented throughout (rules 40, 43); the resultative לְ on כֹהֵן is unrepresented where the ASV writes no preposition (17.5, 17.10 twice, 17.12) and takes the written to in 17.13 (rule 49).
- אֶלֶף וּמֵאָה -> eleven hundred is one noncompositional unit of אֶלֶף and מֵאָה with the intervening waw unrepresented (17.2, 17.3): the English compound is a rendering no morpheme owns a part of, no and stands between the numerals (rule 42), and the source head goes to מֵאָה, whose lexeme hundred writes, rather than to the first-token fallback (rule 6).
- מִלֵּא אֶת־יַד -> consecrated: the verb and יָד share one unit headed on the verb, and the object marker is unrepresented (17.5, 17.12). The ASV margin's literal gloss filled the hand of corroborates the fusion and decides nothing (rules 7, 38).
- The italic pieces the ASV supplies after a numeral of silver names the unit the money is counted in, not the numeral's own lexeme, so it is added under rule 33's supplied-measure-noun clause rather than joining the numeral under rule 31 (17.2, 17.3, 17.4, 17.10). The same reading gives the italic a place its own added unit after find (17.8, 17.9).
- בֵּית לֶחֶם arrives as two Np tokens and each takes the English word written for it (Beth, lehem) rather than fusing; the following יְהוּדָה takes the qualifying judah (17.7, 17.8, 17.9).
