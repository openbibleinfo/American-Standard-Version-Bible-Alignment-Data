# Hebrews 13 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **HEB.13.17** — Where **no** Greek preposition is expressed, rule 36 is out of scope and the English preposition is placed by following the **lemma's own corpus units**, not by any general test. `ἀνέχομαι` has four and they all keep the particle on the verb, so 13.22 reads `ἀνέχεσθε` → *bear with* whole and its article takes a bare *the*, matching 2CO.11.19's token-identical frame; `ἐπιζητέω` likewise gives 13.14 *we seek after*. `ἡγέομαι` has none, so 13.7, 13.17 and 13.24 follow the nearest same-shape precedent, 2CO.1.24's `κυριεύομεν` → *we have lordship* / `πίστεως` → *over faith*, and give *over you* to the pronoun — the reading two of the three verses already had. Both directions are live in the corpus and each unit's note says so: 2CO.11.2 gives a bare case to the pronoun unhedged, while 1PE.5.3 `κατακυριεύοντες` → *lording it over* keeps it on the verb unhedged. Do NOT decide this by the verb's **compound prefix**: that carve-out is not the corpus's test, EPH.5.7 `συμμέτοχοι` giving its *with* to the pronoun though `συμ-` stands and the uncompounded GAL.2.7 `πεπίστευμαι` → *I had been intrusted with* keeping its own particle. Rule 21 does not reach the question either, allocating a case preposition between an article and its noun rather than between a noun phrase and the verb governing it.
- **HEB.13.1** — Where one Greek compound is rendered by a multiword English phrase, the head follows the **English phrase's lexical nucleus**, which is rule 17 and not a prefix-versus-stem test. `φιλαδελφία` → *love of the brethren* heads on *love* at 13.1, `φιλοξενία` → *to show love unto strangers* on *love* at 13.2, and `ἀφιλάργυρος` → *free from the love of money* on *love* at 13.5 (rule 84 getting the head off *free* first); `εὐαρεστέω` → *is well pleased* heads on *pleased* at 13.16 and `εὐάρεστος` → *is well pleasing* on *pleasing* at 13.21; and `εὐποιΐα` → *to do good* heads on *good* at 13.16, rule 17's light-verb clause putting the head on the meaning-bearing complement as 3JN.1.11's `ἀγαθοποιῶν` → *that doeth good* and `κακοποιῶν` → *that doeth evil* do unhedged. In every one of these the head is the nucleus, so *brethren*, *strangers*, *money* and *do* are the formal complements or empty support words rule 17 forbids choosing. Do NOT read this as a rule that a `φιλ-` prefix takes the head and an `εὐ-` prefix does not: the corpus refuses that test, heading `δυσνόητος` → *hard to be understood* on *hard* at 2PE.3.16 and `ἀγαθοποιῶν` → *that doeth good* on *good*, both unhedged. Where the lemma itself has corpus units, rule 91 decides it directly and no family query is needed — `φιλαδελφία` has three, at 1PE.1.22 and 2PE.1.7 twice.
