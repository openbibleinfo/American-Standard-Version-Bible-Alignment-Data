# Joshua 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The doubled distributive `אִישׁ אֶחָד אִישׁ אֶחָד מִשָּׁבֶט` (JOS.4.2, JOS.4.4), rendered *out of every tribe a man*, gives the distributive *every* to the second `אֶחָד` under rule 41 and *a man* with its indefinite article to the first `אִישׁ` under rule 15, leaving the remaining `אֶחָד` and `אִישׁ` `unrepresented` under rule 22. The chapter takes the pairs in that order in both verses; the mirror allocation is equally available and is named in each verse's note.
- In the catechetical formula `מָה הָאֲבָנִים הָאֵלֶּה` → *What mean these stones* (JOS.4.6, JOS.4.21) the *mean* is its own `added` unit and `מָה` keeps only *What*. It is not rule 20's construction-supplied copula: that rule's own example writes *is* (*What man is this*), while *mean* predicates meaning of the stones and is the supplied lexical verb rule 20 expressly leaves `added`. The `לְ` of `לָכֶם` still takes the *by* the ASV writes in JOS.4.6, a present morpheme taking its English before a supplied-word analysis does.
- `תַּמּוּ … לַעֲבוֹר` → *were clean passed over* (JOS.4.1, JOS.4.11) puts the adverb *clean* on the finite `תָּמַם` and the whole *were passed over* on the infinitive construct, discontinuous around it under rule 25, with the `לְ` `unrepresented` because the ASV writes no function word for it. The competing reading — *were clean* as the finite verb's own rendering — is named in both verses.
- `לִפְנֵי` fuses whole even where the ASV spends four English words on it — `לִפְנֵי הָעָם` → *in the presence of the people* (JOS.4.11) — because no part of that compound is the `לְ`'s own relation, and rule 30 reads its condition as one rendering rather than one token. `בְּעֵינֵי כָּל־יִשְׂרָאֵל` → *in the sight of all Israel* (JOS.4.14) splits instead, because the `בְּ`'s own locative sense is what the written *in* renders and the *of* is the chain's relation on the rectum. Read whether the English preposition states the Hebrew preposition's own relation before fusing or splitting.
