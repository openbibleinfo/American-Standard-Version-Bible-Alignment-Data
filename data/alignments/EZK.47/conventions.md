# Ezekiel 47 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Boundary-formula closing. The refrain we-'et pe'at X -> 'This is the X side' (47.17, 47.18, 47.19) writes no conjunction, no subject and no verb: the waw and the object marker are each their own unrepresented unit, the supplied 'This' is added under rule 17, and pe'ah as the clause's predicate carries the construction-supplied 'is the' under rules 15 and 20. Where the Hebrew does write its own demonstrative, as zo't pe'at-yam in 47.20, 'This' is aligned to it and nothing is added. Read the clause for a demonstrative morpheme before placing the word, and do not reach for the object marker to carry it.
- derek in this chapter is always rendered 'by the way' (47.2 three times, 47.15), so the construct noun keeps its own English word 'way' and the supplied 'by' joins it under rule 33, while the rectum keeps the relational 'of' under rule 19 and a present article takes the written 'the' (47.15) where an absent one leaves it to the noun's expression (47.2). Rule 19's derek -> 'toward' clause is not engaged anywhere here: it governs a construct whose whole rendering is the preposition, and none of these is.
- The measuring walk's depth phrases - me 'ophsayim (47.3), mayim birkayim (47.4), me motnayim (47.4), me sahu (47.5) - all give the entire supplied relational frame ('that were to the', 'to swim in') to the second noun, headed on that noun. The rule cited follows MACULA's morphology, rule 19 where the first noun is construct and rule 33 where it is absolute and the second stands in apposition, but the unit boundaries and the head are the same in both, so the morphological difference is not a reason to divide them differently.
- Directive he in the boundary list is fused with its host wherever the ASV writes a single English word for the pair, including where that word is not itself directional - qadimah -> 'east' (47.18), temanah -> 'south' (47.19) - and is marked unrepresented only where another morpheme has already taken the word that carries the direction, as lipe'at saponah -> 'On the north side' and lebo' sedadah -> 'unto the entrance of Zedad' (both 47.15). Rule 32 is read here as a one-English-word test, not a test of whether the English word is directional; the chapter is uniform on it and 47.1's 'eastward' is the same test coming out the other way.
