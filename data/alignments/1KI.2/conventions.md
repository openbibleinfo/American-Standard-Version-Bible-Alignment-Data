# 1 Kings 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **1KI.2.16** — hashiv panim (heshiv et-pene, 'turn away the face' = refuse): the analysis follows the ASV's surface rendering, not the Hebrew. Where the ASV collapses the idiom into a single English word (deny, 1KI.2.16 s011+s013, 1KI.2.20 s012+s014 and s027+s029), rule 30 applies: the verb and panim form one aligned unit headed on the verb, at medium confidence, and panim is never left unrepresented beside the rendered verb. Where the ASV supplies two English words for it (say ... nay, 1KI.2.17), rule 30's one-word condition is not met, so rule 2 splits it: the finite verb takes he will say and panim takes nay. In every case the object marker et is its own unrepresented unit (rule 16) and the pronominal suffix takes the English object pronoun (rules 18 and 40), never folded into the verb's unit.
- **1KI.2.25** — Fixed prepositional expressions the ASV renders with a single English word are one aligned unit under rule 30, headed on the morpheme whose sense that English word realizes -- the leading preposition -- at medium confidence, and the second morpheme is never left unrepresented beside the rendered one: beyad -> by (1KI.2.25 s006+s007), mippene -> from (1KI.2.7 s023+s024), me-im -> from (1KI.2.33 s034+s035). MACULA's tag on the second morpheme does not change this. me-im is preposition + preposition, while mippene and beyad are preposition + a full noun in construct (paneh, yad), yet all three behave alike, because rule 30's condition is that the ASV collapse the expression into one English word, not that the expression be a listed compound preposition. Where the whole construct chain collapses, the of that rule 19 would give the nomen rectum disappears with it (by Benaiah, not by the hand of Benaiah). Where the ASV supplies a separate English word for the second morpheme (from before), rule 30's one-word condition is not met and rule 2 splits it.
