# Philippians 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **PHP.3.19** — A supplied English copula in a verbless clause rides with the predicate, not with the subject: 'whose end is perdition' puts *is* on apoleia, 'whose god is the belly' puts *is* on koilia with the intervening *the* left on its own article, and 'whose glory is in their shame' puts *is* on en. This settles the chapter's one split: v19's three parallel relative clauses had analysed the same construction two ways. Elsewhere the chapter already attached every supplied copula to the predicate (v1 'is irksome', 'it is safe'; v15 'are perfect'; v21 'that it may be conformed'), and rule 31's 'the copula goes with the preposition' is the same instinct inside a supplied relative clause (v6, v9, v13). Not promoted to the contract: the finished corpus contradicts itself here, with EPH.5.23 putting the copula on the predicate noun and REV.21.21 putting it on the subject.
- **PHP.3.12** — A name the ASV's Greek carried but the projection does not is `added`, and the note must name the textual unit and why the fallback dropped it: 'by Christ Jesus' at PHP.3.12 excludes the bare Χριστοῦ of r00-base, but SBLGNT.PHP.3.12.v007 stays underdetermined because r01 (Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ) and r02 (τοῦ Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ) both remain, so the projection carries a base fallback the evidence itself excluded and no token answers to *Jesus*. JHN.10.13 is the corpus precedent for the shape and the standard for the note. Not promoted to the contract: rules 5 and 12 already settle the status, and what this adds is the citation a later reader needs, which is a note convention rather than a rule.
- A supplied English nominal completing a phrase a Greek article governs belongs to the article, headed on the nominal, whatever the complement's class, and is neither the complement's nor `added`: τὰ ἐπίγεια → *earthly things* (3:19) and τὰ πάντα → *all things* (3:8, 3:21) give *things* to the article and a bare *earthly* or *all* to the complement, and the identity pronoun αὐτός is no different — τὰ αὐτά → *the … things* / *same* (3:1) and τῷ αὐτῷ → *by that … rule* / *same* (3:16), the italics on *rule* being evidence only, since the ASV's own text lacked κανόνι and the selected reading agrees with it. The contrast is anarthrous Greek, where no article competes and the completion stays with the complement: ἄλλος → *other man* heads on *other* (3:4), and the anarthrous πάντα → *all things* heads on *all* in the same verse as the articular τὰ πάντα (3:8). Where the ASV supplies no nominal the article keeps only what was written, so Τὸ λοιπόν → *Finally* leaves it `unrepresented` (3:1).
- A supplied English predicate frame — complementizer, subject pronoun and copula together — rides with the standing token it is predicated of and is not `added`: *that they are* goes to the predicate noun ἐχθρούς (3:18) and *that it may be* to the predicate adjective σύμμορφον (3:21), the article keeping only its own determiner. It is `added` only where no token stands to be predicated of, as at 3:13, where the ASV supplies both subject and lexical verb over an accusative object — *one thing I do* — and ἕν is that verb's object rather than its predicate. The test is what the supplied verb predicates of, not whether its English subject is supplied: at 3:18 the subject is supplied too.
