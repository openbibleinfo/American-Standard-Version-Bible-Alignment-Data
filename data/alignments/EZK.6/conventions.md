# Ezekiel 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The formula אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה is rendered *the Lord Jehovah* throughout the chapter (6.3 twice, 6.11). The ASV writes no possessive, so the suffix on אֲדֹנָי is `unrepresented` under rule 22, and the *the* standing before *Lord* is a supplied article joining אֲדֹנָי under rule 29. In דְּבַר אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה -> *the word of the Lord Jehovah* (6.3) the chain holds two supplied *the*s: the construct דְּבַר takes the first and אֲדֹנָי the second together with the relational *of*. BSB aligns the suffix in all three places; that is its convention and not an error here.
- A preposition prefixed to פָּנֶה or תָּוֶךְ is settled by counting the ASV's words rather than by the idiom. לִפְנֵי -> *before* (6.4, 6.5) and בְּתוֹךְ -> *among* (6.13) are one English rendering and fuse `noncompositional` under rule 30, headed on the lexeme-bearing morpheme; בְּתוֹךְ -> *in the midst of you* (6.7) and בִּפְנֵיהֶם -> *in their own sight* (6.9) spend a word on each morpheme, so rule 2 splits them and the preposition takes only its own *in*. The chapter reads אֶל־חִנָּם -> *in vain* (6.10) the same way, giving *in* to אֶל and *vain* to חִנָּם; fusing the pair as one adverbial rendering headed on חִנָּם is the competing reading, rejected because a word stands for each morpheme.
- Where a relative clause carries a resumptive שָׁם and the ASV writes a single word for the pair - אֲשֶׁר ... שָׁם -> *whither* (6.9), *where* (6.13) - the relative takes that word and שָׁם is `unrepresented` under rule 22. The finite verb stands between the two morphemes in both verses, so rule 30's contiguity condition for a fused expression is unmet and they are not made one unit; reading *whither* as a rule 35 fusion carrying both morphemes' sense across the verb is the competing analysis. BSB aligns שָׁם in both places.
- Where coordinated verbs share one written English auxiliary or modal, the first verb's unit takes it and each later verb takes only the English written for it: 6.6 *may be laid waste*(יֶחֶרְבוּ) ... *made desolate*(יֶאְשְׁמוּ) and *may be broken*(נִשְׁבְּרוּ) ... *cease*(נִשְׁבְּתוּ), 6.14 *I will stretch out*(נָטִיתִי) ... *make*(נָתַתִּי). The auxiliary cannot be partitioned twice, and giving it to the verb it stands before follows rules 42 and 47's counting of what the ASV actually wrote. A copula the construction supplies to a later predicate is a separate matter and stays with that predicate under rule 20, as at 6.12 s019 *is besieged*.
