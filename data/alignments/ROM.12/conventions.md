# Romans 12 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The charismata list supplies an English imperative predicate for three of its items, and all three are `added` whole: ROM.12.6 let us prophesy, ROM.12.7 let us give ourselves, ROM.12.8 let him do it. The prepositional phrase standing in each clause (κατὰ τὴν ἀναλογίαν, ἐν τῇ διακονίᾳ, ἐν ἁπλότητι) already has its own complete English and is the supplied verb's adjunct rather than the predicate it predicates of, so rule 62 does not reach it, and the noun of each item is spent on its own English (rule 103). The contrasts inside this chapter are ROM.12.9, where the supplied Let ... be folds onto the expressed predicate adjective ἀνυπόκριτος under rule 53, and ROM.12.18 and ROM.12.19, where the supplied lieth and belongeth fold onto ἐξ ὑμῶν and Ἐμοί because those are what the supplied verb predicates of (rules 62, 63).
- φρονέω and its compounds are headed by rule 91's operational test throughout. The head is mind wherever the ASV writes a mind-frame — ROM.12.16 Be of the same mind and Set your mind on, which match COL.3.2 and PHP.2.2 word for word, the corpus heading this lemma on mind, minded, mindest or thought and never on think. ROM.12.3's φρονεῖν -> to think and so to think head on think only because no other word stands in the unit; σωφρονεῖν -> to think soberly heads on soberly, the corpus spending sober and soberly on that stem, as rule 91's own σωφρονήσατε -> be ye of sound mind heads on sound; and ὑπερφρονεῖν heads on think with the preverb realized in more highly under rule 106.
