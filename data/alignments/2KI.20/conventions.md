# 2 Kings 20 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A cognate accusative noun whose lexeme the ASV writes once in the finite verb is the unrendered occurrence and is its own `unrepresented` unit under rule 22, while an attributive standing on it takes whatever adverb the ASV writes for it: `וַיֵּבְךְּ ... בְּכִי גָדוֹל` → *wept sore* at 20.3 gives *wept* to the verb, *sore* to `גָדוֹל`, and nothing to `בְּכִי`. Rule 30 does not fuse the pair, because *sore* carries the magnitude and not the weeping. BSB attaches the noun to its own phrase and disagrees.
- A negative and a following `דָּבָר` that the ASV renders by the single word *nothing* share one unit under rules 30 and 35, headed on the negative, with the clause's verb keeping its own English: 20.13 and 20.15 give *there was* / *there is* to `הָיָה` beside the fused *nothing*, and 20.17 gives *shall be left* to `יִוָּתֵר`. Rule 35 names `כֹּל` and `מְאוּמָה`; a bare `דָּבָר` standing under the negation is read on the same terms, since the one English word carries both the negation and the thing-ness. Where the ASV instead writes a word for each, rule 2 splits them as usual.
- Where `אָנָּה` and `נָא` both stand in one entreaty (20.3) and the ASV writes two entreaty renderings, each particle takes one: `אָנָּה` takes the full formula *I beseech thee*, headed on *beseech*, and `נָא` takes the enclitic *now* standing on the imperative. Rule 30's sentence assigning *I beseech thee* to `נָא` governs a clause where `נָא` stands alone; where a second entreaty particle is written, rule 11's priority gives the stronger interjection the stronger formula. The competing pairing is named in the verse's own note.
