# Lamentations 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- ADONAY (Ncmpc + Sp1cs) is split throughout: the noun takes the ASV's supplied 'the' (3.31, 3.36, 3.37) or the vocative 'O' under rule 36 (3.58), and the 1cs suffix is unrepresented in all four places, because the ASV never writes 'my Lord'. The competing reading fuses both morphemes under rule 30 into the single English word 'Lord'; it was not taken, since 'Lord' carries nothing of the suffix and rule 35's test is what the English word carries. BSB reports all four suffixes as represented, which is this convention difference and not an error.
- Where the ASV's verb reaches its object through an English preposition and the Hebrew has no object marker and no preposition morpheme to carry that word, the preposition stays inside the verb's unit and the suffix takes only the pronoun: 'call for help' (3.8), 'wait for ... him' (3.25), 'called upon ... thee' (3.55, 3.57), 'rose up against ... me' (3.62). Rule 16 hands such a preposition to H853 only where the marker is present; where it is not, nothing else licenses the word. The competing reading, which gives the preposition to the suffix, is named in the notes at 3.25 and 3.62.
- A supplied relative pronoun introducing an asyndetic relative clause joins that clause's finite verb and heads on the lexical verb: 'the man that hath seen' (3.1), 'the soul that seeketh' (3.25), 'the day that I called upon' (3.57). Rules 41 and 46 do not reach these, since no article, participle or asher is present to take the relative. A supplied subordinating conjunction that is not a relative in an argument slot goes the other way and is its own added unit: the 'when' of 3.37 carries a relation no morpheme has. Not promoted to the contract: corpus units in Isaiah put the same supplied relative on a present negative instead (lo as 'that not' ISA.63.8 and ISA.65.1, 'which not' ISA.64.3), so the placement is not settled outside this chapter.
- An English 'and' standing between two separately rendered items that no waw joins is added (3.14 between the two predicates, 3.60 and 3.61 between the two objects). An 'and' written inside a single idiomatic English rendering of one morpheme is not: shub rendered 'again and again' (3.3) is one unit, because no part of that phrase belongs to any other morpheme and dropping 'and again' would leave English the ASV wrote unlicensed. Rule 42's surplus-'and' clause is about numerals; this is the same reasoning drawn wider, with rule 30's 'one English rendering, not one English token' marking the boundary.
- A determiner the ASV supplies for a noun that has no morpheme to license it joins that noun's unit and is never added, beyond the 'a' rule 15 already covers: a possessive where no suffix morpheme is present ('his cheek' 3.30, 'our hands' 3.41) and a quantifier ('any intermission' 3.49). This does not reach a supplied object pronoun standing inside a verb phrase, which stays added under rule 44 (3.2, 3.5, 3.20, 3.28, 3.37, 3.66); the test is whether the supplied word is the noun's determiner or the verb's argument.
