# Ruth 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- English kinship compounds render one Hebrew noun and are one unit: כַּלָּה → daughter in law, daughters in law (vv. 6, 7, 8, 22), חֲמוֹת → mother in law (v. 14), יְבֶמֶת → sister in law (v. 15, twice). The words in law stand inside that noun's unit — never split off, never added, never given to a neighbouring morpheme — and the head is the first word under rule 31's subtraction, since in law names nothing on its own. A suffix on the noun takes its own possessive under rule 18 and stands outside the compound.
- An English particle written with שׁוּב belongs to that verb: turn again (vv. 11, 12), is gone back (v. 15), hath brought me home again (v. 21). None of the bare verbs the ASV chooses for it — turn, go, bring — writes the returning, so rule 44's first condition is unmet and the particle is never surplus. Contrast הָלַךְ in v. 21, where went already writes the going and the supplied out states a direction no morpheme in the clause writes, so that word is its own added unit. This is the line the chapter draws between rule 44's kept particle and its surplus one; yatsa keeps its forth in vv. 7 and 13 on the first ground.
- The distributive אִשָּׁה of vv. 8 and 9 is rendered by the whole partitive phrase each of you, which is that noun's own rendering, headed on each. The of belongs to no other morpheme and neither word is added; rule 15's partitive-quantifier sentence does not reach it, because a morpheme standing in the clause licenses the expression.
