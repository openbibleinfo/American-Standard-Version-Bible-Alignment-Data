# Joshua 10 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where a demonstrative pronoun stands written in the phrase - ha-melakim ha-elleh, ha-yom ha-hu, ha-yom ha-zeh - that pronoun takes the ASV's these, those, this or that, and both the noun's own article and the demonstrative's agreement article are unrepresented. This is rule 41's limit read with a written demonstrative in the place its suffix example occupies: a present morpheme licenses the determiner before the article can. Taken in JOS.10.14, 16, 22, 23, 24, 27, 28, 35 and 42. Name the competing reading in the note: the corpus records four noncompositional ha+hu -> that units, which fuse the agreement article with the demonstrative under rule 30 instead, and that reading is defensible on the one-English-word condition. Two riders this chapter needed. Where the phrase is the rectum of a construct chain and the ASV therefore writes a second determiner - the necks of these kings, JOS.10.24 - the noun's article takes that written the, fronted to the construct under rule 29, and only the agreement article goes unrepresented. And an ordinary attributive that is not a demonstrative is rule 47's plain case rather than this one: ha-yom ha-sheni -> the second day (JOS.10.32) gives the written the to the first article and leaves the agreement article unrepresented.
- The conquest refrain le-fi-hereb -> with the edge of the sword (JOS.10.28, 30, 32, 35, 37, 39) is divided the same way in all six: le takes with, the construct pi takes the edge with a supplied the, and hereb takes of the sword with the second supplied the. No article morpheme stands anywhere in the phrase, so neither the is fronted from anything and each noun carries the one written before it; do not give both to the construct or read the second as part of an English compound.
- lo hish'ir sarid -> he left none remaining (JOS.10.28, 30, 37, 39, 40): the negative takes none, the noun sarid takes remaining, and the verb keeps he left with its subject pronoun under rule 17. Do not read none remaining as one rendering of sarid. Where the clause is 'ad bilti hish'ir-lo sarid -> until he had left him none remaining (JOS.10.33), 'ad keeps until and bilti takes the none, the ASV having spent a word on each, so rule 30's one-English-word condition is unmet; the dative le is unrepresented under rule 40 and its suffix takes him.
- A relative the ASV supplies is placed by what stands in the Hebrew, and this chapter has both cases. For a finite clause merely juxtaposed to a written antecedent - we-ha-seridim sardu -> the remnant which remained (JOS.10.20) - the which is added under rule 55, since rule 46 does not reach such a clause and the article has already taken the phrase's the. Where instead a determined noun is recast verbally - kol ha-neshamah -> all that breathed (JOS.10.40) - every English word must come from the two morphemes present, so the article takes the that under rule 41 and the noun takes breathed.
