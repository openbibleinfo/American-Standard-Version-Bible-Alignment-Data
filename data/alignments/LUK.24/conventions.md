# Luke 24 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- πᾶς governing a substantival article before a participle: where the ASV writes a generic noun and a separate relativizer, the quantifier keeps a bare 'all', the article takes the generic noun, and the participle takes the relativizer and its verb. πάντων τῶν συμβεβηκότων τούτων → 'all these things which had happened' (24.14) and πάντα τὰ γεγραμμένα → 'all things ... which are written' (24.44). Rule 80's closing clause is the shape and rule 102 scopes rule 17's generic-noun clause to anarthrous Greek, so the supplied nominal is not the quantifier's where an article stands. Rule 90 does not reach these, writing more than one English word for the article-plus-participle pair.
- The intensive αὐτός in a temporal phrase takes 'very' and the article takes the supplied demonstrative 'that' under rule 60: ἐν αὐτῇ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ → 'that very day' (24.13) and αὐτῇ τῇ ὥρᾳ → 'that very hour' (24.33). Where a Greek preposition stands but the ASV writes none, as at 24.13, the preposition is its own unrepresented unit under rule 36; the anarthrous 24.33 shows that the ASV's phrasing and not the preposition decides.
- An English particle realizing a Greek preverb stays inside the verb's unit and the head is the verb word, not the particle: ἐξήγαγεν → 'led out' (24.50), ἐξαποστέλλω → 'send forth' (24.49), ἀνεφέρετο → 'was carried up' (24.51), συζητεῖν → 'questioned together' (24.15), εἰσῆλθεν → 'went in' (24.29). The same holds for a particle belonging to the English verb rather than to a prefix — 'delivered up' (24.7, 24.20), 'rise again' (24.7, 24.46), 'rolled away' (24.2).
- ἐγένετο in the narrative frame takes the whole 'it came to pass' as one unit headed on 'came' (24.4, 24.15, 24.30, 24.51, and 'came to pass' at 24.21), and the governing ἐν τῷ + infinitive gives the English clause-introducer — 'while' or 'when' — to the preposition with the article unrepresented under rule 61. The accusative subject the infinitive expresses is its own unit on the pronoun (24.4, 24.15, 24.30, 24.51).
