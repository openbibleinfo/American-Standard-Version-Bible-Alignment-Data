# Ezekiel 34 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- רעה with a bare מִרְעֶה: where the ASV reaches the verb's complement through a supplied preposition, that preposition belongs to the verb's unit under rule 44 and not to the noun under rule 33, because מִרְעֶה after רעה is the verb's own complement rather than an adverbial accusative. Applied at 34.14 (on fat pasture shall they feed) and 34.18 (to have fed upon the good pasture); the same reading gives of to the verb in 34.18's to have drunk of the clear waters and in 34.15's will be the shepherd of my sheep. Where a Hebrew preposition or an object marker is present it takes the word instead, as ב does at 34.14 and 34.18 and אֶל at 34.13 and 34.14.
- בֵּין X לְ Y (34.17, 34.22): the לְ of the second member takes the ASV's and, on rule 26's reasoning that the morpheme standing where the connective stands is what licenses it; the בֵּין keeps between and nothing is added. Where the phrase runs on into further לְ members that the ASV writes as bare coordinated nouns (34.17 לָאֵילִים, לָעַתּוּדִים), those לְ are unrepresented under rule 40 and the waw takes the and written for it.
- מִבְּלִי רֹעֶה (34.5) is read exactly as מֵאֵין רֹעֶה (34.8): the privative מִן takes the complementizing because and בְּלִי takes the negation together with its presentative frame, there was no, headed on the negation. Rule 48's last clause names אַיִן by lemma, but בְּלִי is likewise a present negative morpheme and takes its English before a supplied reading does; the pair splits rather than fusing because the ASV has spent words on both, so rule 30's מֵאֵין -> without condition is unmet.
- The אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה formula (34.2, 34.8, 34.10, 34.11, 34.15, 34.17, 34.20, 34.30, 34.31 and the נְאֻם variants): אֲדֹנָי takes the supplied the Lord under rule 15, its 1cs suffix is unrepresented because the ASV writes no possessive answering to it, and יְהוִה takes Jehovah. BSB represents that suffix in all nine places; the difference is conventional and is not a review candidate.
- An appositional even. Where the ASV writes even before a phrase whose every morpheme already holds its own English and no object marker stands on it, the word is added under rule 16's limit: 34.2's say unto them, even to the shepherds leaves it added, the appositive's own לְ having taken to. Where even instead stands on an emphatic pronoun doubling one already rendered, it is what English spends on that pronoun and joins its unit headed on the pronoun: הִנְנִי אָנִי -> Behold, I, even I at 34.11 and 34.20 gives even I to אָנִי. The subtraction test rules 16 and 31 use is what separates them, since dropping the word at 34.2 diminishes no morpheme's rendering while dropping it at 34.11 and 34.20 removes the whole contribution of a pronoun whose only work is emphasis. Competing reading: mark those two added as well, on the ground that I alone already realizes אָנִי.
