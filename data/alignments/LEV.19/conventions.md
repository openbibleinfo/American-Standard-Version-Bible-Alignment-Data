# Leviticus 19 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The chapter's closing refrain. Where the clause is the bare ani YHWH 'eloheykem, the name is the predicate and takes the construction-supplied copula (I am Jehovah your God), the explicit pronoun taking only its own I under rule 20; this is the reading in 19.3, 4, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 25, 28, 30, 31, 32, 34, 36 and 37. Where an adjectival predicate stands in the same clause, that predicate takes the copula instead and the name is left a bare Jehovah: 19.2's ki qadosh 'ani YHWH 'eloheykem is read I Jehovah your God (subject) am holy, with am on qadosh. Read which side is the predicate before placing the copula; the refrain's shape alone does not decide it. In every occurrence the construct 'elohey takes a bare God with no of, because the ASV writes a possessive for the suffix rather than an of-phrase (rule 18), and 19.12's shem 'eloheykha is the contrasting case where a written construct chain does put of on the rectum.
- Rule 35 fusion is applied throughout this chapter by asking what the single English word carries, not by which connective lemma stands in the clause. Beyond the ordinary waw plus lo' rendered neither or nor, 19.29's we-lo' tizneh is read as one lest, which carries both the connective and the negation, and 19.20's 'o ... lo' nittan is read as one nor, which carries both the disjunction and the negation, on the reasoning rule 14 gives for reading 'o exactly as a waw and rule 35 gives for 'im lo' as for we-lo'. In each the competing reading was to give the word to the connective alone and mark the negative unrepresented; the fusion was preferred because the negation is what distinguishes lest from so and nor from or. Where the ASV instead writes a word for each morpheme, as at 19.17 and at 19.20's first clause, the pair is split; and where no coordinator stands at all, the negative carries neither by itself (19.16, 19.26).
- At 19.23 kol 'ets ma'akhal is read all manner of trees for food with the whole quantifier phrase all manner of belonging to kol as one rendering, and 'ets keeping only trees. The competing reading, giving all to kol and manner of trees to the noun as a generic expanding it, is what rule 35's kol-mela'khah lo' ta'asu example produces, but that example is decided by the fusion having consumed kol's own English and left manner of nowhere else to go. Here kol keeps a written all, and English all manner of is a fixed quantifying expression of which no part modifies the noun's class, so nothing is transferred to the noun. Read whether the kol still holds an English word of its own before dividing such a phrase.
