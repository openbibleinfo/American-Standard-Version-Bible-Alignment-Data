# 2 Kings 13 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The two-token name בֶּן הֲדַד is aligned as two units, Ben and hadad; where the ASV writes of before it as a chain's rectum (v3, v25) the of joins the first token, headed on Ben, and where it stands bare (v24) each token takes only its own word. A single fused unit was the alternative and was not taken.
- עַד־כַּלֵּה (v17, v19) is read as till + infinitive absolute recast finite: the infinitive takes the supplied subject and auxiliary (thou have consumed / thou hadst consumed), headed on consumed, and the object pronoun the ASV writes after it (them, it) is its own added unit under rule 44, since no object morpheme stands in the Hebrew.
- At v4, חִלָּה אֶת־פְּנֵי יְהוָה → besought Jehovah retains the separate *besought* verb across the intervening subject, while the contiguous face-of-Jehovah nominal forms its own noncompositional *Jehovah* unit, headed on Jehovah. Both independent deferred-investigation judgments supersede only the former unrepresented פָּנֶה allocation in that nominal; no verb-face fusion across the subject is introduced. The original v4 convention is archived in the deferred-investigation audit. At v14, בָּכָה עַל־פָּנָיו → wept over him, the scoped retrospective judgment supersedes the former unrepresented פָּנֶה: the preposition and construct noun fuse, while the suffix retains him separately, following rule 30's prohibition on leaving the construct unrepresented. Where the ASV writes מֵעַל פָּנָיו → from his presence (v23), the noun still takes its written rendering. That earlier v14 amendment remains limited to v14; it does not fuse the noun and suffix into him.
- The regnal formula's italic and reigned (v1, v10) is one added unit headed on reigned: the Hebrew writes the reign-length numeral directly after the accession clause, and no morpheme licenses either the connective or the verb.
