# Deuteronomy 19 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Fusion status in this chapter: a contiguous group of morphemes the ASV renders by one English word is recorded `aligned` under rule 30 — עַל־כֵּן → *Wherefore* (19.7), כַּאֲשֶׁר → *as* (19.8, 19.19), לִפְנֵי → *before* (19.17 twice), מִשָּׁם → *thence* (19.12), שָׁמָּה → *thither* (19.3, 19.4), כָּל־הַיָּמִים → *ever* (19.9), בִּבְלִי־דַעַת → *unawares* (19.4), כַּזֶּה → *such* (19.20). `noncompositional` is reserved for a multi-word English rendering whose pieces cannot be assigned to particular morphemes, which in this chapter is only מִתְּמֹל שִׁלְשֹׁם → *in time past*. This is a chapter choice and not a rule: the corpus records לִפְנֵי → *before* as `aligned` in 365 units and `noncompositional` in 263, and כַּאֲשֶׁר → *as* as `aligned` in 197 and `noncompositional` in 107.
- מִתְּמֹל שִׁלְשֹׁם → *in time past* is one `noncompositional` unit of all three morphemes in both DEU.19.4 and DEU.19.6, with the source head on the מִן as rule 6's deterministic anchor and the target head on the lexical noun *time*. The English expresses the idiom jointly and no morpheme owns a part of it, so rule 30's one-English-word condition is unmet and rule 2 does not split it either.
- A preposition standing before תָּוֶךְ or קֶרֶב is split from it throughout this chapter, because the ASV writes a word for each morpheme every time it occurs: בְּתוֹךְ → *in* + *the midst* (19.2), בְּקֶרֶב → *in* + *the midst* (19.10, 19.20), מִקֶּרֶב → *from* + *the midst* (19.19). Rule 30's one-rendering condition is unmet in all four, so rule 2 splits them and the construct noun takes a supplied *the* under rule 29. The corpus's 27 fused preposition + תּוֹךְ/קֶרֶב → *among* units are the other shape of the same fork and do not reach a rendering the ASV writes in two words.
- Where a single English *that* stands between a waw and a purpose לְ + infinitive construct, this chapter gives it to the preposition under rule 52 and leaves the waw `unrepresented`: DEU.19.3 וְהָיָה לָנוּס שָׁמָּה → *that every manslayer may flee thither* puts *that* on the לְ of לָנוּס, and both the waw and הָיָה, for which the ASV writes nothing at all, are `unrepresented`. The reasoning is that the purpose the *that* states is what לָנוּס contributes and not what the consecutive waw does. Where no infinitive stands in the clause the waw takes the *that* as usual, as at DEU.19.12 וָמֵת → *that he may die* and DEU.19.13 וְטוֹב לָךְ → *that it may go well with thee*. Rule 45 is not the competing reading here, because the ASV writes no presentative frame for הָיָה anywhere in the verse.
- In the manslaughter law's אֲשֶׁר clauses, read what the ASV made of the relative before placing a supplied noun. At DEU.19.4 the clause stands in a nominal slot and the ASV writes one word for antecedent and relative together, so אֲשֶׁר takes *whoso* under rules 30 and 46. At DEU.19.5 the ASV recasts the clause adverbially as *as when a man goeth*, rendering אֲשֶׁר by the subordinator *when* and writing a lexical subject *a man* for which the Hebrew has no nominal, so that noun is its own `added` unit under rule 17. Rule 46's rider, which joins a supplied antecedent to a present relative, reaches only a clause standing in a nominal slot and only the pronoun antecedent of *him that* or *they that*. DEU.19.14's *they of old time* is neither case: רִאשֹׁנִים is written, and the whole supplied noun phrase is its rendering under rule 46.
