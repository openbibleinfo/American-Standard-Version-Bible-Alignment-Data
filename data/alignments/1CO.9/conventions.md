# 1 Corinthians 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- `εὐαγγελίζομαι` is rendered *preach the gospel* and the whole phrase belongs to that one verb token (9:16 twice, 9:18). At 9:18 `τὸ εὐαγγέλιον` also stands as a separate object and takes its own *the gospel*, so the same two English words answer to different tokens in one verse. Rule 103's test is licensing and not recurrence, and neither occurrence is `added`.
- Interrogative `μή` opening the rhetorical questions of 9:4, 9:5, 9:8 and 9:9 is `unrepresented` throughout under rule 44, the ASV marking each by inversion alone. At 9:9 the inversion is the cleft *Is it … that*, and that frame is the impersonal `μέλει`'s own English rather than a supplied interrogative word, so it does not bring the particle back under rule 44's second arm.
- An article governing a substantival adjective or quantifier takes the supplied generic nominal even where the ASV writes no determiner, with the case preposition riding along, and leaves the adjective or quantifier bare: `τὰ πνευματικά` -> *things* / *spiritual* and `τὰ σαρκικά` -> *things* / *carnal* at 9:11, `τὰ ἱερά` -> *things* / *sacred* at 9:13, `τοῖς πᾶσιν` -> *to … men* / *all* at 9:22. Anarthrous Greek is the contrast and keeps the nominal on the quantifier itself, `πάντα` -> *all things* at 9:12, 9:22 and 9:23 and `πάντων` -> *all men* at 9:19, and 9:22 writes both halves in one verse.
- Two supplied English words in this chapter are `added` and every other one folds. 9:10's italic *to thresh* is `added` because `ἀλοῶν` is already spent on *thresheth* (rule 103), and 9:13's italic *and* is `added` because it seams two finite clauses the Greek left asyndetic and neither is a participle converted to a finite clause (rule 98, not rule 88). Where a supplied word instead completes a token's own unfinished English it folds: *the corn* on `ἀλοῶντα` at 9:9, *men* on `πάντων` at 9:19, *in the games* on `ἀγωνιζόμενος` and *do it* on `ἐκεῖνοι` at 9:25, *this* on `τῆς` at 9:12 and *it were* on `καλόν` at 9:15. Rule 11 keeps the italics from deciding either way.
