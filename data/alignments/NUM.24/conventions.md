# Numbers 24 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A supplied English relative is placed by what stands before the clause it opens. Where a relative morpheme is written it takes the word (NUM.24.4, `אֲשֶׁר` → 'Who'); where the ASV recasts an articleless attributive as a relative clause the frame stays with the attributive under rule 41 (NUM.24.18, the italic 'who were' with `אֹיְבָיו`); and where the word merely subordinates a finite clause juxtaposed to a written antecedent it is `added` under rule 55 (NUM.24.6 'which' after `אֲהָלִים`, NUM.24.16 'Who' after `שֹׁמֵעַ`). A written substantive is such an antecedent even where this rule supplied its own English noun phrase, so the twin oracles NUM.24.4 and NUM.24.16 differ only in that one writes `אֲשֶׁר` and the other does not.
- `עֲדֵי אֹבֵד` → 'shall come to destruction' takes the whole frame on `עַד` under rule 49's resultative clause, headed on 'to' (NUM.24.20, NUM.24.24). The italic 'shall come' of NUM.24.24's first clause is `added` under rule 20 instead, and the two are not in conflict: `מִיַּד כִּתִּים` keeps its own written 'from', so dropping the English leaves a lexical motion verb missing and no relation unstated, while dropping it before `עֲדֵי` leaves the terminative relation unsaid. Read whether the preposition already holds a word of its own before choosing.
- `נְאֻם` heading an oracle is recast throughout this chapter as the finite 'saith', with its *nomen rectum* standing as the English subject of that verb and the unit discontinuous from it under rule 25 (NUM.24.3, 4, 15, 16, and the second `נְאֻם` of 3 and 15). The construct itself takes only 'saith'; no copula and no subject pronoun is placed with it.
