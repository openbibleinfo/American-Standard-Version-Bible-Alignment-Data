# 2 Chronicles 20 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where a negative existential אַיִן, or a possessive or obligation לְ, is the morpheme that makes the clause existential, possessive or modal, the whole English frame the ASV writes in the copula's place belongs to that morpheme's unit — including a modal or auxiliary written inside it — and the unit heads on the negation word or on the relational word, while a dependent infinitive keeps only its own written לְ and its lexical verb. 20.6 `אֵין ... לְהִתְיַצֵּב` gives *none is able* to אַיִן, *to* to the לְ and *withstand* to the infinitive; 20.12 `אֵין בָּנוּ כֹּחַ` gives *have no* to אַיִן, *we* to the suffix and *might* to the noun; 20.17 `לֹא לָכֶם לְהִלָּחֵם` gives *shall ... need* to the לְ, *Ye* to its suffix and *fight* to the infinitive; 20.25 `לְאֵין מַשָּׂא` gives *more than they could* to אַיִן and *carry away* to the noun. What decides it is that the frame states the existence, possession or obligation the Hebrew predicates by that one morpheme, and that giving it to the infinitive would leave one Hebrew verb holding two English predications. The competing reading, which gives the modal to the infinitive under rules 48 and 52, is named in each unit's note.
- The ASV spells some single English words as two target tokens: *To morrow* for מָחָר at 20.16 and 20.17, *watch tower* for מִצְפֶּה at 20.24. Both tokens belong to the one source noun's unit and the head goes by rule 31's subtraction to the token carrying the lexeme — *morrow* in the first, *watch* in the second, the two falling on opposite sides because the test is run on the words and not on their order. The opening *To* of *To morrow* is not a preposition supplied for an adverbial accusative under rule 33 and is never `added`, even though מָחָר is an adverbial accusative of time and the word stands exactly where such a supplied preposition would stand.
