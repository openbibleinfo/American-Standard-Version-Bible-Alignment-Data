# 2 Timothy 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where a Greek compound verb's prefixed element is realized in English and no Greek preposition stands to answer to it, that English rides inside the verb's own unit and the head stays on the word glossing the verbal root: συν- gives 'with me' to συγκακοπάθησον (2:3) and 'with him' to συναπεθάνομεν and συζήσομεν (2:11) and συμβασιλεύσομεν (2:12); ἐπι- gives 'on' to ἐπικαλουμένων (2:22); λογο- gives 'about words' to λογομαχεῖν (2:14); στρατο- gives 'as a soldier' to στρατολογήσαντι (2:4). Rule 36 points the other way only where a Greek preposition is expressed, as ἐπί does at 2:16 -> 'in ungodliness' against προκόπτω's own 'proceed further'.
- The ASV's 'soldier' is spent on στρατιώτης (2:3), so the chapter's στρατ- verbs head elsewhere: στρατευόμενος -> 'soldier on service' heads on 'service' and στρατολογήσαντι -> 'who enrolled him as a soldier' heads on 'enrolled' (2:4). This is rule 91's operational test applied to a lexeme the corpus was not queried for; heading 2:4's participle on 'soldier' is the competing reading and is named in its note.
- Impersonal δεῖ is aligned to the English modal alone — 'must' at 2:6 and 2:24 — and the governed infinitive keeps its own verb with any infinitival marker ('to partake' 2:6, a bare 'strive' and 'be' 2:24). Where a modal instead absorbs the marker the infinitive's English is likewise bare, as ἀρνήσασθαι -> 'deny' does under οὐ δύναται -> 'cannot' at 2:13.
- An English possessive formed on the article's own head noun is not rule 20 displacement. At 2:21 τῷ δεσπότῃ -> 'the master's use' gives 'the' to the article under rule 19 and a bare 'master's' to the noun, because 'the' determines 'master' and nothing occupies δεσπότης's own determiner slot; rule 20's τῆς Καίσαρος οἰκίας -> 'Caesar's household' is the contrast, where the possessive stands in front of a different head noun. Where the Greek is anarthrous no article competes and the whole supplied determiner is a helper on the noun, as κυρίου -> 'the Lord's' at 2:24 and κύριος -> 'The Lord' at 2:19.
- A supplied lexical noun expanding a relative pronoun is read as rule 77's generic-noun arm and takes the head, not as a bare antecedent: ἃ -> 'the things which' heads on 'things' (2:2) and οἵτινες -> 'men who' heads on 'men' (2:18), against the same lemma's bare οἵτινες -> 'who' at 2:2. Heading on the relativizer stays defensible if 'men' is read as a bare antecedent, and 2:18's note says so.
