# Matthew 5 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- ἕνεκεν wrapped in the discontinuous 'for … sake' frame is headed on the prepositional word 'for' (5:10, 5:11), following rule 78's scoping of that frame rather than rule 71's scoping of improper prepositions. ἕνεκεν is improper by rule 95's test, and the corpus is genuinely divided on this very lemma: LUK.9.24 and MRK.8.35 head 'for'; MRK.10.7 and MRK.10.29 head 'cause'/'sake'. The reading here rests on rule 46's stated test, that an expressed Greek preposition is what decides, and on rule 78's frame-specific precedent being unbroken. A head on 'sake' remains defensible and stands in the corpus.
- ἔνοχος ἔσται is split throughout (5:21; 5:22 x3): the adjective takes 'in danger' headed on 'danger' under rule 17's meaning-bearing-complement clause, and the expressed copula ἔσται keeps 'shall be'. Rules 53 and 63 do not arise anywhere in this chapter's predicate clauses, the copula being expressed at 5:21, 5:22, 5:34, 5:35 and 5:48.
- τοῖς ἀρχαίοις → 'to them of old time' is split the same way at 5:21 and 5:33: 'to them' to the substantival article under rule 22 with the case preposition riding along, and 'of old time' to the adjective headed on 'old' under rule 66, 'time' being the generic completion.
- Rule 20's second arm was used at 5:39 (τὴν → 'thy') and 5:40 (τὸ → 'thy'), the article taking a supplied possessive because no genitive pronoun stands in the phrase to carry it. This is deliberate against the chapter's roughly twenty rule 20 proper emptyings, where an expressed σου/αὐτοῦ/ὑμῶν is present and free; the test applied throughout is whether some other source word is available for the possessive, never whether the ASV wrote one.
- In the inverted οὐχί questions at 5:46 and 5:47 the fronted English 'do' is read as the finite verb of the question and given to ποιοῦσιν, with οὐχί keeping only 'not' under rule 64. Reading 'do' as a supplied auxiliary on οὐχί and leaving ποιοῦσιν without an English word is the competing analysis; the two verses are analysed alike under rule 24.
