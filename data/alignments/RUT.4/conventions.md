# Ruth 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- גֹּאֵל is rendered *near kinsman* throughout (4.1, 4.3, 4.6, 4.8, 4.14) and every unit heads on *kinsman*: rule 31's subtraction ties, and the ASV's own use decides it, since *near* is the specifier it writes beside a class noun it also writes alone (RUT.2.20 *near kinsmen*, RUT.3.9 and RUT.3.12 *near kinsman*). A supplied indefinite article joins that nominal under rule 15 (4.14) and is never the head, and a supplied preposition standing beside the noun is the governing verb's under rule 44 (4.14 *without*), not this noun's.
- The particle of this chapter's phrasal verbs stays with the verb and is not marked `added` under rule 44's surplus-particle sentence: *sat/sit down* for יָשַׁב (4.1 three times, 4.2 twice), *turn/turned aside* for סוּר (4.1 twice), *drew off* for שָׁלַף (4.7, 4.8), *raise up* for hiphil קוּם (4.5, 4.10), *is come again* for שׁוּב (4.3), *cut off* for כָּרַת (4.10), and *went in* for בּוֹא (4.13, whose direction the following אֶל writes). In each the bare English verb fails to write the source verb's whole lexeme, so both of that sentence's conditions fail. The supplied reflexive object *him* of 4.1's *sat him down* is a different matter and is `added`, Qal יָשַׁב licensing no reflexive.
- For קָרָא with שֵׁם, count the ASV's words against the two morphemes rather than carrying one division into both places. At 4.11 the ASV writes only *be famous*, so the finite verb takes the *be* that carries its imperative slot and שֵׁם takes *famous*, which writes the renown; at 4.14 it writes *let his name be famous*, so שֵׁם takes *name*, its suffix *his*, and the verb takes *let ... be famous* headed on *famous*, a morpheme already holding an English word of its own not being a home for a second. The fused reading of 4.11 under rule 30 is named in that unit.
