# Ecclesiastes 5 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A relative clause that resumes its antecedent with a suffix on a preposition, where the ASV writes a preposition and a bare relative pronoun and no separate pronoun: the preposition takes the word written for its own relation and the relative and the suffix share the one relative pronoun, headed on the relative (ECC.5.19, 'to whom God hath given'). Do not mark the suffix unrepresented; rule 22's first sentence reaches an element the ASV leaves unwritten, and this one is written inside 'whom'.
- A supplied English 'hath' or 'have' was placed by rule 43's count three different ways in this chapter and none of them is a split: on the possessive lamed at 5.16 ('what profit hath he'), on the possessive suffix of chalyo at 5.17 ('and hath sickness'), where no hayah and no preposition stands and the suffix carries no other English word, and as its own added unit at 5.4 ('he hath no pleasure in fools'), where the bet is already spent on 'in' and nothing else in the clause makes it possessive.
- A repeated English preposition belongs to a written preposition whose scope spans the coordinated phrases (ECC.5.7, both 'in's to the one bet, the first waw left unrepresented). Where no preposition morpheme licenses the English word, the count runs the other way: at ECC.5.10 the first 'with' is the preposition the English verb needs to reach its bare object under rule 44, and the second, standing in a line whose verb the Hebrew gaps, was given to the noun it stands before under rules 33 and 34. That second placement is the chapter's one open call and is noted on its unit; the alternative folds it into the distant verb's discontinuous unit.
