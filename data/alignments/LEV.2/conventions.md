# Leviticus 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- minchah is headed on 'offering' in all 15 units of the chapter ('meal offering', 'of a meal offering', 'the meal offering'). Rule 31's tie-break decides it: the ASV writes a bare 'offering' for minchah (GEN.4.3-5, MAL.3.4, 1CH.16.29) and never a bare 'meal', so 'meal' is the ASV's specifier and 'offering' the word realizing the lexeme. This is a deliberate divergence from the corpus majority, which heads the two-word phrase on 'meal' in about 32 units against 11 on 'offering'; each unit names the competing reading. Do not harmonize toward the majority without new evidence about how the ASV writes the word alone.
- soleth -> 'fine flour' is headed on 'fine' in all five units (vv1, 2, 4, 5, 7). The ASV never writes a bare 'flour' for a Hebrew word of this class - it writes 'meal' for qemach - so 'flour' is the supplied generic under rule 31 and the tie-break does not rescue it. The competing head on 'flour' is named in every unit note.
- isheh -> 'an offering made by fire' is headed on 'fire' in all six units (vv2, 3, 9, 10, 11, 16), on rule 31's own olah -> 'burnt offerings' analogy: 'offering' alone is any offering. Where the phrase also carries the clause's copula ('it is an offering made by fire', v16) that copula joins the same unit under rule 20 and does not move the head.
- Vessel and grain words: machavat -> 'baking pan' and marchesheth -> 'frying pan' head on 'pan' (vv5, 7); aviv -> 'grain in the ear' and karmel -> 'the fresh ear' head on 'ear' (v14); geres -> 'bruised grain' heads on 'bruised' (vv14, 16), 'grain' being the generic the ASV also supplies inside 'grain in the ear'. The working test across the chapter is whether the shared English noun is the ASV's own rendering of the Hebrew word ('pan', 'ear', 'offering') or a word it writes for something the Hebrew does not name ('grain', 'flour', 'thing', 'fruits'). The vv5/7 note cites the shared class noun instead; the firmer ground is EZK.4.3, where 'an iron pan' renders machavat by a bare 'pan'.
- kol ... lo rendered by a single 'No'/'no' (v11, both clauses) is read as one rule 30/35 fusion covering both morphemes, headed on the negative and discontinuous across the intervening relative clause. The article of hamminchah in that phrase is then unrepresented under rule 29, since the ASV writes no 'the' for the phrase. Where a second kol stands with no negative of its own it takes the 'any' the ASV writes for it (rule 15), and the waw of the coordinated member takes the 'nor'.
- The definite object marker takes an English word only where the ASV writes one for the object relation on the very phrase the marker stands on: 'as' at v2 ('shall burn it as the memorial thereof') and 'for' at v14 ('shalt offer for the meal offering of thy first fruits'). It is unrepresented at vv6, 8, 9, 12 and 16, where the object is written bare. The resumptive 'it' of 'shall burn it' is added at v2 and v9: no object morpheme stands in either clause, and it does not join the verb merely by standing inside its rendering (rules 44, 30).
- The second lamed of 'shall be Aaron's and his sons' (vv3, 10) is unrepresented: the possessive lamed heading the predicate carries the copula under rule 49, but the ASV coordinates the second member bare, writing neither a second copula nor a preposition for it, and the waw already has the 'and'. BSB attaches that lamed to its phrase; that is the documented convention difference and not an error.
