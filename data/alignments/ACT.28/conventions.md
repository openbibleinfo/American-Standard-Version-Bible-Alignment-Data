# Acts 28 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A supplied English locative pro-adverb is folded onto the verb whose English it completes as a rule 7 helper and is not marked `added`: 'there' at 28:12 and 'hither' at 28:21. This holds even where the place expression the pro-adverb echoes is already spent on its own tokens in the same verse, as εἰς Συρακούσας is at 28:12. Rule 103's `added` arm was read as reaching full lexical content only, its own stated contrast naming a resumptive pronoun and rule 7's list ending in 'and similar words'. The competing reading is recorded in the 28:12 unit's note.
- A concessive participle the ASV expands with the discontinuous frame 'though … yet' keeps that whole frame, the 'yet' included, in the participle's own unit under rules 15 and 86: διασωθέντα at 28:4 and ποιήσας at 28:17. Giving 'yet' to the main clause's finite verb as the word it introduces, or marking it `added` under rule 86, was considered and rejected in both verses — the participle is the token that carries the concession, and rule 27 forbids splitting the correlative from what licenses it. Both notes record the alternative.
- Every supplied juncture 'and' at a seam the ASV creates by converting Greek participles into finite clauses is folded onto the constituent it introduces under rule 88: 28:2, 28:3, 28:5, 28:6, 28:7, 28:13, 28:14, 28:15 and 28:21. This includes 28:8, where the constituent introduced is itself a participle ('and laying'), on the reading that rule 98's `added` arm is scoped to a seam whose two constituents are rendered alike — at 28:8 the flanking participles are rendered finite and participial respectively, so neither rule 88's letter nor rule 98's reaches it and rule 88's principle decides. The chapter's only `added` juncture is the verse-initial 'And' of 28:13, which introduces no converted participle at all.
