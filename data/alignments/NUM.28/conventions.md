# Numbers 28 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Distributive repetition of the form `X בְּXוֹ` — `שַׁבַּת בְּשַׁבַּתּוֹ` (v.10), `חֹדֶשׁ בְּחָדְשׁוֹ` (v.14) — gives the ASV's single *every* to the `בְּ`, which is what makes the phrase distributive, while the repeated noun and its resumptive suffix are `unrepresented` under rule 22 and the first occurrence keeps its own noun and its relational *of*. Giving *every* instead to the first noun as a supplied determiner under rule 15 was the competing reading; it is not promoted to a rule because no corpus evidence for the construction was consulted, so a later chapter with that evidence may settle it differently.
- In `לַ<noun> הָאֶחָד`, count the determiners the ASV wrote. Where it writes one distributive determiner, `אֶחָד` takes it and both articles in the phrase — the noun's own and the agreement article on the numeral — are `unrepresented`: *for each bullock* (vv.12, 28), *unto/for every lamb* (vv.13, 21, 29). Where it writes *the one X*, two determiners are written and each morpheme takes its own: the noun's article takes *the* and `אֶחָד` takes *one*, the agreement article still `unrepresented` (vv.7, 12, 14, 28). Promoted as an amendment to rule 41.
- `לַ[הַ]יּוֹם` is divided two ways in this chapter, on the English and not on the Hebrew. At v.3 the ASV writes *day by day*, so the preposition takes *by*, the noun takes both occurrences of *day* in one discontinuous unit under rule 25, and the assimilated article is `unrepresented`. At v.24 it writes the single word *daily*, so all three morphemes share one unit headed on the noun under rule 30. Do not read the second as licence to fuse the first, or the first as licence to split the second.
- The chapter's two `בְּנֵי` constructs are not treated alike, because the ASV spends a different number of words on them. `בְּנֵי בָקָר` → *young* is one fused unit headed on `בֵּן`, with `בָּקָר` inside it rather than `unrepresented` beside it (vv.11, 19, 27). `בְּנֵי שָׁנָה` → *old* leaves `שָׁנָה` its own *a year*, so the two morphemes are split under rule 2 (vv.3, 9, 11, 19, 27). Likewise the supplied specifier *he* joins the noun's unit in `כְּבָשִׂים` → *he lambs* and in `שְׂעִיר` → *he goat* (v.22), but where `עִזִּים` is written it is that morpheme's *he* and the construct keeps only *goat* (vv.15, 30).
- In the verbless offering formula the ASV's whole supplied subject-and-copula goes to the predicate `עֹלַת`, whichever dummy it chooses: *It is a continual burnt offering* (v.6) and *this is the burnt offering of every sabbath* (v.10) are read alike, and the supplied demonstrative is not `added`. Where `זֹאת` is written the pronoun takes *this* alone and only the copula joins the predicate (v.14). Reading v.10's *this* as `added` would split one construction on the ASV's choice of dummy word.
- Supplied generics and ASV italics that expand one substantive join that substantive's unit and never head it: *part*/*parts* on `עִשָּׂרוֹן` and on the ordinals `רְבִיעִת`, `שְׁלִישִׁת`, `עֲשִׂירִית`; the italic *of an ephah* on `עִשָּׂרוֹן` where the ASV writes it (vv.9, 12) and not where it does not (vv.12b, 13, 20, 21, 28, 29); the italic *feast of* on `שָׁבֻעֹתֵיכֶם` (v.26). The chapter's only `added` units are the supplied object pronouns *it* (v.8) and *them* (v.31), where no object morpheme stands in the clause.
- Recurring head choices for the offering vocabulary, held constant across all 31 verses: `עֹלָה`→*burnt*, `מִנְחָה`→*meal*, `נֶסֶךְ`→*drink*, `אִשֶּׁה`→*fire*, `חַטָּאת`→*sin*, `סֹלֶת`→*fine*, `שֵׁכָר`→*strong*, `עִשָּׂרוֹן`→*tenth*, `רֵיחַ`→*savor*, `קֹדֶשׁ`→*holy*, `תָּמִים`→*blemish*, `מִלְּבַד`→*besides* on `בַד`. `מוֹעֵד` → *due season* heads on *season* instead, and the contrast with `סֹלֶת` → *fine flour* is deliberate: rule 31's tie-break turns on whether the ASV writes the English noun alone for that Hebrew word elsewhere, which it does for *season* and does not for *flour*.
