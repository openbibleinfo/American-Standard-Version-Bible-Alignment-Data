# Ezekiel 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Possessive recasting of a relative clause with a prepositional predicate: `אֲשֶׁר קֶסֶת הַסֹּפֵר בְּמָתְנָיו` → *who had the inkhorn by his side* (9.3 s031, 9.11 s011) gives the supplied *had* to the בְּ heading that predicate under rules 43 and 49, the unit discontinuous around the object and headed on the preposition's own *by*. It is given neither to אֲשֶׁר as a possessive relative nor to the suffix on מׇתְנַיִם, which keeps *his*, and it is not `added`: dropping *had* diminishes only the possession relation, which is rule 43's test. Where the ASV writes no possessive verb for the same Hebrew — 9.2 s035, *with a writer's inkhorn by his side* — the preposition takes only its *by*.
- Where the ASV writes a single English word for a multi-morpheme Hebrew expression, this chapter fused under rule 30 rather than rendering one morpheme and stranding the rest as `unrepresented`: `מֵעַל` → *from* (9.3, headed on מִן), `אֲשֶׁר … עָלָיו` → *whereupon* (9.3, headed on אֲשֶׁר, which carries the *where-*), `לְמַשְׁחִית` → *utterly* (9.6, headed on מַשְׁחִית), `בִּמְאֹד מְאֹד` → *exceeding* (9.9, headed on מְאֹד), `כַּאֲשֶׁר` → *as* (9.11, headed on כְּ). The rule 22 reading that renders one occurrence and marks the repetition unrepresented was weighed at 9.9 and rejected. Where the ASV instead spends a second English word on a particular morpheme the condition is unmet and the pair splits: `עַל … אֲשֶׁר` → *upon whom* at 9.6 s016/s017 is two units, with the resumptive suffix unrepresented.
- The slaughter list `זָקֵן בָּחוּר וּבְתוּלָה וְטַף וְנָשִׁים` (9.6): each anarthrous substantive takes the ASV's supplied *the* into its own unit (rules 15, 46), and the heads follow rule 31's subtraction test rather than the English phrase's noun — `זָקֵן` → *the old man* on *old*, `בָּחוּר` → *the young man* on *young*, `טַף` → *little children* on *little*. The competing reading heads the two noun lexemes on *man* and *children*, on the ground that בָּחוּר and טַף already name their class; it is named in the verse notes and was not taken. `חֲלָלִים` → *with the slain* (9.7 s014) and `מֻטֶּה` → *wresting of judgment* (9.9 s023, italic completion included) are headed on the same principle.
