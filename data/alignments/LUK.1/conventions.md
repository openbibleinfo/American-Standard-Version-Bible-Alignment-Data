# Luke 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- hierateuein / hierateias -> 'the priest's office' heads on *priest's*, the word carrying the priestly lexeme, with *office* the generic completion under rule 66's test (LUK.1.8, LUK.1.9). Heading on *office* was weighed and is recorded as defensible in 1:8's note; the chapter takes the same line for every one-word-to-phrase rendering it meets -- 'make ready' on *ready* (1:17, 1:76), 'shall call blessed' on *blessed* (1:48), 'hath given help' on *help* (1:54), 'waxed strong' on *strong* (1:80), 'made signs' on *signs* (1:22, 1:62), 'she lifted up her voice' on *voice* (1:42).
- Possessive dative. Where the Greek expresses a verb, that verb takes the English *have* and the dative keeps only the subject pronoun the ASV builds on it: ouk en autois teknon -> 'they had no child' gives *had* to en and *they* to autois (1:7), as estai chara soi -> 'thou shalt have joy' gives *shalt have* to estai and *thou* to soi (1:14). Where the clause is verbless the dative carries the supplied verb as well, no relational noun standing to verbalize the case under rule 75: gyne auto -> 'he had a wife' gives *he had* to auto and *a wife* to gyne (1:5), which is rule 68's 'Ti emoi kai soi' -> 'what have I to do with thee'. The candidate table shows these as three treatments of one lemma pair; the presence or absence of a Greek verb is what licenses the difference.
- The egeneto frame. The ASV supplies a complementizer *that* only at 1:59 ('it came to pass on the eighth day, that they came'), and it is `added` under rule 86: the clause it introduces is a finite indicative and the verse's two kai are already spent on their own English. 1:8, 1:23 and 1:41 write the same frame with no such word, so nothing is left `unrepresented` there and the difference is the ASV's alone. Luke will meet this frame repeatedly; decide it from whether the ASV wrote the word, not from the frame.
- An English *face* in a 'before ... face' phrase belongs to the Greek noun where one is expressed and to the improper preposition where none is: pro prosopou kyriou -> 'before the face of the Lord' gives *before* to pro and *the face* to prosopou (1:76), while enopion autou -> 'before his face' gives *before ... face* whole to enopion headed on *face* under rule 71 and *his* to autou (1:17). One chapter writing the same English phrase two ways with different Greek behind it, so the candidate row is not a split.
