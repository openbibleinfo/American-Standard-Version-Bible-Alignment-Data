# Luke 10 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where a Greek verb governs a bare case complement and no Greek preposition is expressed, the English preposition the ASV supplies rides with the governed word and rule 17 heads the unit there, not on the verb. `λῃσταῖς περιέπεσεν` → *among robbers* at 10:30, as `ἐκπειράζων αὐτόν` → *of him*, `ἐπεμελήθη αὐτοῦ` → *of him*, `νηπίοις` → *unto babes* and `Σοδόμοις` → *for Sodom* do. Rules 21 and 69 are what license it — rule 21's determinerless clause leaves the case preposition with the noun, and rule 69 says in terms that it rides along. An expressed Greek preposition is the contrast and takes the word itself, which is why the identical *fell among the robbers* gives *among* to `εἰς` at 10:36 and to `λῃσταῖς` at 10:30. Do not read the preposition off a verbal prefix that could be made to explain it.
- Where the ASV recasts a Greek impersonal or dative construction as a personal English clause, the non-nominative pronoun takes the supplied English subject and the verb keeps only its own word. `τίς ... δοκεῖ σοι` → *thinkest thou* gives *thou* to `σοι` and *thinkest* to `δοκεῖ` at 10:36, `οὐ μέλει σοι` → *dost thou ... care* does the same at 10:40, and `ἦν τῇδε ἀδελφή` → *she had a sister* gives *she* to the dative demonstrative and *had* to the expressed copula at 10:39. The verb here is expressed rather than supplied, so rule 75 is not in question; what the pronoun takes is the subject slot the recasting opens, not a supplied verb.
- A single Greek verb the ASV renders by a whole English idiom keeps that idiom entire in one unit — unsplit, and with no part of it `added`. *Go your ways* (10:3), *went on their way* (10:38), *passed by on the other side* (10:31, 10:32), *made answer* (10:30), *made trial* (10:25), *was moved with compassion* (10:33), *is come nigh* (10:9, 10:11). Rule 91's operational test then decides the head, and it comes out both ways: the verb word where the lexeme is the verb (*passed* for `ἀντιπαρέρχομαι`, *came* for `προσέρχομαι`, *nigh* for `ἐγγίζω`), and rule 17's meaning-bearing complement where the English verb is a light one (*answer*, *trial*, *care*, *compassion*).
- An English particle or adverb realizing a Greek verbal prefix stays inside that verb's own unit and is never split off or marked `added`: *turn ... again* (10:6), *are set before* (10:8), *wipe off* (10:11), *brought down* (10:15), *going down* (10:30, 10:31), *stood up* (10:25), *pouring on* (10:34), *spendest more* and *come back again* (10:35), *send forth* (10:2, 10:3), *go out* (10:10). This is rule 27 holding for a directional particle, which rule 7's list does not name; the prefix is what licenses the word.
