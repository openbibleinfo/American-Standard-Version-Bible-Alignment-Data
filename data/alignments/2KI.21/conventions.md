# 2 Kings 21 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The regnal age formula `בֶּן X שָׁנָה` (2KI.21.1, 2KI.21.19): the construct `בֵּן` is the predicate of the verbless clause and carries the ASV's *was ... old*, discontinuous under rule 25 around the numeral and its counted noun, which keep their own words; the copula joins that predicate under rule 20. Do not mark `בֵּן` unrepresented, and do not leave *old* to the numeral or to the counted noun.
- The succession formula `תַּחְתָּיו` → *in his stead* (2KI.21.18, 2KI.21.24, 2KI.21.26): the preposition takes *in ... stead* as one compound rendering, discontinuous under rule 25 around the possessive, and the suffix takes *his* under rule 18. The unit heads on *stead*, which carries the relation.
- `כָּעַס` hiphil → *provoke to anger*, *provoked to anger* (2KI.21.6, 2KI.21.15) heads on the verb rather than on *anger*: the ASV never writes *provoke* alone for this lexeme, so *to anger* is what English spends on the relation and not a complement the verb lacks. Both units name the competing rule 44 reading, which heads on the complement. A supplied object pronoun standing inside that frame is `added` where the Hebrew writes no object morpheme, as at 2KI.21.6, and belongs to the suffix where one is written, as at 2KI.21.15.
- The formula `הָרַע בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה` (2KI.21.2, 9, 15, 16, 20): the substantival article takes *that which* headed on the pronoun under rule 41, the adjective takes the construction-supplied copula with *evil*, `עַיִן` takes a supplied *the sight* under rule 29 and the divine name takes *of Jehovah* under rule 19. Where the ASV instead renders the adjective attributively — *much evil* at 2KI.21.6 — no determiner stands before the adjective's word, so the article is `unrepresented` under rule 29 and the adjective keeps the bare *evil*.
- A two-morpheme subordinating pair rendered by a single English word is recorded `aligned` under rule 30 and headed on the governing first morpheme under rule 23, not `noncompositional`: `כַּאֲשֶׁר` → *as* (2KI.21.3, 13, 20), `יַעַן אֲשֶׁר` → *because* (2KI.21.11, 15), `עַד אֲשֶׁר` → *till* (2KI.21.16). The corpus writes `כַּאֲשֶׁר` → *as* both ways — 264 units `aligned` against 109 `noncompositional` — and this chapter takes the `aligned` reading throughout, since rule 6 reaches a genuinely indivisible phrase and rule 2 prefers the split wherever a defensible source head is available.
