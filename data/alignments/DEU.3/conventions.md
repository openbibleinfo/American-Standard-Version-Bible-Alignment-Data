# Deuteronomy 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- In a construct chain whose nomen rectum carries an article, count the ASV's written *the*s and let the count decide, rule 29; a segmented article on a toponym is read on exactly the terms of one on a common noun. One *the* with the rectum rendered anarthrously goes to the present article and the construct noun keeps only its own word — the way to Bashan (3.1), the king of Bashan (3.1, 3.3), half the hill country of Gilead (3.12), the rest of Gilead, the half tribe of Manasseh, the region of Argob (3.13), the slopes of Pisgah (3.17), the top of Pisgah (3.27). Two written *the*s give each morpheme the one standing before its own word — the hand of the two kings of the Amorites (3.8), the remnant of the Rephaim (3.11), the border of the Geshurites (3.14), the middle of the valley (3.16), the sea of the Arabah (3.17). No *the* at all leaves the article `unrepresented` — all Gilead, all Bashan (3.10, 3.13), king of Bashan (3.11), and the bare toponyms of 3.15, 3.16 and 3.25. The same toponym appears all three ways in this chapter, so a supplied *the* on the construct is licensed only where no article morpheme stands or where a second English *the* is written.
- Where such a chain is also a directional or locative accusative, the preposition rule 33 supplies for the accusative and the *the* rule 29 gives the rectum's article both stand in the phrase, and the construct's unit is discontinuous around the article rather than absorbing it: `רֹאשׁ הַפִּסְגָּה` → *unto* … *top* with the article's *the* between them (3.27), and `אַשְׁדֹּת הַפִּסְגָּה` → *the slopes of Pisgah* on the same terms with no preposition supplied (3.17). Do not fold the *the* into the construct noun's unit to make it contiguous; the fronting is forced by the anarthrous rendering of the rectum, not by the phrase's left edge.
