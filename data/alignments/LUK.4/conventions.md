# Luke 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- An English verb particle that no source token answers to rides inside its verb's own unit and never heads it, whether it realizes a Greek verbal prefix (ἐξῆλθεν → went out 4.14, κατῆλθεν → came down 4.31, συνελάλουν → spake together 4.36, ἐξεπορεύετο → went forth 4.37) or only completes the English phrasal verb (ἀροῦσίν → bear … up 4.11, ἐκλείσθη → was shut up 4.25, ἀποδοὺς → gave it back 4.20), and it stays inside the unit discontinuously under rule 15 (ἐξέβαλον → cast … forth 4.29, ῥίψαν → thrown … down 4.35, κατακρημνίσαι → throw … down headlong 4.29). About twenty units, no exceptions. The line against rule 36 is what the English preposition governs: a particle governing nothing is the verb's own, while a preposition governing an oblique noun phrase answers to that phrase's case. That is why 4.40 gives 'on' to the dative ἑνί rather than to ἐπιτιθείς, and why 4.42 keeps 'after' on ἐπεζήτουν, whose object is a bare accusative that needs no preposition.
- The chapter carries two rule 67 complex frames with different carriers and one head reading. 4.26's τῆς Σιδωνίας → 'in the land of Sidon' has no expressed preposition, so the article carries the frame and is headed on the determiner it supplies, as rule 67 requires of an article carrier. 4.27's ἐπὶ Ἐλισαίου → 'in the time of Elisha' has an expressed proper preposition, which rule 67 leaves genuinely divided; this chapter took the prepositional-word head, on 'in'. A head on 'time' remains defensible and stands elsewhere in the corpus (EPH.5.32, PHP.1.5, PHP.2.22, EPH.3.1), so a later Luke chapter departing from 'in' should say so in its note rather than treat 4.27 as settling it.
- εὐαγγελίζομαι rendered by a supplied verbal frame plus 'good tidings' heads on 'tidings' at both 4.18 and 4.43, the εὐαγγελ- lexeme being what 'good tidings' glosses under rules 66 and 91 and 'preach' being the supplied frame. Both verses take the same reading and both flag that a head on 'preach' remains defensible; do not let the two drift apart.
