# Numbers 12 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A construct numeral carrying a pronominal suffix splits: the numeral takes its own English word and the suffix takes the bare personal pronoun the ASV writes beside it, headed on that pronoun. שְׁלָשְׁתְּכֶם gives three to the numeral and ye to the suffix (12.4), שְׁלָשְׁתָּם gives three and they (12.4), שְׁנֵיהֶם gives both and they (12.5). This is rule 19's bare-pronoun sentence handing the case to rule 18. The competing reading gives they to the finite 3mp verb under rule 17 and leaves the suffix unrepresented; 12.4's ye three, which no third-person verb could carry, fixes the reading and the two they phrases follow it.
- מִחוּץ לַמַּחֲנֶה is divided the same way at 12.14 and 12.15: מִן and חוּץ share one unit rendered by the single word without, headed on חוּץ (rule 30); the לְ is unrepresented, the ASV writing no word for its own relation (rule 40, and its סָבִיב לַמִּשְׁכָּן case); and the article assimilated into the pointed לַ takes the written the (rule 12). BSB attaches the לְ to its phrase and disagrees at both verses.
- נָא takes whatever entreaty the ASV writes at that point and nothing more — now (12.6), I pray thee (12.11), I pray (12.12), I beseech thee (12.13) — the formula's pronouns being the particle's under rule 30 and an absent object simply not aligned. Where one verse writes the particle twice for a single English formula, as 12.13's אֵל נָא … רְפָא נָא does, the occurrence standing with the imperative the entreaty attaches to takes the formula and the other is unrepresented under rule 22; the competing reading assigns it to the first.
