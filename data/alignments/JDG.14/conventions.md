# Judges 14 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- be-eyney with a following rectum, where the ASV renders the phrase by a single evaluative adverb, is one fused unit of the preposition and ayin under rules 30 and 40, headed on the construct noun, while the rectum keeps its own English and is aligned separately. JDG.14.3 (be-eynay to 'well', suffix to 'me') and JDG.14.7 (be-eyney Shimshon to 'well', Shimshon to 'Samson'). Do not split the pair and do not mark the preposition unrepresented beside a noun holding the whole word: no English word in the phrase renders ayin's own lexeme, so rule 40's subtraction test finds the noun's rendering gone with the adverb.
- An intensifying adverb the ASV writes beside a Hebrew verb's own English word belongs to that verb's unit and does not move the head off the lexical verb: tsalach to 'came mightily' heads on 'came' (JDG.14.6, JDG.14.19) and hetsiq to 'pressed ... sore' heads on 'pressed' (JDG.14.17), the unit discontinuous around the object under rule 25. Rule 44's light-verb clause does not reach these, because the English verb is the lexical predicate and not a light verb; rule 44's surplus-particle sentence does not either, because the adverb renders force the source verb's own stem carries rather than something the Hebrew leaves unwritten.
