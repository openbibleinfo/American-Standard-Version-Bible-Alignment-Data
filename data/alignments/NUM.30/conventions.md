# Numbers 30 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- In the formula `אֲשֶׁר־אָסְרָה עַל־נַפְשָׁהּ` (vv. 4 ×2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11) the ASV's *wherewith* is the relative's own single-morpheme unit and is not fused with `עַל` and its noun under rule 22. The `עַל` phrase denotes her soul rather than the antecedent bond, and `נַפְשָׁהּ` holds an English word of its own, so nothing here answers to the `אֲשֶׁר בּוֹ` → *wherein* pattern; `עַל` is its own `unrepresented` unit under rule 40, because the ASV writes *her soul* as a bare direct object.
- `הָפֵר יָפֵר` (vv. 12, 15) → *make ... null and void*: the doublet's *null and* is what the ASV spends on the infinitive absolute and takes rule 56's slot, while the finite verb keeps the *make ... void* that is its rendering wherever no infinitive absolute stands (v. 8, v. 12's `הֲפֵרָם`, v. 13). The competing reading — the *and* `added` under rule 14's closing sentence, since a source morpheme's rendering stands on either side of it — is named in both units' notes and is not settled by any corpus precedent.
- Read the Hebrew for a preposition before placing the ASV's *in* on `בֵּית`. v. 3 writes `בְּבֵית אָבִיהָ`, and that `בְּ` heads the phrase's predicate and takes *being in* under rule 49; vv. 10 and 16 write the bare locative accusative `בֵּית אִישָׁהּ` / `בֵּית אָבִיהָ`, where the supplied *in* joins the noun whose case-function licenses it under rule 33. The same English phrase is therefore aligned two ways in this chapter, and the difference is in the Hebrew.
