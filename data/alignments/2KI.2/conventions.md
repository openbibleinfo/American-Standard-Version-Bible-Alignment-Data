# 2 Kings 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **2KI.2.2** — Oath formula חַי־יְהוָה וְחֵי־נַפְשְׁךָ אִם־אֶעֶזְבֶךָּ → 'As Jehovah liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee': each חַי/חֵי takes 'As … liveth' discontinuous around its noun (rule 55), the waw takes 'and', and the oath אִם takes the 'not' the ASV writes for its implicit negation (rule 11); the verb keeps only 'I will leave'. Applied identically in 2.2, 2.4, 2.6.
- **2KI.2.2** — MACULA segments בֵּית־אֵל into two Np tokens. Each takes its own English word ('Beth' / 'el'); any supplied preposition or copula for the bare place-name (directional 'to'/'unto' under rule 33, locative 'were at' under rules 20/33) joins the first token, and the second holds only 'el'. Applied in 2.2 (twice), 2.3, 2.23.
- **2KI.2.7** — מִנֶּגֶד → 'over against' is one fused unit (rule 30) headed on נֶגֶד, which carries the 'opposite' content the מִן lacks (rule 23's content-bearing sentence, not its first-morpheme default for contentless compounds); the ASV's supplied object pronoun ('them' 2.7, 'him' 2.15) answers to no suffix and is `added`. Likewise מֵרָחוֹק → 'afar off' heads on רָחוֹק and בְּטֶרֶם → 'before' on טֶרֶם.
- **2KI.2.6** — שְׁנֵיהֶם as subject or object: the numeral takes 'two' or 'both' and the suffix takes the pronoun the ASV writes for it ('they two' 2.6, 2.7, 2.8; 'them both' 2.11); the suffix is never left unrepresented beside the numeral.
- **2KI.2.6** — Particle 'on'/'away' with verbs of motion is read by rule 44's clause-level test: 2.11 'went on' keeps 'on' with the participle because the infinitive absolute הָלוֹךְ (→ 'still') writes the continuation; 2.6 'went on' for a bare וַיֵּלְכוּ leaves 'on' `added` because nothing in the clause writes it. 2.3/2.5 'take away' keeps 'away' with לֹקֵחַ because the direction is written by מֵעַל.
- **2KI.2.3** — הֶחֱשׁוּ → 'hold ye your peace' is one unit headed on 'peace' (rule 44 light verb + complement); the possessive 'your' is part of the fixed English idiom's rendering and is kept inside the unit rather than marked `added` under rule 18's closing sentence. Applied in 2.3 and 2.5; the competing reading is noted on both units.
