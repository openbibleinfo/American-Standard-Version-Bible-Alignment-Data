# Mark 7 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where the ASV renders a Greek genitive noun with 's, the article governing the head noun is unrepresented under rule 20 and the article governing the POSSESSOR takes the English 'the', headed on 'the'. ton arton ton teknon to 'the children's bread' (7:27) and ton psichion ton paidion to 'the children's crumbs' (7:28) both leave the outer article empty and align the inner one to 'the'. Rules 20 and 57 name only the outer article's emptying (tes Kaisaros oikias has no article before the possessor), so the inner article's treatment is this chapter's judgment.
- A genitive pronoun the ASV has already spent on one noun's English possessive is not available to a second noun in the same construction, so that second noun's article takes the supplied possessive under rule 76's availability test. autou eis ten kardian ... eis ten koilian to 'into his heart ... but into his belly' (7:19) gives the first 'his' to autou and the second to ten; 7:32 gives 'his' to ten with auto spent on 'upon him'. Rule 76's own examples spend the pronoun in a DIFFERENT syntactic role; this chapter reads 'available' as also covering a second instance of the same role.
- Supplied English is marked added only where no source token stands to license it. 'This he said' at 7:19 is added under rule 86 - no verb of saying stands in the verse and katharizon is already spent on 'making ... clean', so rule 103 bars folding a full lexical verb. Everywhere else the supplied English folds onto its licensing token: 'when they come' onto ap' at 7:4 under rule 62, 'as their' onto didaskalias at 7:7, 'to God' onto Doron at 7:11, and the resumptive 'it' onto pan at 7:18 under rule 103's pronoun contrast. The cross-verse candidate table cannot show this, so it is recorded rather than left to be rediscovered.
- 7:4 extends rule 62 from a verbless clause's prepositional PREDICATE to an elliptical prepositional ADJUNCT: ap' agoras to 'when they come from the marketplace' gives the whole supplied 'when they come from' to the preposition, headed on 'from', the elliptical clause having no other token to carry the supplied verb. The verse note names reading 'when they come' as added under rule 86 as the competing analysis. No corpus precedent for this shape was consulted, so a later chapter meeting it should say which reading it takes.
- A bare adverb in -then takes the whole English 'from X' phrase as one unit, headed on the word glossing the lexeme and never on 'from': exothen to 'from without' (7:15, 7:18), esothen to 'from within' (7:21, 7:23), Ekeithen to 'from thence' (7:24). The supplied 'from' spells the ablatival suffix and is not a case preposition an article could carry under rule 21, so it is not split off.
