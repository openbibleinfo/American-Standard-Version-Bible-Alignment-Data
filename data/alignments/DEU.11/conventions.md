# Deuteronomy 11 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The complementizer of the `וְהָיָה` frame is placed on the apodosis waw even where that waw falls in the next verse. DEU.11.13 gives `הָיָה` only *And it shall come to pass*, because the ASV writes no *that* inside verse 13; the *that* of *that I will give* belongs to the waw of `וְנָתַתִּי` at the head of DEU.11.14, where that verse aligns it. DEU.11.29 is the same construction inside one verse, its apodosis waw before `וְנָתַתָּה` taking the *that*. Neither `הָיָה` unit reaches across the boundary and nothing in DEU.11.13 is marked `added` or `unrepresented` on account of the missing word: read each verse's own side of the frame (rule 45).
- A relative and its resumptive share one unit wherever the ASV writes a single rendering for the pair, and are split wherever it writes a word for each. This chapter fuses `אֲשֶׁר … שָׁמָּה` → *whither* (11.8, 11.11, 11.29), `אֲשֶׁר … בּוֹ` → *whereon* (11.24), `בָּהּ` → *therein* (11.31), and `אֲשֶׁר … מִשָּׁם` → *from whence* (11.10), that last read as one compound rendering rather than two tokens under rule 30. It splits `אֲשֶׁר תִּדְרְכוּ־בָהּ` → *that ye shall tread upon* (11.25), where the relative holds *that* and the stranded preposition holds *upon*, leaving the resumptive suffix `unrepresented` under rule 22. Count the ASV's words before fusing; the two shapes stand side by side at 11.24 and 11.25.
- A preposition standing before a construct noun is fused or split by the number of renderings the ASV writes, and this chapter writes both shapes often enough that the candidate table flags it as a split when it is not one. Fused on one English word: `לִפְנֵי` → *before* (11.26, 11.32), `בִּפְנֵי` → *before* and `עַל־פְּנֵי` → *upon* (11.25), `בְּעֵבֶר` → *beyond* (11.30). Split under rule 2, each morpheme holding a word of its own: `בְּתוֹךְ` → *in the midst* (11.3), `בְּקֶרֶב` → *in the midst* (11.6), `מִלִּפְנֵי` → *from* + *before* (11.23). `עַל־פְּנֵיהֶם` (11.4) is a third case: the ASV writes nothing for the phrase and the single word present, *them*, realizes the suffix, so there is no word to fuse into and both the preposition and the construct noun are `unrepresented`.
