# Leviticus 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The offering chains mizbah ha-olah, meqom ha-olah, zevah ha-shelamim and qetoret has-sammim fall under rule 29's fronting branch, not its supplied branch. The ASV renders the rectum anarthrously (of burnt offering, of peace offerings, of sweet incense), so the the it writes for the chain is fronted and belongs to the article on that rectum, and the construct takes no supplied the. Where the chain is multi-level and the ASV writes two the's (the base of the altar of burnt offering, the ox of the sacrifice of peace offerings, the horns of the altar of sweet incense), the article takes the inner one and the outer construct keeps the second as its supplied one. Contrast the arthrous chains of this chapter, where the ASV writes a the before the rectum's own word (the head of the bullock, the blood of the sin offering, the fat of the lamb): there nothing is fronted and the construct's the is the supplied one.
- For any one of the commandments, count the phrase's determiner morphemes against the ASV's determiner words as rule 42 counts waws against and's. Where kol stands on the noun it takes the any and the numeral ahat is unrepresented (4.13); where the ASV writes both any one and all, ahat takes any one and kol takes all (4.22); where no kol stands, ahat takes the any (4.27); and where a partitive min stands before the numeral and the ASV writes any one of them, the first min takes any, ahat takes one and the second min takes of (4.2). nefesh takes any one whole where no numeral stands (4.2) and only any where one does (4.27). Each such unit names the competing reading.
- hevi et-qorbano -> bring for his oblation gives the for to the object marker (4.23), while the same clause without a marker gives the supplied for or as to qorban itself under rule 33 (4.28, 4.32). Read whether a marker stands on the noun before placing the word; neither reading leaves it added.
- kaasher rendered by the single word as is one rule 30 unit whose source head is the comparative preposition ke, not the relative asher. The chapter takes that reading at 4.10, 4.20, 4.21, 4.31 and 4.35 and names the relative as the competing head.
- Three lexical pairs fuse under rule 30 in this chapter: ben baqar -> young (4.3, 4.14), seir izzim and seirat izzim -> goat (4.23, 4.28), and be-shegagah -> unwittingly (4.2, 4.22, 4.27), the last of which the ASV may front away from its clause position (4.22). tamim and temimah are not fused: they take without blemish whole, headed on blemish.
- The recast commandment phrase miswot YHWH asher lo teasenah -> the things which Jehovah hath commanded not to be done gives the whole the things ... hath commanded to miswot, headed on commanded under rule 31, while the divine name keeps only the name it is recast as subject with. Uniform at 4.2, 4.13, 4.22 and 4.27.
