# 2 Kings 17 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Periphrastic הָיָה with a participle, where the ASV collapses the pair into a single English finite verb, gives the lexical verb to the participle, which carries the lexeme under rule 24, and leaves הָיָה only the English subject pronoun realizing its own person and number under rule 17: הָיוּ יְרֵאִים אֶת־יְהוָה → *They feared Jehovah* gives *They* to הָיָה and *feared* to the participle (17.32, 17.33). Where the ASV writes no pronoun for it — because the clause writes an explicit subject noun (וַיִּהְיוּ הַגּוֹיִם הָאֵלֶּה יְרֵאִים → *So these nations feared*, 17.29, 17.41), because a waw standing in the clause has taken the English relative under rule 17 (וַיִּהְיוּ לָהֶם עֹשִׂים → *who sacrificed for them*, 17.25, 17.32), or because a second הָיָה in the same coordination is left unrendered (17.33, 17.41) — הָיָה is its own unrepresented unit under rule 22 and the participle still keeps the verb. Nine occurrences in this chapter; BSB attaches the English verb to הָיָה in six of them, so the disagreement is this judgment and not an error.
- A pronominal suffix standing on a morpheme inside a fused expression is no part of the fusion, whatever kind of expression it is. Rule 30 states this for a preposition and its construct noun, and this chapter reads a verb-plus-noun idiom on the same terms: לְבַדּוֹ → *only* (17.18) and שָׂם שְׁמוֹ → *named* (17.34) each fuse the two morphemes the one English word carries and leave the suffix its own unrepresented unit, because neither word writes any person or number for it to take. Where the ASV does write a pronoun the suffix takes it under rule 18, as לְפָנָיו → *before him* does at 17.2. BSB attaches both suffixes to their host words.
