# Deuteronomy 29 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The Hebrew positive existential יֵשׁ is read on the terms rule 50 gives the Aramaic אִיתַי. It takes whatever overt finite frame the ASV writes, the supplied dummy subject included, headed on the copular verb rather than on the *there*: פֶּן יֵשׁ בָּכֶם אִישׁ → *lest* ***there should be*** *among you man* (DEU.29.18, twice). Where the clause writes a predicate of its own and the ASV spends no separate frame on the particle, it is `unrepresented`, and a suffix on it goes the same way under rule 22 where the relative's supplied antecedent already carries the reference: אֲשֶׁר יֶשְׁנוֹ פֹּה עִמָּנוּ עֹמֵד → *him that standeth here with us* leaves both יֵשׁ and its suffix nothing, while the אַיִן of the answering clause takes *is not* under rule 50 because no participle stands there to hold the copula.
- Where the ASV writes a lexically contentful English verb in the slot a copula would occupy in a verbless clause, this chapter joins that verb to the predicate morpheme rather than marking it `added`: מֶה חֳרִי הָאַף הַגָּדוֹל הַזֶּה → ***what meaneth*** *the heat of this great anger* gives *what meaneth* to מָה, which is the clause's predicate, on the reasoning rule 49 uses for a belonging verb written where a copula would stand and rule 20 uses for מִי הָאִישׁ הַלָּזֶה → *What man* ***is*** *this*. The same chapter reads לַיהוָה → *belong unto Jehovah* and לָנוּ → *belong unto us* that way at DEU.29.29, and יֹאבֶה → *will* at DEU.29.20, where dropping the word leaves that verb with no rendering at all. The competing reading treats *meaneth* as rule 20's wholly supplied lexical verb and marks it `added`, leaving מָה the bare *what*; it is recorded at DEU.29.24 and was not taken, since only the clause's predication and not מָה's own sense is lost when the word is dropped.
