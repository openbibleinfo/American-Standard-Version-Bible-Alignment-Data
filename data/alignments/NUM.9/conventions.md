# Numbers 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- mo'ed rendered 'appointed season' is one substantive's multi-word rendering headed on 'appointed' under rule 31's subtraction, with 'season' named in each note as the available alternative. Applied at NUM.9.2, 9.3, 9.7 and 9.13; the suffix, where present, takes the possessive separately under rule 18.
- The idiom beyn ha-'arbayim, rendered 'at even', is not rule 26's beyn X u-beyn Y: there is one beyn and no second member. The preposition takes the written 'at', the article is unrepresented because the ASV writes no 'the', and the dual noun takes 'even'. Applied at NUM.9.3, 9.5 and 9.11, and matched by the singular ba-'erev of NUM.9.15.
- peh in the command formula is split or fused by what the ASV writes, not by the phrase. 'al-pi YHWH is three units, the preposition taking whatever word stands for it ('At', 'at', or the compound 'according to' as one rendering under rule 30), the construct noun taking 'the commandment' with a supplied 'the' under rule 29, and the name taking 'of Jehovah'. le-fi at NUM.9.17 instead meets rule 30's one-rendering condition and fuses with its preposition into 'whenever'. Likewise be-yad at NUM.9.23 fuses to 'by'.
- The chapter's multi-morpheme temporal idioms are read as single fused renderings under rule 30 rather than split with parts unrepresented: yesh 'asher to 'sometimes' at NUM.9.20 and 9.21, headed on the existential particle; kol yeme 'asher to 'as long as' at NUM.9.18, headed on 'long', with the three-way split named in the note as the competing reading; and 'ish 'ish to 'any man' at NUM.9.10, headed on the noun, with the rule 22 split (first 'ish takes 'man' with its determiner, second unrepresented) named there.
- Where the ASV reorders coordinated members, each connective morpheme is matched to the pair of members the English connective actually stands between, not taken in linear order. NUM.9.10 fronts lakhem 'o le-doroteikhem ahead of the journey clause, so the first written 'or' belongs to the second Hebrew 'o and the second 'or' to the first; NUM.9.1 does the same with the two dated phrases, giving the written 'in' to the month's preposition and the recast 'of' to the year's.
