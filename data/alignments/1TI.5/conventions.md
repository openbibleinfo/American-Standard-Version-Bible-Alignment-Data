# 1 Timothy 5 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied case prepositions: where the Greek expresses no preposition and an oblique case alone governs, the supplied English preposition rides with the governed word — or with an article that takes a determiner — and never with the governing verb, even where the English reads as a phrasal verb. 5:8 *for his* (article) and *than an unbeliever*, 5:9 *of one man*, 5:11 *against Christ*, 5:17 *of honor*, 5:20 *in fear*, 5:22 *on no man* and *of sins*. The contrast is a verse where no governed token is expressed: there the particle is the English verb's own and stays in the verb unit, as 5:10 *well reported of* and *used hospitality to strangers*, 5:13 *going about*, and 5:24 *follow after* do. 5:11 was corrected to this reading, rule 36 forbidding a preposition to be read off a verb merely because *wax wanton against* reads as phrasal.
- The recurring `ὄντως χήρα` phrase is decided by rule 92's antecedent-pronoun test, not by rule 31's carve-out: the predicate noun keeps the supplied relativizer and copula in all three verses, and the article takes a pronoun only where the ASV writes one. 5:3 *Honor widows that are widows indeed* leaves `τάς` unrepresented; 5:5 *she that is a widow indeed* gives *she* to `ἡ`; 5:16 *relieve them that are widows indeed* gives *them* to `ταῖς`. The 5:3 article is a BSB disagreement and is a documented convention difference, not an error.
- One Greek verb rendered by an English phrase is headed by rule 91's test, and the chapter applies it twice over. Where the English verb glosses an element of the Greek word, the head is that verb word: 5:10 `ἐτεκνοτρόφησεν` → *brought* up children, 5:14 `τεκνογονεῖν` → *bear* children and `οἰκοδεσποτεῖν` → *rule* the household, 5:10 `μαρτυρουμένη` → well *reported* of. Where the English verb glosses nothing of the lexeme, the head is the complement: 5:4 `εὐσεβεῖν` → to show *piety*, 5:5 `ἤλπικεν` → hath her *hope* set, 5:10 `ἐξενοδόχησεν` → used *hospitality*, 5:11 `καταστρηνιάσωσιν` → have waxed *wanton*, 5:17 `ἀξιούσθωσαν` → be counted *worthy*, 5:22 `κοινώνει` → be *partaker*, 5:23 `ὑδροπότει` → be a *drinker* of water, 5:6 `σπαταλῶσα` → giveth herself to *pleasure*. A generic noun completing the phrase never takes the head, which is why 5:16 `πιστή` → woman that *believeth* heads on the verb and not on *woman*, exactly as 5:1 and 5:2 head *the younger men* and *the elder women* on the adjective.
- Complex prepositional frames are headed by carrier class, and the chapter's two readings are not a split. The improper `ἐνώπιον` → *in the sight of* heads on the frame's lexical noun *sight* at 5:4, 5:20 and 5:21 under rules 71 and 95, the trailing *of* staying inside the frame because the governed word carries no determiner and rule 95's REV.14.10 shape leaves the following article owed nothing. The proper `ἐπί` → *at the mouth of* at 5:19 falls in rule 67's still-open case and is taken on the prepositional word *at*, its note recording that the corpus also heads such a frame on its noun. Rules 67 and 95 both forbid carrying either reading across to the other carrier class.
