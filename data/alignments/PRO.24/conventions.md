# Proverbs 24 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A preposition on an absolute noun that the ASV recasts as a single English word fuses with that noun under rules 30 and 40 even where the word is a predicate adjective rather than the adverb rule 40's examples name: בְּעֹז renders is strong (24.5) as one unit headed on עֹז, with the construction-supplied copula joining that fused unit under rules 20 and 49, and בַּחוּץ renders without (24.27) on the same terms. Do not mark the preposition unrepresented beside a noun holding the whole rendering, and do not read the margin's literal gloss (Hebrew is in strength) as writing an in the printed text does not have (rule 38). An article morpheme standing in such a phrase is no part of the fusion: it takes a written the where one is written and is unrepresented where none is (24.5, 24.27).
- Where the ASV converts a Hebrew rhetorical question into an English prohibition, the interrogative particle is unrepresented under rule 17's inversion sentence and the English negative belongs to the prohibitive אַל standing at the head of the coordinated first colon under rule 25's distribution, not to the הֲ whose rhetorical force the recasting has consumed: וְהֲפִתִּיתָ בִּשְׂפָתֶיךָ renders And deceive not with thy lips (24.28), the second not going to the אַל of Be not a witness. Rule 25's distribution is read over more than two cola where the Hebrew writes the particle once: 24.12 carries one הֲ and one לֹא across three coordinated clauses, taking Doth ... doth ... shall and three nots, each headed on the first occurrence.
- Where a colon writes both a participle or substantive needing an English noun phrase and an explicit resumptive pronoun, count the ASV's pronouns against the two morphemes before placing them (rule 42's counting reasoning). PRO.24.12 writes one he in the first colon, which goes to the explicit הוּא while the participle keeps only the supplied that, and two hes in the second, where the participle takes he that under rule 46 and the הוּא takes the second he. One verse writes both shapes; the competing reading, giving he that to the participle and marking הוּא unrepresented, is named in that verse's notes.
