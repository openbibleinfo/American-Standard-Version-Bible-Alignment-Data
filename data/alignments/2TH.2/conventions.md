# 2 Thessalonians 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- 2:7 places the supplied existential there is on the substantival article that supplies one (ho katechon -> there is one that restraineth). The Greek clause is verbless and expresses no predicate at all, so rule 56 keeps the supplied verb with the subject, and the article's own nominal is that subject; rule 32's and nothing else is read as barring absorption of the participle's relative clause, not a copula predicated of the pronoun the article itself realizes, which is how rule 90 already reads it. Putting there is on the participle's unit, or marking it added, remains defensible.
- 2:9 marks the supplied appositional even its own added unit (hou he parousia -> even he whose coming). Rule 105 settles only two carriers, an expressed preposition (fold) and a substantival article (added); where the appositive is carried by a relative pronoun this chapter follows the article side, because the relative realizes no part of even and rule 77 gives it only its supplied antecedent and its relativizer. Folding even onto the relative with he remains defensible and is named in the verse note.
- 2:4 leaves the expressed finite estin unrepresented. The ASV collapses apodeiknunta heauton hoti estin theos into setting himself forth as God: as goes to hoti as the word introducing its English clause under rule 40, theos keeps God, and no English word is left for the verb. This is rule 23's reasoning applied to a finite verb rather than an article, and a reference comparison reports it as a disagreement; that report is not a correction.
- Two-word English clause-introducers are kept whole on the conjunction and headed on the first word: hoste -> so that on so (2:4) and hoti -> for that on for (2:13), on rule 61's before that precedent. Do not split the archaic that off as a separate target.
- 2:3 splits me tis -> no man, giving no to the negator and man to tis. Rule 59's plain-indefinite branch reads as though it joined the pair unconditionally, but its stated reason - that nothing is left for the indefinite to carry alone - fails where the ASV writes two words, and rule 94's precondition and rule 45's closing clause both require a single English word for one unit. The same verse's medena -> any keeps the clause's negation on me under rule 69, so 2:3 writes both halves of that test.
