# Mark 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- English that realizes a Greek verbal prefix is a rule 7 helper on the verb and is never `added`, whether it is a bare particle ('out', 'up', 'again', 'off', 'forth', 'down'), an adverbial ('round about' 9.8, 'grievously' 9.20), or a prepositional phrase with a supplied pronoun ('with him' for paralambanei 9.2, 'to him' for prostrechontes 9.15, 'in his arms' for enankalisamenos 9.36). No Greek preposition is expressed in these, so rule 36 does not reach them. The head stays on the word glossing the Greek root: the verb word where the root is verbal (9.2 'taketh', 9.15 'running'), the noun where the lexeme is denominative (9.36 'arms', on rule 104's phlogizo analogy).
- An expressed comparative adverb the ASV spells into a single comparative English word joins that word's token as one `aligned` unit: kalon + mallon -> 'better' at 9.42, under rule 39's test. Where no mallon stands, a supplied 'rather' is an ordinary helper on the comparative e (9.43, 9.45, 9.47). The ASV's own contrast is what decides it, not the reviewer's ear: 'better' where mallon is present, 'good ... rather than' where it is not.
- A supplied English subject that is a full lexical noun is `added` (9.26 'the boy' for egeneto), while a supplied subject pronoun folds onto its verb under rule 9 (9.6 'they became', same lemma, same construction). Rule 7's helper list names pronouns, and rule 103 says in terms that a full lexical noun is not among them. The ASV's italics agree here but are only evidence under rule 11.
- heos pote -> 'how long' is one unit headed on 'how' (9.19, both instances), the interrogative word realizing the source head pote, with rule 64's own gloss of the pair cited for joining. A lone degree correlative expanded into the same two English words heads on the dimension word instead: Posos -> 'How long' heads on 'long' at 9.21 under rule 104. The two readings differ because the first has a second source token to fix its head, not because the English differs.
- Interrogative Hoti opening a question takes the ASV's whole supplied frame 'How is it that' as one aligned unit (9.11, 9.28), on rule 44's pattern for a supplied interrogative frame (mepote -> 'Can it be that'). The italicized 'How is it' is not `added` and the conjunction is not left to the bare 'that'.
- kat' idian is one `noncompositional` unit on whichever single English word the ASV writes - 'apart' (9.2), 'privately' (9.28) - under rule 45, neither word answering to either token separately. The differing gloss is rule 24's habit and not two analyses.
