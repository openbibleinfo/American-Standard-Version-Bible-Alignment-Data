# Psalms 130 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The three `עִם` phrases of vv. 4 and 7 are read alike: each is its verbless clause's predicate and takes the construction-supplied copula, together with the presentative *there* where the ASV writes one (*there is with*, *with there is*, *with is*), while the single nominal of each clause — סְלִיחָה, חֶסֶד, פְּדוּת — is its subject and keeps only its own word. Rule 49's closing paragraph decides it, since the ASV writes no separate English subject in any of the three; the competing reading, which would put *there is* on the nominal and leave `עִם` a bare *with*, was named in each verse's notes and set aside.
- Verse 6 carries three ASV italics and they are analysed three ways, by rule 7. *waiteth* is a supplied lexical verb for the verbless `נַפְשִׁי לַאדֹנָי` and *wait* is a supplied predicate for the gapped comparative colon; both answer to no morpheme and are `added` under rule 20's supplied-lexical-verb clause and rule 34's boundary, and neither is folded into the `לְ` or into the participle rendered *watchmen*. The italic *more than* opening the third colon is not `added`: it is the one written `מִן`'s scope distributed over an asyndetic parallel member and joins that morpheme's discontinuous unit under rule 25. The supplied *Yea* standing beside it is `added` under rule 14, no conjunction morpheme standing anywhere in the verse.
