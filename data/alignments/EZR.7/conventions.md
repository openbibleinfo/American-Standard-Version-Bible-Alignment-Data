# Ezra 7 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Aramaic אֱלָהּ שְׁמַיָּא → 'the God of heaven' (7.12, 7.21, 7.23 twice): under current rules 15 and 29 the determined-state ending on שמיא takes the single written 'the' by fronting; אלה keeps 'God' and שמיא keeps 'of heaven'. In the inner chain of 'the house of the God of heaven' (7.23), only the inner determiner fronts; the outer house's supplied determiner remains unchanged. This supersedes the original convention's contrary ownership and exclusion of fronting for these scoped occurrences. Its cited DAN.2 precedent remains historical evidence and does not create an exception to the current contract.
- בַּעֲבַר נַהֲרָה → 'are beyond the River' (7.21, 7.25): the ב and the construct עבר share one unit with the construction-supplied copula, headed on עבר (rules 30, 49); נהרה takes 'River' and its determined-state ending takes 'the'. The preposition is never left unrepresented beside 'beyond'.
- An Aramaic participial predicate with no written subject pronoun takes only its copula and lexical verb; the subject pronoun the ASV supplies is its own `added` unit (7.14 שְׁלִיחַ → 'art sent' with 'thou' added; 7.24 מְהוֹדְעִין → 'certify' with 'we' added). A participle carries no person for rule 17's permission to reach, and no nominal earlier in the verse denotes the referent for rule 25 to resume.
- The decree formula מִנִּי שִׂים טְעֵם → 'I make a decree' (7.13, 7.21): מן is `unrepresented` under rule 40, the 1cs suffix takes the subject 'I', שים takes 'make' (or 'do make' where the ASV writes the auxiliary), and טעם takes 'a decree'. In 7.21 the second 'I' goes to the written אנה and the 'even' between them is `added` under rule 16.
- Relational דִּי standing between two nominals is aligned to the 'of' the ASV writes for it (7.12, 7.17, 7.21, 7.26), its own unit headed on 'of'; a construct noun standing before such a די keeps only its own word and any supplied 'the'.
