# 1 Thessalonians 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Stacked articles before participles divide by the count of English relativizers the ASV wrote. Where two articles stand before two participles and the ASV writes a relativizer for each — ἡμεῖς οἱ ζῶντες οἱ περιλειπόμενοι → *we that are alive that are left* at 4:15 and 4:17 — each article takes its own *that* under rules 32 and 90, the pronoun slot being already filled by the expressed ἡμεῖς, and each participle keeps its supplied copula and lexical word. Where the article instead follows an expressed nominal head that already carries its own determiner — τὰ ἔθνη τὰ μὴ εἰδότα at 4:5, τὸν θεὸν τὸν … διδόντα at 4:8, οἱ λοιποὶ οἱ μὴ ἔχοντες at 4:13 — the article is unrepresented under rule 31 and the single relativizer is the participle's helper.
- A supplied English subject for an infinitive the ASV expands into a finite clause rides with the infinitive under rule 74: *man* at 4:6, *one* at 4:9, *ye* at 4:10 and 4:11. An expressed accusative subject instead takes the English subject word as its own unit, ὑμᾶς at 4:1, 4:3 and 4:13. The consequence at 4:6 is that *no man* splits — μή keeps the bare *no*, as οὐ keeps the *no* of *no need* at 4:9 and μή the *no* of *no hope* at 4:13, and *man* belongs to ὑπερβαίνειν. Keeping *no man* whole on the negator remains defensible.
- The chapter's five supplied *even*s divide by the class of the token that carries what the *even* introduces, not by the ASV's italics. The correlative ones are aligned — to καί at 4:1, 4:5 and 4:13 under rule 82's bare-*even* arm, and inside οὕτως' *even so* at 4:14 under rule 100 — while the appositional *even* at 4:3 is `added` under rule 105's article-carrier arm, the appositive being carried by an article rule 20 has already emptied and by no expressed preposition.
