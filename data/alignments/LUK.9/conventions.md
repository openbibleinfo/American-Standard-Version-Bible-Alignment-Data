# Luke 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied English 'and' juncture: rule 98 makes it 'added' only where both of its arms hold, the seam standing between like Greek constituents AND both of them rendered alike. LUK.9.29 meets both (adjective and participle rendered as the bare predicate words 'white' and 'dazzling') and is the chapter's one 'added' juncture. Where either arm fails the juncture is a rule 88 helper on the constituent it introduces: LUK.9.16 has like Greek constituents rendered unlike (two aorist participles, 'he took' and 'and looking up'), and LUK.9.59 has unlike Greek constituents rendered alike (participle plus infinitive, 'to go and bury'), and both fold. The chapter's other fifteen junctures are ordinary participle-to-finite conversions under rule 88.
- The modal particle 'an' is never left unrepresented, and where the ASV writes no '-soever' word and no modal it is joined to the token whose single English word the pair shares, rather than split off empty: 'hosoi an' to 'as many as' at 9.5, 'heos an' to 'till' at 9.27, and 'an eie' to a bare 'was' at 9.46. This extends rule 50's refusal beyond the two realizations that rule names. Each such unit carries a note naming the competing split, since rule 72 takes the other arm for a generalizing 'ean' where no '-soever' is written; the chapter's one 'ean' (9.57, 'hopou ean' to 'whithersoever') is a '-soever' word and joins on rule 72's own first arm.
- The Lukan 'egeneto' frame is decided by the English alone. It takes 'it came to pass' whole, with its expletive subject, at 9.18, 9.28, 9.33, 9.37 and 9.51, and is its own unrepresented unit at 9.29, where the ASV writes no such clause and the verse's two verbless predicates take their own supplied copulas under rule 53 ('was altered' on heteron, 'became white' on leukos). Where the verb governs an expressed nominal subject rather than the frame, it is aligned in the ordinary way (9.34 'there came', 9.35 'came'). A reference comparison reports the unrepresented 9.29 token as a disagreement, and that report is not a correction.
- 'heneken emou' at 9.24 heads its discontinuous 'for ... sake' wrap on 'for', the prepositional word, under rule 78, not on 'sake'. Rule 71 and rule 95 would classify 'heneken' improper (one case, no verbal compounds) and settle a complex frame on the frame's lexical noun, but rule 71 is scoped to a contiguous frame, whereas rule 78 governs the discontinuous wrap and rests on rule 46's test, whether a Greek preposition is expressed. A later Luke chapter carrying 'heneken' or 'charin' should follow this and say so in the note.
