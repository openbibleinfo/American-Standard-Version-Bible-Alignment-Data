# John 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A determiner the ASV supplies for an anarthrous Greek phrase belongs to the noun that licenses it, even when an ordinal or adjective stands between them; the unit is then discontinuous under rule 15, and the modifier takes its own English word alone. So ὥρα … ἕκτη -> 'the … hour' + 'sixth' (4:6), ὥραν ἑβδόμην -> 'at the … hour' + 'seventh' (4:52), δεύτερον σημεῖον -> 'the … sign' + 'second' (4:54), matching the supplied 'a' everywhere in this chapter — 'a woman' on γυναικός across 'Samaritan' (4:9), and likewise 4:5, 4:7, 4:14, 4:19, 4:24, 4:27, 4:29, 4:44. Any case preposition realizing the noun's own case rides with the noun too. Where nothing competes the question does not arise: ὥρα -> 'the hour' (4:21, 4:23). Recorded medium, because the finished corpus is genuinely split on the ordinal case — EPH.6.2 puts the supplied 'the' on ἐντολή across 'first', JHN.1.39 puts it on δεκάτη — so this settles John 4 and is deliberately not promoted to the contract.
- Where the ASV renders a purpose ἵνα clause as a bare English infinitive, ἵνα takes the infinitival 'to' and the verb stands alone: ἵνα … ἀγοράσωσιν -> 'to' + 'buy' (4:8), ἵνα ποιήσω -> 'to' + 'do' (4:34). Rule 24 requires the two to agree, and the corpus aligns ἵνα to 'to' eleven times (JHN.3.17 does it beside a 'that' in one verse). Where a second subjunctive is coordinated under the same ἵνα, its repeated 'to' is an ordinary helper on that verb (τελειώσω -> 'to accomplish', 4:34). Elsewhere ἵνα keeps the ASV's 'that' (4:15, 4:36, 4:47).
