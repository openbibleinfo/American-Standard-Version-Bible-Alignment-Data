# Leviticus 20 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- ha-ish ha-hu and ha-nephesh ha-hiv (20.3, 20.4, 20.5, 20.6): the ASV writes one determiner, that, for the whole phrase, and the written pronoun hu/hiv takes it under rule 18, leaving both articles - the one on the noun and the one on the demonstrative - unrepresented under rule 41's limit. The competing reading fuses article and pronoun into one unit for the single word, which is how the corpus reads ha-hu at 1KI.10.10 (these); it was not taken here, because the pronoun is written and rule 41 gives the determiner to the morpheme that licenses it.
- Distributive ish ish (20.2, 20.9): the count of English words decides, not the construction. At 20.2 the ASV writes one word for the pair (Whosoever he be), so the first occurrence carries Whosoever he and the second is unrepresented under rule 22; at 20.9 it writes a word for each (every one), so rule 2 splits them one to one. The difference between the two verses is the ASV's and is not an inconsistency.
- A coordinating and the ASV supplies before a le + infinitive phrase it has recast as a coordinate clause is its own added unit and never the lamed's: 20.4 (and put him not to death) and 20.16 (and lie down thereto) were both corrected to this reading, the lamed going unrepresented under rule 40. Rule 14's closing sentence and rule 52's inventory are what forbid making a preposition the home for a coordinator; rule 26's contrary treatment reaches only the beyn X le-Y connective, which neither of these is.
- The casuistic asher of the death-penalty series takes the ASV's if (20.12, 20.13, 20.14, 20.15, 20.16, 20.17, 20.18, 20.20, 20.21): a written relative stands in each clause, so rule 55's count marks nothing added. Where the ASV keeps a relative instead (20.2, 20.6, 20.10, 20.11) the same morpheme takes that, and at 20.10 the second asher additionally takes the supplied antecedent under rule 46's rider (he that).
- ov heads on familiar in both its renderings - have familiar spirits (20.6) and a familiar spirit (20.27) - since spirit alone is any spirit under rule 31's subtraction. The supplied have of 20.6 belongs to the noun's own periphrasis, no hayah or possessive morpheme standing in that clause, whereas 20.27's hath is hayah's under rule 43.
- The waw at 20.23 takes and therefore as one rendering written at its clause's left edge, on the model of rule 55's coordinator-plus-subordinator pair, rather than taking and alone with therefore left added under rule 51's count. Rule 55's sentence names a subordinator and this is a conjunctive adverb, so the reading is an extension of it; elsewhere in the chapter a waw for which the ASV writes therefore alone takes just that word (20.7, 20.22, 20.25).
