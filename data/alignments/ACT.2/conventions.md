# Acts 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- epi to auto is decided by rule 94's word count and this chapter writes all three outcomes, which rule 24 does not require to match because what differs is the ASV's phrasing. ACT.2.1 *in one place* splits three ways (epi -> *in*, article `unrepresented` under rule 49's shape, auto -> *one place* headed on *one*); ACT.2.44 *together* is one `noncompositional` unit on epi + auto with the article its own `unrepresented` unit under rule 93; ACT.2.47 *to them* splits under rule 45's closing clause, epi taking *to* and auto *them*. Do not harmonize these toward one another.
- Where an adjacent te kai pair is rendered by a single English *and*, this chapter gives that word to kai and leaves te its own `unrepresented` unit (ACT.2.9, ACT.2.10). The assignment is arbitrary and the reverse is equally defensible, which is why it is recorded: do not flip it. Where the pair is separated by intervening words and the ASV writes two *and*s, both align in the ordinary way (ACT.2.43), as does a te standing alone (ACT.2.33, ACT.2.37, ACT.2.40, ACT.2.46).
- The vocative pair Andres adelphoi -> *Brethren* leaves andres its own `unrepresented` unit, the ASV writing only one English noun for the two (ACT.2.29, ACT.2.37), while Andres plus a gentilic -> *Ye men of ...* aligns it (ACT.2.14, ACT.2.22). The ASV's own wording decides, exactly as rule 89's test does for an article before a vocative. A reference comparison will report both empty andres tokens as disagreements, and that report is not a correction.
- dexios substantival: this chapter reads *hand* as the supplied generic completion throughout, not as part of the lexeme's own English gloss. Where the Greek is anarthrous, rule 17's generic-noun clause keeps *right hand* on the adjective headed on *right* (ACT.2.25, ACT.2.34); where a Greek article stands, rule 102 gives the article the determiner and the supplied noun, so tei dexiai -> *by the ... hand* heads on *hand* and the adjective keeps a bare *right* (ACT.2.33), as ta megaleia -> *the ... works* does at ACT.2.11. Where a possessive has already emptied the article, nothing is left for it and the supplied noun stays with the adjective (ACT.2.27's ton hosion sou -> *thy Holy One*).
- kath' hemeran -> *day by day* splits under rule 94, the ASV having written three words: the distributive *by* goes to kata and the doubled *day* to hemeran as one discontinuous unit under rule 15 (ACT.2.46, ACT.2.47). kat' oikon -> *at home* splits the same way in the same verse.
- an is never left `unrepresented` in this chapter, as rule 50 requires. Where the ASV writes a modal the particle licenses, it takes that word alone and the relative keeps its own English (ACT.2.39's hosous an -> *as many as* plus *shall*); where the ASV writes only the conjunction phrase and nothing else is left for the particle, it joins the conjunction in one `aligned` unit under rule 39's test, headed on the conjunction word (heos an -> *Till* at ACT.2.35, kathoti an -> *according as* at ACT.2.45).
- heis or hena with hekastos splits under rules 2 and 26, the numeral taking the second English word of the two-word rendering: *one* at ACT.2.3 (*each one*) and *man* at ACT.2.6 (*every man*), with hekastos taking *each* or *every*. A bare hekastos takes the whole two-word phrase headed on the quantifier under rule 17's generic-noun clause (ACT.2.8 *every man*, ACT.2.38 *every one*). Joining the pair into one unit headed on the quantifier remains defensible and is named in ACT.2.6's note.
