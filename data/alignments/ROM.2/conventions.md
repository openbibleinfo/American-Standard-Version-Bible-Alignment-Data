# Romans 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- te ... kai rendered *and also* is two written English words, so rule 70's two-conjunction arm applies and each particle keeps one: te takes *and*, kai takes *also* (2:9, 2:10). Rule 70's word *conjunction* is read as a count of the English words the ASV wrote and not as a part-of-speech test, the corpus's `unrepresented` te units being those where the ASV wrote a single *and* for the pair. ROM.1.16 reads the identical Ioudaioi te proton kai Helleni -> *to the Jew first and also to the Greek* the same way. Emptying te and giving *and also* whole to kai remains defensible.
- A postnominal attributive article whose modifier is a participle rendered as a finite clause is `unrepresented`: rule 31 leaves the supplied relativizer with the participle, and where the ASV writes no determiner for the expressed head noun the article has nothing left to carry, so rule 23 empties it. anthropou tou katergazomenou -> *of man that worketh* (2:9) and ethne ta me nomon echonta -> *Gentiles that have not the law* (2:14) are both read this way. Rule 31's clause giving a following article only *which* reaches a verbless modifier, not a participle, and rule 32's apposition clause reaches the same answer, the expressed noun having already written what the relative clause is predicated of. A reference comparison reports both articles as disagreements, and that report is not a correction. An expressed appositional predicate noun is the contrast and is rule 92's case, not this one: 2:27's ton ... parabaten gives the article only the supplied *a* and leaves *who art ... transgressor* to the noun.
- en toi phaneroi and en toi kryptoi rendered by a single English word are one `aligned` unit of preposition plus adjective headed on the adjective, with the intervening article `unrepresented` under rule 93 (2:28 twice, 2:29). Rule 93's pleonastic arm is scoped to a governed adverb, and a substantival adjective does not spell an English adverb by itself: the adverbial force of *outwardly* and *inwardly* is what en and the dative contribute, so rule 94's count test joins the pair and rule 39 keeps it `aligned`. The adjectival *outward* at 2:28 is analysed the same way under rule 24 rather than as a pleonastic en with the adjective taking the word alone, which remains defensible for that occurrence alone.
