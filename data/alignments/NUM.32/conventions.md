# Numbers 32 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A generic English noun the ASV supplies to complete a present substantive's phrase joins that substantive's unit and heads on the word carrying the lexeme (rule 31), whether the substantive is an adjective or participle - טַף to 'little ones' (vv. 16, 17, 24, 26), חָלוּץ to 'armed man' (v. 21) - or a noun the ASV recasts as the possessor of the supplied English head noun - אֲבוֹת to 'of the fathers houses' (v. 28). In this chapter 'added' is reserved for a supplied lexical verb (the 'to go' of vv. 17 and 20), a supplied possessive with no suffix in the phrase (vv. 33, 38), and an appositional 'even' standing before a phrase no object marker marks (v. 33, twice).
- Where the ASV writes 'to go before' for לִפְנֵי (vv. 17, 20), the fused לִפְנֵי takes 'before' alone and the supplied 'to go' is its own added unit under rule 20, since nothing in the clause is diminished by dropping it. Both verses are aligned that way, and both notes name the competing reading, which takes the whole 'to go before' as the preposition's rendering.
- מֵעֵבֶר לַיַּרְדֵּן is split in v. 19 and fused in v. 32 because the ASV writes three words in the one place and one in the other, not because the chapter analysed it twice: 'on the other side of the Jordan' gives 'on' to מִן, 'the other side' to עֵבֶר and 'of' to the לְ, while 'beyond the Jordan' meets rule 30's one-rendering condition, fuses מֵעֵבֶר headed on the noun and leaves the לְ unrepresented under rule 40. The same reading governs the construct מֵעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן later in v. 19, where rule 19 puts the 'of' on the rectum.
- The reflexive English pronoun beside Niphal חלץ answers to different morphemes in vv. 17 and 20, and the source licenses the difference: v. 17 writes an explicit אֲנַחְנוּ and the ASV's 'we ourselves' is emphatic apposition to that pronoun, so both words are the pronoun's; v. 20 writes no pronoun and its 'yourselves' renders the Niphal's own voice, which rule 44 keeps with the verb.
