# Judges 16 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A relative with a preposition and a resumptive suffix pointing at its antecedent is fused or split by the number of English words the ASV writes, not by the Hebrew: 16.26 reads the three morphemes as one unit on whereupon under rules 22 and 30, and 16.29, whose Hebrew clause is the same, is split because the ASV writes upon which there - the relative takes which, the preposition takes upon, and the suffix is the unrendered resumption, unrepresented under rule 22. Do not read the pair as a chapter inconsistency, and do not give which to the suffix under rule 18 while marking the written relative unrepresented. 16.11 (wherewith) follows 16.26.
- In the refrain 'wherein thy great strength lieth' (16.5, 16.6, 16.15) the ASV's lieth is the construction's linking verb for a verbless clause with an adjectival predicate, so it joins the adjective's unit and that unit heads on great (rule 20). It is not a supplied lexical verb: dropping it removes the link English writes for a predication the Hebrew has already made with the adjective, not the predication itself.
- The noun rendered inner chamber (16.9, 16.12) heads on chamber, not on inner. The ASV writes chamber alone for this word, so inner is its specifier and rule 31's class-noun test keeps the head on the noun; the competing head on inner is named in both verse notes and was declined for the chapter as a whole.
- In the doubled temporal phrase at 16.20 (rendered 'as at other times') the English noun goes to the second occurrence and the first is unrepresented, because each occurrence carries its own preposition and both prepositions are rendered, so the noun stands inside the second phrase. Rule 22's distributive paragraph, which gives the noun to the first occurrence, governs a repetition joined by a waw with a single preposition opening the phrase, and does not reach this shape.
