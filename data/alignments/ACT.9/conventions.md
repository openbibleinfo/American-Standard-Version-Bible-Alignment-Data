# Acts 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Connective relative recast as an English coordinate or causal clause: the supplied clause-introducer is the relative pronoun's own, alongside whatever personal pronoun realizes the relative, and it is neither `added` nor a rule 88 juncture on the finite verb. hos en paralelymenos -> *for he was palsied* (9:33), hoitines epestrepsan -> *and they turned* (9:35) and hon ... anegagon -> *And ... they brought him* (9:39) all read this way, under rule 40's principle that a subordinating element takes whatever word introduces its English clause. Rule 88 is scoped to a participle converted into a coordinate finite clause and does not reach a relative pronoun; giving the introducer to the verb instead remains defensible and is what 9:39 originally took.
- A verbless speech frame whose *said* the ASV supplies is placed by what is left in the clause, and by whether a verb of saying is already spent in the verse. 9:11's ho de kyrios pros auton has a prepositional predicate, so pros takes *said unto* under rule 62. 9:5's ho de has no predicate at all, and rule 56 would fold the verb onto the subject article, but eipen (s001) is already spent on the verse's first speech, so the second *said* is `added` under rule 103. The spent lexeme is what divides the two, not the word order; reading 9:5 as ho -> *he said* under rule 56 remains defensible.
- An indefinite tis with an expressed anarthrous noun rendered *a certain N* keeps the whole *a certain* as one `aligned` unit headed on *certain*, and the noun stands bare, whether the Greek writes the indefinite before its noun (9:10, 9:36) or after it (9:33). Rule 58's determiner-to-the-noun clause is scoped to an attributive adjective and does not reach an indefinite, which is a determiner in its own right. The corpus is divided: seventeen units read *a certain* on tis, two more read *a certain man* on it, and ACT.8.36 states the reasoning, while ACT.10.1 gives the noun the *a* for the identical anthropon-tina-onomati frame. This chapter follows the majority throughout.
- The chapter has two unrelated runs of untranslated Greek and they rest on different rules. 9:28's parresiazomenos en toi onomati tou kyriou is unrepresented under rule 37, its English standing at the head of 9:29, where it is `added` and both sides carry a note naming the other verse. 9:12's en horamati is unrepresented under rule 97's mirror case: SBLGNT.ACT.9.12.v010 is underdetermined between r00-base and r01, the projection carries the longer reading, and the ASV renders no *in a vision* there. In both, each untranslated token is its own unit.
