# Acts 17 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Ἄρειος Πάγος is one aligned unit for the single English 'Areopagus', headed on Πάγος, and the governing article keeps its own 'the' (ACT.17.19 τὸν Ἄρειον Πάγον → 'the Areopagus', ACT.17.22 τοῦ Ἀρείου Πάγου → 'of the Areopagus'). Rule 94's test decides it: the ASV writes one English word for the two Greek tokens, and rule 35 keeps the unit aligned because '-pagus' answers to Πάγος and 'Areo-' to Ἄρειος.
- πολιτάρχης → 'rulers of the city' is one aligned unit headed on 'rulers' (ACT.17.6, ACT.17.8). The -άρχης element is what that word glosses and 'of the city' is the supplied completion, so rule 66 heads it as ἀρχιερεύς → 'chief priest' heads on 'priest'. No token of the verse answers to 'city' separately.
- ἕως governing a genitive and rendered by the frame 'as far as' takes all three English words as one unit headed on 'far' (ACT.17.14, ACT.17.15). At 17:14 the following ἐπί keeps its own 'to' and the article its own 'the', so the frame does not reach across into the governed phrase.
- A supplied adversative juncture is placed exactly as rule 88 places a supplied 'and': at ACT.17.30 the 'but' introducing the main clause after the converted circumstantial participle ὑπεριδών is a rule 7 helper on παραγγέλλει, the finite verb it introduces. It is not `added` under rule 86, a participle standing in the clause being a licensing token, and it does not go to the article of the adverbial τὰ νῦν, which contributes no part of the English 'now' (rule 93). Rule 88's own wording names only 'and', so a later chapter meeting another adversative should say in the note that this reading rests on ACT.17.30.
- The θέλω idiom is decided by the count of English words the ASV wrote, not by the Greek. θέλει … εἶναι → 'mean' at ACT.17.20 is one `noncompositional` unit, no part of that word answering to either lexeme (rules 45, 73). ἂν θέλοι … λέγειν → 'would … say' at ACT.17.18 splits instead: λέγειν keeps 'say' and ἄν with θέλοι takes 'would' as one `aligned` unit, so that rule 50 leaves the particle nowhere empty. Giving 'would' to ἄν alone and leaving θέλοι unrepresented remains defensible and is named in that unit's note.
