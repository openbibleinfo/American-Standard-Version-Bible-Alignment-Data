# Ecclesiastes 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Possessive recastings are divided by counting the ASV's words against the morphemes standing in the clause. Where a possessive `לְ` stands and the ASV writes both a possessive verb and a separate negation or lexical word, the `לְ` takes the verb and the other morpheme keeps its own word: `אֵין לָהֶם מְנַחֵם` → *they had no comforter* (ECC.4.1 ×2) and `אֵין־לוֹ` → *he hath neither* (ECC.4.8) put *had*/*hath* on the `לְ`, *no*/*neither* on `אַיִן` and the subject pronoun on the suffix, and `וְחַם לָהֶם` → *then they have warmth* (ECC.4.11) puts *have* on the `לְ` because `חַם` already holds *warmth*. Where the ASV writes only the possessive verb, the existential particle takes it and the `לְ` goes unrepresented: `יֵשׁ־לָהֶם שָׂכָר` → *they have a reward* (ECC.4.9). Where neither a `לְ` nor a suffix stands, the existential takes the whole recast verb: `וְאֵין שֵׁנִי` → *hath not a second* (ECC.4.8, ECC.4.10). Competing reading, recorded so it is visible: the corpus aligns `אַיִן` to *hath no*, *have no* and *have not* in twelve units, some of them with a written `לְ` beside the existential (LEV.22.13, 2KI.4.14), which would keep the possessive verb on `אַיִן` throughout; this chapter follows rule 43's verbless-clause sentence instead.
- A subject the ASV supplies where no morpheme realizes it is `added` throughout the chapter, including inside an existential or verbless frame, since rule 20 and rule 50 admit only the construction's copula and its dummy *there*: the referential *he* of `וְאֵין שֵׁנִי` → *and he hath not a second* (ECC.4.8, corrected at reconciliation), *a man* for the impersonal `יִתְקְפוֹ` (ECC.4.12) and *they* for the verbless `עִם הַיֶּלֶד` clause (ECC.4.15) all go the same way. Where a suffix stands, it takes the pronoun instead (ECC.4.1, ECC.4.8 `אֵין־לוֹ`, ECC.4.9).
- `אֶחָד` recast as *alone* takes two shapes in this chapter and the article decides which. Where an article stands on the numeral, it takes the relative the ASV writes and the numeral takes *is alone* with the copula rule 20 supplies: `הָאֶחָד` → *him that is alone* (ECC.4.10, ECC.4.12). Where the numeral is anarthrous, no morpheme is present for the relative, so the whole supplied *that is alone* joins the numeral's own unit under rule 46 and heads on *one*: `אֶחָד` → *one that is alone* (ECC.4.8). At ECC.4.11 the numeral already holds the *one* the ASV wrote, so the italic *alone* standing beside that rendering is `added`, with the rule 25 second-realization reading named in the verse's note.
- ECC.4.16 `אֲשֶׁר ... לִפְנֵיהֶם` → *over whom*: the relative and the resumptive suffix share the single *whom*, headed on the relative, while the fused `לִפְנֵי` keeps the *over* written for it. Rule 22's one-English-word condition is met by *whom*, which carries the relative's relation and the resumption alike, even though the preposition is written separately rather than fused into a *wherein*; the suffix is 3mp and denotes the antecedent `כֹל`, which is the referent test that rule makes decisive. Do not mark such a suffix `unrepresented`. The supplied *them* of *all them* stays with `כֹּל` as rule 31's completion of the quantifier, because it stands outside the relative clause as its antecedent and so cannot be the clause-internal resumption.
