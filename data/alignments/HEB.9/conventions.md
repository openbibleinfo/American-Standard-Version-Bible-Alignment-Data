# Hebrews 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Substantival adjective (ἅγια, πρώτη, ἐπουράνια) with the ASV supplying a nominal: where a Greek article governs it, the article takes both the determiner and the supplied nominal and heads on that nominal — 'the … covenant' (9.1, 9.18), 'into the … place' (9.8), 'the … place' (9.12, 9.25), 'the … things' (9.23); where the adjective is anarthrous, the supplied determiner and nominal are rule 7 helpers on the adjective and head there — 'the Holy place' (9.2), 'the Holy' (9.3), 'a holy place' (9.24). The presence of the article decides, not the English, which is why the same ἅγια reads both ways in this chapter.
- Supplied English repetition of a word whose Greek token is already spent is `added` under rule 103 — 9.1 'a sanctuary', the appositional restatement of an ἅγιον already realized at t013. The exception taken here is a fixed English idiom that renders the whole Greek phrase and forces the repetition: 9.25 κατ᾽ ἐνιαυτόν → 'year by year' gives 'by' to the distributive κατά and both 'year' tokens to the one accusative, discontinuously under rule 15, the repetition being obligatory idiom as in rule 29 rather than a second realization the translator chose.
- Supplied multiword frames are headed on the prepositional word where an expressed Greek preposition carries the frame — 9.9 κατά → 'as touching the' on 'touching', 9.15 διά → 'for … cause' on 'for', 9.20 πρός → 'to … ward' on 'to' — and on the word glossing the lexeme where a noun or adverb carries it — 9.16 ἀνάγκη → 'there must of necessity' on 'necessity', 9.22 σχεδόν → 'I may almost say' on 'almost'. This is the reading rule 67 leaves open for a proper preposition; the corpus's own κατά → 'as touching' units head on 'touching' likewise, and the EPH/PHP reading that would head such a frame's noun is the competing one.
