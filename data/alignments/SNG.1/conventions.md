# Song of Solomon 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- An ASV-supplied comparative *as* standing before a bare nominal predicate is its own `added` unit. SNG.1.3 (*Thy name is as oil poured forth*), 1.13, 1.14 and 1.15 each write it in italic with no כְּ in the clause, and the predicate nominal takes only the supplied determiner rule 15 gives it and the copula rule 20 gives it, never the comparison. Where a כְּ is written it takes the word itself, as in 1.5 twice and 1.7.
- The italic *of hair* completing תֹּרִים → *plaits* in SNG.1.10 is `added` rather than folded into that noun's unit under rule 31, because SNG.1.11 renders the same lemma by *plaits* alone with a written rectum (*plaits of gold*): the ASV's whole rendering of תּוֹר is *plaits*, and the supplied prepositional phrase completes no lexeme. A supplied generic that does complete the lexeme stays in its noun's unit, as *strings of jewels* for חֲרוּזִים (1.10) and *henna flowers* for כֹּפֶר (1.14) do.
- Where the ASV recasts a verbless clause with a predicating English verb and the clause's one relational morpheme is a preposition on which the ASV has spent nothing, this chapter gives the verb to that preposition rather than marking it `added`: *have* to the לְ of לְרֵיחַ in SNG.1.3 (rule 43, the suffix already holding *Thine*) and *sat* to the בְּ of בִּמְסִבּוֹ in SNG.1.12 (rule 49). The 1.12 unit is the contestable one, since that preposition keeps its own *at*, so rule 49's subtraction test does not itself carry the verb and rule 20's supplied-lexical-verb clause is the competing reading named in the unit's note.
