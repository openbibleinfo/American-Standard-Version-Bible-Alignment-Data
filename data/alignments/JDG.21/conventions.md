# Judges 21 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Indefinite אִישׁ under a negation is read as rule 35's indefinite quantifier. Where the ASV spends a single negative word on the pair, the negative or existential and אִישׁ share one unit headed on the negation word — לֹא־בָא אִישׁ → *there came none* (21.8), אֵין־שָׁם אִישׁ → *there were none* (21.9), the latter with the existential frame joining under rule 50. Where the ASV instead writes a word for each, rule 2 splits them and אִישׁ keeps its own indefinite determiner — אִישׁ מִמֶּנּוּ לֹא־יִתֵּן → *There shall not any of us give* (21.1). Read what the ASV wrote, not the Hebrew's negation.
- A lexicalized adverbial phrase the ASV renders by one English adverb absorbs its article into the fused unit: הַיּוֹם → *to day* (21.3) and כָּעֵת → *now* (21.22, article assimilated) are one unit each, because the English word carries the definiteness as well as the noun. Rule 30's דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים sentence governs the other case, where the fused word carries nothing of the article: there the article stands outside and takes a written *the*, as בְּנֵי הֶחָיִל → *the valiantest* does at 21.10. Where the ASV writes a separate determiner instead — בָּעֵת הַהִיא → *at that time* (21.14, 21.24) — no fusion arises and rules 41 and 47 count the phrase's determiners as usual.
- For the twice-written question מַה־נַּעֲשֶׂה ... לַנּוֹתָרִים לְנָשִׁים, the identical English *for them that remain* divides two ways because the two verses write different morphemes. At 21.7 the clause also writes לָהֶם: its preposition takes the *for* standing before *them*, its suffix takes *them* under rule 18, the appositional לַ is unrepresented under rule 40, and the article keeps only the relative *that* under rule 41. At 21.16 no such phrase stands: the לַ takes the *for* and the substantival article takes the whole supplied *them that*, headed on the pronoun. Count the ASV's two *for*s against the prepositions and read which word each stands before, before treating the two verses as inconsistent.
