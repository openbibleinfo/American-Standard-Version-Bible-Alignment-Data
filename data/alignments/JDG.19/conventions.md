# Judges 19 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A directional particle inside an English phrasal verb stays with the verb in the verbal renderings listed here, and only a supplied noun or pronoun is marked `added` beside one. The particle of *went away from him* (19.2), *took her up upon the ass* (19.28), *rose up*, *lifted up*, *sat him down*, *fell down*, *went out*, *go in* and *beset round about* is part of the rendering of the verb it stands with, because in each the direction it states is written in the clause or is inside the source verb's own lexeme. The supplied objects are the other side of the same count: *your way* (19.5) and *their way* (19.14) are `added` because *go* has already written halak and *way* names a path the Hebrew does not write, while at 19.27 derekh is written and takes *way*; *her* at 19.25 and *it* at 19.30 are `added` because no object morpheme stands in those clauses, whereas the reflexive *him* of *sat him down* (19.15) and *gat him* (19.28) and the *you* of *get you early* (19.9) render the verb's own sense. At 19.25, *forth* belongs to the separately written spatial nominal *ha-huts*: its article and noun share that definite spatial rendering under current rule 30. Both independent scoped judgments preserve this baseline allocation of *forth* and make no change to the verb. This exception supersedes only the former inclusion of *brought her forth* in the verbal-particle list; the original convention is preserved in the deferred-investigation audit.
- In ha'ish ba'al habbayit hazzaqen (19.22) and ha'ish ba'al habbayit (19.23) the written *the* of *the old man* goes to the article on 'ish, the agreement article on the adjective is unrepresented under rule 47, and ba'al - a construct, which can carry no article - takes a supplied *the* under rule 29. Nothing is fronted from the article on bayit, which takes the *the* standing before its own word. JDG.19.22 was corrected to this division during the verse pass, so a later reader meeting the phrase should not re-derive it the other way.
- shakham is headed on *arose* at 19.5 and 19.8 and on *early* at 19.9, and that difference is the ASV's, not a split: *arose early* is a lexical verb with an adverb beside it, while *get you early* is rule 44's light verb, where *get* alone names nothing. The same reading governs lin, which takes *tarry all night* (19.6, 19.9) and *tarry that night* (19.10) whole and heads on *tarry*, with *night* named as the competing head.
