# 2 Timothy 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Light-verb collapse (rule 73): where the ASV renders a Greek verb-plus-noun pair by a single English verb, this chapter puts the SOURCE head on the noun carrying the lexical content and not on the finite verb - Charin echō -> 'I thank' at 1:3 and hypomnēsin labōn -> 'having been reminded' at 1:5, both headed on the noun. Rule 73 leaves that head open and the corpus takes it both ways (noun at JHN.13.10 and JHN.16.30, verb at JHN.2.25), so the choice is the chapter's rather than the contract's. Rule 73's contrast is what keeps adialeipton echō tēn mneian at 1:3 split instead, the ASV having spelt both elements out there (echō -> 'is', mneian -> 'remembrance').
- Where the ASV's verb requires an English preposition and the Greek governs its object by case alone, that preposition rides with the verb and is never 'added': epaischynthēs -> 'Be ... ashamed of' at 1:8, epaischynthē -> 'was ... ashamed of' at 1:16, apestraphēsan -> 'turned away from' at 1:15. Rule 36 scopes its own test to an expressed Greek preposition and so does not reach these. Where the ASV repeats that preposition for a second coordinated object, the repetition stays with the object it introduces rather than being absorbed into the verb's unit: mēde eme -> 'nor of me' at 1:8 gives the second 'of' to the pronoun. Reading both instances of 'of' as one discontinuous unit on the verb under rule 15 remains defensible.
- parathēkē recast as a finite relative clause (1:12 tēn parathēkēn mou -> 'that which I have committed unto him'; 1:14 Tēn kalēn parathēkēn -> 'That good thing which was committed unto thee'): the noun's own unit takes the relativizer, the supplied auxiliary or copula and the indirect object English requires, headed on 'committed'; the governing article keeps only the determiner the ASV writes, plus a supplied nominal where there is one ('thing' at 1:14, rule 102); and a genitive dependent that becomes the English clause's subject stands on its own word (mou -> 'I' at 1:12). This is rule 92's reasoning - what is left in the phrase to carry the supplied words - applied to a recast object noun rather than to a predicate noun. Reading 'which' as the article's supplied relativizer under rule 31 remains defensible at both verses.
