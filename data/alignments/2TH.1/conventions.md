# 2 Thessalonians 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A multi-word or archaic English clause-introducer is taken whole by the source token that introduces the clause and headed on its leading word, never split at the trailing *that*: ὅτι → *for that* headed on *for* (1.3), ὥστε → *so that* on *so* (1.4), εἴπερ → *if so be that* on *if* (1.6), εἰς + articular infinitive → *to the end that* on *to* (1.5), εἰς + relative → the discontinuous *To … end* on *To* (1.11), κατά → *according to* on *according* (1.12). Rule 61's refusal to split the *that* of *before that* is the model and the chapter extends it uniformly; the competing head is always the complementizer, and 1.3's note names it.
- 2TH.1.7 carries the ASV's English for 2TH.1.8's ἐν φλογὶ πυρός. Under rule 37 the three source tokens are their own `unrepresented` units in 1.8 and the three English tokens *in flaming fire* are one `added` unit at the end of 1.7, each side naming the other verse. The underdetermined variant SBLGNT.2TH.1.8.v004 covering φλογί/πυρός keeps the base among its compatible readings, so this is rule 37 and not rule 97. The BSB comparison reports all three tokens as disagreements and that report is not a correction.
- An article standing in apposition to an expressed pronoun that the ASV has already realized separately is substantival for rule 90's purposes, not a rule 31 article governing an expressed head noun: at 1.7 ὑμῖν τοῖς θλιβομένοις → *to you that are afflicted*, ὑμῖν takes *to you*, the article takes the single relativizer *that*, and the participle keeps *are afflicted* with its supplied copula. A pronoun antecedent in apposition is not a head noun the article governs, so rule 90's exclusion of units with an expressed head noun does not bite. Where the ASV writes two English words for the pair the chapter splits in the ordinary way (1.6, 1.10).
