# Exodus 36 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Rule 30 fusions are recorded `aligned` in this chapter, not `noncompositional`: מִלְּמַטָּה → *beneath* and מִלְמָעְלָה → *above*, מִלִּפְנֵי → *of*, לְבָד → *by themselves*, תּוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי → *scarlet*, עֹרֹת תְּחָשִׁים → *sealskins*, the directive-he compounds תֵּימָנָה and יָמָּה → *southward*/*westward*, the compound numerals עַשְׁתֵּי עֶשְׂרֵה → *eleven* and שִׁשָּׁה עָשָׂר → *sixteen*, and לָהּ → *thereunto*. This is a chapter convention and not a contract ruling: rule 30 fixes the unit but not its status, and the corpus is split on the question, reading לִפְנֵי as noncompositional in 225 units and aligned in 144. What matters is that one chapter answers it one way throughout.
- In the tabernacle's measurement and board formulas — אֹרֶךְ הַיְרִיעָה הָאַחַת, רֹחַב הַקֶּרֶשׁ הָאֶחָד, לִשְׁנֵי הַמִּקְצֹעֹת and their kin — the ASV's determiner is read this way throughout. אֶחָד/אַחַת takes whatever determiner the ASV writes for it, *each*, *every*, *one* or *another*, which is that numeral's own word and never the article's. The chain's articles are then decided by how many *the*s the ASV writes: where it writes one, the present article on the rectum takes it, fronted by English onto the construct, and the attributive numeral's agreement article is `unrepresented` under rule 47 (vv. 9, 15, 21, 29); where it writes none, both articles are `unrepresented` under rule 29 (vv. 22, 24, 26, 29, 30); where it writes one before each word, each article takes the one standing before its own (vv. 25, 31, 32). A *the* is never supplied on the construct while a present article morpheme in the same phrase is left unrepresented.
- The two recurring fabric phrases are divided oppositely, and the same subtraction test decides both. תּוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי is rendered by the single English *scarlet*, so the two morphemes share one unit headed on שָׁנִי (vv. 8, 35, 37); שֵׁשׁ מָשְׁזָר is not a fusion, because the ASV spends *twined* on the participle in particular, so שֵׁשׁ takes *fine … linen* headed on *linen* and the participle takes *twined* (vv. 8, 35, 37). The same asymmetry governs the material chains: עֹרֹת תְּחָשִׁים fuses into *sealskins* while עֹרֹת אֵילִם is rendered *rams skins* and splits (v. 19).
