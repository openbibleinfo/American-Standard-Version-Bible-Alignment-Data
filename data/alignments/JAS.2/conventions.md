# James 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **JAS.2.17** — Where houtōs and a separate kai stand together, houtōs takes the whole English correlative — so or even so, headed on so — and the kai is decided on its own under rule 65: aligned on also where the ASV writes one, unrepresented where it writes none. Even so is never split across the two. The corpus is unanimous at thirteen units (JHN.5.21, JHN.5.26, 2CO.1.5, EPH.5.28, 2CO.1.7, JAS.1.11 and others), and JAS.1.11 shows a preceding comparative is not required.
- **JAS.2.26** — chōris plus a genitive keeps apart from whole on the adverb, headed on apart, wherever the ASV gives the governed noun no determiner that a standing article could anchor; the presence or absence of a Greek article does not move the from (JHN.15.5, EPH.2.12 anarthrous and whole; contrast 2CO.12.3, where the article takes the ASV's the and so carries from the). Rule 21 hands a case preposition to the noun only where no improper preposition is expressed to hold it.
- **JAS.2.3** — Where a Greek article governs a substantival adjective and the ASV supplies a generic noun, the article takes both the determiner and the supplied noun and the unit heads on that noun, leaving the adjective bare — to the ... man / poor at 2.3, the ... man / poor at 2.6, the things / needful at 2.16. Anarthrous Greek is the licensed contrast and keeps the supplied noun on the adjective, as a poor man at 2.2. Every corpus unit finalized since rule 49 entered the contract reads it this way, twelve to none.
- **JAS.2.6** — Where a one-word-to-phrase rendering's generic noun is a word the corpus already spends on a different lexeme, rule 91's operational test is settled by that fact alone: kritēria to the judgment seats heads on judgment, the corpus spending seat on bēma (2CO.5.10, JHN.19.13) and the kri- word on krisis, krima and krinō in all 67 of their units.
