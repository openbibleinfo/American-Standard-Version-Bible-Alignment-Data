# Proverbs 18 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied italic verb in a colon whose Hebrew writes none: test three things in this order before placing it. First, whether the Hebrew writes that verb elsewhere in the coordination — PRO.18.3's second 'cometh' is the written בָּא realized twice and belongs to that verb's discontinuous unit under rule 34, not added. Second, whether a predicate morpheme stands in the colon for the word to link — PRO.18.12's 'goeth' links עֲנָוָה to the written prepositional predicate לִפְנֵי כָבוֹד and adds no participant, so it is rule 20's expanded copula and joins that preposition's unit under rule 49. Third, only where the word introduces an event, and often an argument, the Hebrew never wrote is it added: PRO.18.17 'pleadeth', PRO.18.19 'to be won', PRO.18.24 'doeth it'. The italic corroborates and decides none of them (rule 7).
- Construct chains rendered as English possessives (PRO.18.4, 18.6, 18.7, 18.11, 18.16): the phrase's single determiner stands before the possessor's own word and belongs to the possessor's unit under rule 29, the construct keeping only its own word — 'A fool's lips', 'A fool's mouth', 'A man's gift', 'a man's mouth'. PRO.18.11 is the case to get right: the possessor is an anarthrous substantival adjective, so עָשִׁיר takes the whole 'The rich man's' — the determiner under rule 29, the supplied generic 'man' under rule 31 with the head staying on 'rich', and the possessive — while הוֹן takes the bare 'wealth'. A construct takes a determiner of its own only where the ASV writes a second one, as PRO.18.4's 'The words of a man's mouth' does.
