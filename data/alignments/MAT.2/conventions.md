# Matthew 2 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- μάγος → 'Wise men' is one unit headed on 'Wise' (2:1, 2:7, 2:16 twice): 'men' is the generic completion and 'Wise' carries the lexeme, as rule 66's πλεονέκτης → 'covetous man' does. παιδίον → 'young child' heads the other way, on 'child' (2:8, 2:9, 2:11, 2:13 twice, 2:14, 2:20 twice, 2:21), because there the English noun glosses the lexeme and 'young' is the modifier; and παῖδας → 'male children' (2:16) heads on 'children' for the same reason. Ask which English word glosses the Greek lexeme, not which is the phrase's formal head.
- ἀκριβόω (2:7 'and learned … exactly', 2:16 'he had exactly learned') is one unit with 'exactly' inside it, discontinuously at 2:7 under rule 15, headed on 'learned'. The lexeme occurs nowhere else in the corpus, so rule 91's operational query returns nothing and rule 104's neighbouring class returns only the adverb ἀκριβῶς, which 2:8 aligns separately to 'exactly'. Heading instead on 'exactly', treating -όω as rule 66's verbalizing suffix and 'learned' as its realization, remains defensible; both verses were taken the same way.
- χρηματίζω → 'being warned of God' (2:12, 2:22): the ASV's italicized 'of God' completes this token's own English, the lexeme meaning to be divinely instructed, so it is folded here under rule 103 and not marked added, following ACT.10.22. Rule 11 makes the italics evidence rather than a verdict.
- τὸ ῥηθέν is decided by the count of English words the ASV wrote, not by the construction, which is identical in all three places. At 2:15 and 2:23 the ASV writes only 'which', which is pronoun and relativizer at once and is the article's under rule 90, the participle keeping 'was spoken'. At 2:17 it writes 'that which', so rule 32's ordinary split applies: 'that' to the article, 'which was spoken' to the participle.
