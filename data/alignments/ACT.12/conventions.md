# Acts 12 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where the ASV renders a Greek participle by an English participle, that participle licenses no supplied subject word: an English participle has no subject slot, so a supplied he or they standing in front of it belongs to the finite verb whose subject it is, discontinuously under rule 15. 12.17 gives κατασείσας a bare beckoning and he … declared to διηγήσατο. A participle the ASV renders finite is the contrast and takes the supplied subject itself, as at 12.9, 12.10, 12.12, 12.16, 12.19 and 12.25, and a genitive absolute whose own pronoun is expressed gives the subject word to that pronoun, as 12.13 gives he to αὐτοῦ while 12.18, having no pronoun, leaves the supplied it with the participle. Decide it by which verb the supplied subject is the subject of, never by the ASV's word order.
- ἐπικαλέω in the ASV's whose surname was X frame heads on surname, which is the word glossing the lexeme under rule 91; the copula rides along under rule 9 and the relativizing pronoun stays on the substantival article under rule 32 (12.12, 12.25).
- This chapter's several Greek verbs the ASV renders by a support frame whose supplied noun carries the sense are headed on that noun throughout, under rule 17: σιγᾶν -> to hold their peace on peace (12.17), ἀπαχθῆναι -> be put to death on death (12.19), ἐδημηγόρει -> made an oration on oration (12.21), ἐξέψυξεν -> gave up the ghost on ghost (12.23), πείσαντες -> made … their friend on friend (12.20). A compound Greek word whose English words each gloss one of its elements is the contrast and heads on the core under rule 66: θυμομαχῶν on displeased, ὁμοθυμαδόν on accord, σκωληκόβρωτος on eaten, διϊσχυρίζετο on affirmed. Ask first whether any English word glosses the lexeme at all.
