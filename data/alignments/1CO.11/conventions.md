# 1 Corinthians 11 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A supplied English object pronoun resuming an antecedent that is realized elsewhere is a rule 7 helper on the **verb** that requires it in English, not a discontinuous second realization of the antecedent: `παρέδωκα` → *I delivered them* at 11:2, `πιστεύω` → *I believe it* at 11:18, `ἔκλασεν` → *he brake it* at 11:24, `πίνητε` → *ye drink it* at 11:25. Rule 103 names such a pronoun as rule 7's own case but leaves the carrier open, and its 3JN.1.13 example folds onto the antecedent because there the antecedent is that same verb's own object; where the antecedent belongs to another verb or another verse, the verb that needs the pronoun takes it.
- Two **adjacent** source tokens the ASV renders by one English word are one `aligned` unit under rule 39's test, whatever their classes, with the source head on the token that word most directly realizes: `ὑμῖν αὐτοῖς` → *yourselves* (11:13, head on the intensive), `μέρος τι` → *partly* (11:18, head on the noun), `ἄχρι οὗ` → *till* (11:26, head on the adverb), `ὃς ἄν` → *whosoever* (11:27) and `ὡς ἄν` → *whensoever* (11:34) under rule 50. Non-adjacent tokens are not joined: 11:9 gives *neither* to `οὐκ` and leaves the `καί` two tokens in front of it `unrepresented` under rule 65, as rule 41 refuses to join across an intervening word.
- A Greek **adverb** the ASV expands into an *in … manner* frame keeps the whole frame as one unit and heads on the word glossing the adverb, *manner* being the generic completion: `ὡσαύτως` → *In like manner* heads on *like* (11:25) and `ἀναξίως` → *in an unworthy manner* on *unworthy* (11:27), as `πρῶτον` → *first of all* heads on *first* (11:18) and `ἱκανοί` → *not a few* on *few* (11:30). Rules 66 and 91 decide it — the corpus spends no *manner* on these lexemes — and rule 99's *what manner of* is a different frame, expanding an interrogative rather than completing an adverb.
- An article governing a substantival adjective or the identity pronoun `αὐτός` takes whatever determiner and supplied nominal the ASV writes, and the adjective or pronoun keeps only its own gloss: `τὸ αὐτό` → *the … thing* on the article and *same* on `αὐτό` (11:5), `τὰ … πάντα` → *things* on the article and *all* on `πάντα` (11:12), `Τὰ … λοιπά` → *the* on the article and *rest* on `λοιπά` (11:34). `αὐτός` is not a demonstrative, so rules 38 and 49 do not empty the article in front of it, and rule 60's `αὐτῇ τῇ ὥρᾳ` → *that very hour* is the shape. Where the Greek is anarthrous nothing competes and the whole phrase is the adjective's, as `πάντα` → *in all things* headed on *all* at 11:2.
