# Ezekiel 46 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Distributive bab-bōqer bab-bōqer → *morning by morning* (46.13, 46.14, 46.15): both nouns are rendered, so this is not rule 22's condensation of one of them. The *by* stands before the second noun, which is where the second bə stands, so that preposition takes it and the first bə — with nothing written before the first *morning* — is `unrepresented` under rule 40. Both assimilated article tokens go `unrepresented`, the ASV writing no *the* for either noun. BSB represents the first bə in all three verses; that is the documented convention difference and not an error.
- The capacity idiom yāḏ + suffix is read off what the ASV wrote, and the chapter splits it under rule 2 rather than taking it as one noncompositional unit. In the verbless mattat yāḏô (46.5, 46.11) mattat takes *to give*, yāḏ takes the discontinuous *as … is able*, and the suffix takes the subject pronoun *he* (rules 18, 40). Where a finite verb stands in the clause — kaʾăšer taśśîg yāḏô (46.7) — that verb carries *is able* under rule 44, yāḏ has no English of its own and is `unrepresented`, and the suffix still takes *he*. Each verse names the competing single noncompositional unit in its notes; BSB represents the 46.7 yāḏ.
- šām: where the ASV writes one *where* for an ʾăšer … šām relative (46.20, 46.24), the relative takes it and the resumptive šām is `unrepresented` under rule 22. Where a locative šām is written in a presentative clause — wə-hinnēh-šām māqôm → *and behold, there was a place* (46.19) — that morpheme takes the written *there* and only the copula and the indefinite article join the predicate māqôm, a present morpheme taking its English before a supplied reading does, as in rules 46, 47 and 48. The competing reading takes the *there* as rule 20's construction-supplied dummy and leaves šām `unrepresented`; it is named in that verse's notes.
- dereḵ + gate, nine occurrences across 46.2, 46.8 and 46.9: the ASV writes *by the way of …* throughout, so the construct never lacks an English noun of its own and rule 19's directional-construct clause (dereḵ → *toward*) is not reached anywhere in this chapter. The supplied *by* and *the* are placed on dereḵ itself as an adverbial accusative under rules 33 and 15 rather than read as rule 19's recast construct relation, and the rectum takes only the relational *of*. 46.8's suffixed darkô → *the way thereof* follows the same division.
