# Luke 15 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- misthios rendered 'hired servants' heads on 'hired': the misth- root is what 'hired' glosses and 'servants' is the generic completion, under rules 66 and 91. Taken at 15:17 (of my father's hired servants) and 15:19 (one of thy hired servants).
- engizo rendered by a support verb plus an adverbial heads on the adverbial, engizo being denominal on engys: 'drawing near' heads on 'near' at 15:1 and 'and drew nigh' heads on 'nigh' at 15:25, with the juncture 'and' a rule 88 helper on the verb it introduces.
- euphraino rendered by the frame 'make merry' or 'be merry' heads on 'merry' throughout, the frame verb carrying no part of the lexeme (rules 17 and 91): 15:23 'and make merry', 15:24 'to be merry', 15:29 'I might make merry', 15:32 'to make merry'. chairo beside it at 15:32 heads the same way, 'be glad' on 'glad'.
- ouketi rendered by the two-word 'no more' heads on 'no', the negation being the lexeme's core and 'more' its temporal completion. Taken at 15:19 and 15:21, which write the identical clause. Heading on 'more' remains arguable and is the reading a later reader should expect to weigh.
- drachme is rendered 'piece' throughout, and where the ASV writes 'pieces of silver' at 15:8 the whole phrase is the noun's own unit headed on 'pieces', 'of silver' being the ASV's expansion of the coin name and answering to no other token. The bare 'piece' at 15:8 and 15:9 is the same lexeme with the expansion unwritten.
- The closing refrain is written twice with different Greek and identical English, and both verses are analysed as one construction: at 15:24 anezesen supplies 'again' from the preverb and the expressed en takes 'he was' before the participle, while at 15:32 the simplex ezesen and the absent copula are what the ASV italicizes 'again' and 'was' for. Both italicized words are folded as rule 7 helpers on the tokens whose English they complete rather than marked added, no token of either verse being spent and rule 97 not applying, both variant units there being underdetermined with the base reading compatible.
