# Proverbs 11 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where a verbless colon writes a resultative lamed with its noun and the ASV supplies a whole lexical verb before it, the supplied verb, together with any subject or object supplied alongside it, is `added` under rule 20's supplied-lexical-verb clause, and the preposition keeps only the bare word the ASV wrote for it: PRO.11.19 *shall attain* beside lamed to *unto*, PRO.11.19 *doeth it* beside lamed to *to*, and PRO.11.24 *it tendeth* beside lamed to *to*. Rule 49's resultative frame was the competing reading in each and is named in the verse notes; it was set aside because dropping the English verb leaves the preposition's own result relation still stated by *unto* or *to*, and because each supplied verb introduces an event, an agent or a dummy subject the Hebrew never wrote, which is rule 20's own boundary between a construction-supplied linking verb and a supplied lexical one.
- The restrictive particle 'akh takes the *only* the ASV writes beside it and is not a home for a second connective, so the adversative *but* standing in the same colon at PRO.11.24 and the italic *But* opening the second colon at PRO.11.23 are each their own `added` unit under rule 14's closing sentences. The PRO.11.24 case is the contestable one and was decided deliberately: the ASV prints that *but* in roman while italicising the *it tendeth* beside it, but under rules 7 and 42 the absence of an italic is not evidence against an `added`, and giving 'akh both *but* and *only* would make one particle carry two English words doing two offices, which rule 51 refuses for a waw.
- A possessive the ASV writes where no genitive relation and no suffix stands behind it is `added` under rule 18's closing sentence, and both instances here carry the ASV's own italic: *his* on tiqvah at PRO.11.7 and *their* on derekh at PRO.11.20. A possessive the English verb's fixed idiom requires is not that, and stays inside the verb's single rendering rather than falling to rule 44's supplied-possessive sentence: charesh to *holdeth his peace* at PRO.11.12 is one unit headed on *peace*, on the reasoning rule 30 gives for na' to *I beseech thee*, since English does not write *holdeth peace*. The competing reading is named in that verse's note.
