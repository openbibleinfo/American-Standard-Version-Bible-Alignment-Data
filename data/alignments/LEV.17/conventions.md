# Leviticus 17 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Distributive ish ish in the casuistic protasis (17.3, 17.8, 17.10, 17.13): the first token takes the lexical 'man' together with the construction-supplied existential frame 'there be' (rule 20), and the repetition, which is what makes the phrase distributive, takes the quantifier - 'What ... soever' in 17.3, discontinuous under rule 25, and 'Whatsoever'/'whatsoever' elsewhere. The reverse assignment of the two identical tokens is defensible and is named in each unit's note. This governs only the case where the ASV writes both a quantifier and a noun; where it renders the pair by a single expression the corpus puts that expression on the first token (LEV.15.2 'any man'; EXO.36.4, EZK.14.4, EZK.14.7 'every'), and those units are not evidence against this reading.
- A copular or resumptive pronoun written in a tripartite nominal clause takes the ASV's 'is' (17.11 hi' and hu', 17.14 hu' and hi'), on the principle the contract uses elsewhere that a present morpheme takes its English before the construction supplies one; the preposition or predicate noun keeps only its own English ('in the blood', 'all one with', 'the blood thereof'). Where no such pronoun stands, the supplied copula joins the predicate as usual: 17.2 gives 'This' to zeh and 'is ... thing' to ha-dabar. The competing reading - copula to the prepositional predicate under rule 49 or to the predicate noun under rule 20, with the pronoun unrepresented under rule 22 - is named in each unit's note and is what the corpus mostly does: hu'/hi' is aligned to a bare 'is' in two units (1KI.20.3; 1KI.8.41, hedged) against 65 marked unrepresented as resumptive subjects. Nothing was promoted to the contract on this, and a later chapter may settle it the other way.
