# Ezekiel 8 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where the numeral echad follows an indefinite noun and the ASV renders the pair with a single indefinite article - `hor echad` -> `a hole` (8.7), `petach echad` -> `a door` (8.8) - the numeral is the morpheme that `a` answers to and takes it, leaving the noun its own word alone. Rule 15's clause about an indefinite article belonging to the noun governs a phrase with no numeral present, and is not a reason to mark echad unrepresented. Both verses record the competing reading in their notes.
- The adverb od is aligned to whatever the ASV spends on its additive sense, and `other` is part of that rendering rather than an `added` word: `od tashuv tireh toevot gedolot` -> `yet other great abominations` gives `yet other` to od, headed on `yet` (8.6, 8.13), while 8.15 writes only `yet` and od takes only that. Nothing else in those clauses answers to `other`; the repetition auxiliary shuv already has `again` and the adjective gedolot its own word.
- Where sham stands in the clause, the ASV's `there` is its rendering and not a presentative dummy, whatever frame the English builds around it: `hayah sham kevod` -> `the glory ... was there` (8.4) and `ve-hinneh sham ha-nashim yoshvot` -> `behold there sat the women` (8.14) give `there` to sham and leave the copula to the predicate and the verb to the participle. Rule 17's dummy `there`, which joins the verb's unit, governs only a clause with no locative adverb in it - as at 8.11, `there stood before them seventy men`, where no sham is written. Read whether sham is present before treating a `there` as supplied.
