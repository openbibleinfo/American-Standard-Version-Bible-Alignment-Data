# Philemon 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A discontinuous English wrap frame around an expressed Greek preposition stays whole on that preposition and heads on the prepositional word: 'dia ten agapen' to 'for love's sake' heads on 'for' at 1.9 and 'hyper sou' to 'in thy behalf' heads on 'in' at 1.13. This extends rule 78's settled 'for ... sake' reading to 'in ... behalf'. Rule 78 names 'in behalf of' among the contiguous multiword prepositions rule 67 leaves divided, but the wrapped form is not the contiguous one, and rule 46's test - head on the prepositional word wherever a Greek preposition is expressed - decides both alike. Each unit names the competing head on the supplied noun.
- A single Greek verb rendered by an English light-verb frame is one unit headed on the meaning-bearing complement: 'elloga' to 'put ... to account' on 'account' (1.18), 'onaimen' to 'let me have joy' on 'joy' (1.20, the imperative auxiliary 'let' riding inside the unit on rule 73's precedent rather than split off as its own head), and 'pepoithos' to 'Having confidence' on 'confidence' (1.21). None of these lexemes has a settled corpus gloss to query under rule 91, so the head rests on rule 17's meaning-bearing clause. The contrast is 1.4's 'mneian poioumenos', where the Greek expresses both elements and the ASV spells both out, so rule 2 splits the pair and rule 73 does not apply.
- Supplied English with no source token of its own is added only where nothing in the verse licenses it - 1.9's resumptive 'yet', whose concessive participle stands in 1.8, and 1.24's 'and so do', whose verb stands in 1.23 and is already spent on 'saluteth' - and is folded as a rule 7 helper wherever a token licenses it: 1.15 'from thee' on the passive verb, 1.18 'thee' and 'aught' as second realizations of 'se' and 'ti' under rule 15, 1.19 'it' on 'egrapsa'. The ASV's asterisked italics fall on both sides of that line, so under rule 11 the markup decided nothing; the test was rule 86's, whether a token of this verse carries the word.
