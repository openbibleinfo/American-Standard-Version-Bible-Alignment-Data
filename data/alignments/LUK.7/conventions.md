# Luke 7 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where the ASV renders `article + noun + article + genitive common noun` as *the X's Y*, the single English *the* goes to the article governing the genitive, because it is that word the English determiner determines, and the head noun's own article is `unrepresented` under rule 20, the possessive having taken its slot. `εἰς τὸν οἶκον τοῦ Φαρισαίου` → *into the Pharisee's house* at 7.36 and `ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ τοῦ Φαρισαίου` → *in the Pharisee's house* at 7.37 are read alike. The competing assignment — the determiner to the outer article and nothing to the inner — is recorded in 7.36's review note.
- Rule 20's second arm decides the chapter's supplied possessives, and availability rather than mere presence is the test throughout: `τοῖς δάκρυσιν` → *with her tears* gives *with her* to the article at 7.38 and 7.44, no genitive pronoun standing in that phrase, and `τὴν συναγωγήν` → *our synagogue* gives *our* to the article at 7.5, ἡμῖν having already been spent on *us* under rule 76. Where a genitive pronoun stands free the article is emptied instead, and 7.5, 7.38 and 7.44 each write both arms in one verse. Where no article stands at all the supplied possessive is a rule 51 helper on the anarthrous noun (`ἐπὶ πόδας` → *for my feet*, 7.44).
- Where the ASV realizes a clause's negation once, as *no* on a plain object noun, the Greek negator takes that *no* and the noun stands bare on its own English: `μὴ ἐσθίων ἄρτον` → *eating no bread* (7.33), `ὕδωρ μοι … οὐκ ἔδωκας` → *thou gavest me no water* (7.44), `φίλημά μοι οὐκ ἔδωκας` → *thou gavest me no kiss* (7.45), `μὴ σκανδαλισθῇ` → *shall find no occasion of stumbling* (7.23). Rules 41 and 59 do not empty the negator here, because what follows it is neither a negative word nor an indefinite. The unrelated `οὐδέ` of 7.9, whose English the ASV spreads over *not … no, not*, is its own single unit and does not bear on this.
- An English preposition or particle that belongs to a Greek verb's own English — a compound verb's prefix or the particle of an English phrasal verb — rides inside that verb's unit where no Greek preposition stands to answer to it: `συνεπορεύοντο` → *went with* (7.11), `προσδοκῶμεν` → *look … for* (7.19, 7.20), `εἰσῆλθον` → *came in* (7.45), `συνανακείμενοι` → *sat at meat with him* (7.49), `ἐξῆλθεν` → *went forth* (7.17), `προσελθών` → *came nigh* (7.14), `ἀνεκάθισεν` → *sat up* (7.15). Rule 36 governs only where a Greek preposition is expressed, and there the English preposition is given to it (`παρὰ τοὺς πόδας` → *at* 7.38, `ὑπὸ ἀνέμου` → *with* 7.24).
- Versification: `καὶ προσκαλεσάμενος δύο τινὰς τῶν μαθητῶν αὐτοῦ ὁ Ἰωάννης` stands in LUK.7.18 of the projection and is translated at the head of LUK.7.19. Rule 37 is applied on both sides — nine `unrepresented` source units in 7.18, one `added` English unit covering *And John calling unto him two of his disciples* in 7.19, each carrying a note naming the other verse — and neither verse reaches across. A reference comparison reports all nine tokens as disagreements and that report is not a correction.
