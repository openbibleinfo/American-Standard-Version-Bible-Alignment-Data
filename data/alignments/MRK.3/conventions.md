# Mark 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A supplied English preposition that marks a verb's own bare object rides with the governed word and not with the verb, whatever the object's case. MRK.3.5 περιβλεψάμενος αὐτούς → *looked round about* / *on them*, MRK.3.9 προσκαρτερῇ αὐτῷ → *wait* / *on him*, MRK.3.10 ἐπιπίπτειν αὐτῷ → *pressed* / *upon him*, MRK.3.11 προσέπιπτον αὐτῷ → *fell down* / *before him*, MRK.3.21 κρατῆσαι αὐτόν → *to lay hold* / *on him*, MRK.3.32 ζητοῦσίν σε → *seek* / *for thee*, and MRK.3.34 τούς → *on them*. Rule 106's closing clause names a bare dative or genitive; this chapter extends it to an accusative object the ASV writes with a supplied preposition, so *lay hold on* and *seek for* are not read as phrasal verbs. A preverb particle that governs nothing — *out*, *up*, *forth*, *together*, *round*, *down* — still rides inside the verb's own unit under rule 106's main clause, which is why 3.10 leaves ἐπιπίπτειν only *pressed* while 3.13 keeps *up* on ἀναβαίνει.
- No supplied English *and* in this chapter is `added`. Rule 88 places the juncture of a participle-to-coordinate conversion on the verb it introduces at MRK.3.6 (ἐδίδουν → *and took*), MRK.3.23 (ἔλεγεν → *and said*) and MRK.3.33 (λέγει → *and saith*), and MRK.3.27's εἰσελθὼν … διαρπάσαι → *enter … and spoil* is read the same way, the circumstantial participle being the token that licenses the word. Rule 98's `added` seam is scoped in terms to an asyndetic seam between two **like** constituents both rendered alike, and a participle converted into a coordinate infinitive clause is not that seam.
- ἐπιτίθημι with ὄνομα rendered by the single English verb *surnamed* is one rule 73 unit with the **source** head on the finite verb rather than on the noun, at MRK.3.16 (ἐπέθηκεν ὄνομα) and MRK.3.17 (ἐπέθηκεν ὀνόματα). Rule 10 leaves the source head of a multi-source unit open and the corpus takes this construction both ways; both instances here read it the same, and the noun remains the defensible alternative.
