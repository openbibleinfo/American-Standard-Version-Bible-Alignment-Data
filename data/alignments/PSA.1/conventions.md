# Psalms 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in `AGENTS.md` once the chapter is finished.

- **PSA.1.1** — In a terse parallel line ASV fuses the coordinating waw and the negative lo into a single 'Nor'; the two morphemes share one unit headed on the negative, since a bare waw would be 'and'.
- **PSA.1.3** — Parallelism can move a relative across the line break: ASV renders the possessive suffix of 'aleihu' as 'Whose leaf', continuing the relative clause opened by asher in the previous line. Align the suffix to that relative word.
- **PSA.1.4** — Gapping is the poetic case that looks new and is not: when a parallel line omits its verb, the copula ASV supplies joins the predicate that licenses it, exactly as in any verbless clause, and is never 'added'.
- **PSA.1.5** — The same English 'Nor' can rest on different source support: at PSA.1.1 it fuses waw and a present negative, while here the negative is gapped from the previous line and the waw alone carries it. Align what is actually there rather than assuming the earlier analysis.
- **PSA.1.1** — A construct noun cannot take the article, so a pointed 'ba-' before one is preposition-only and MACULA supplies no article token: the English 'the' is then supplied and belongs to the noun. Contrast PSA.1.5, where the same pointing before an absolute noun does carry an article token. Follow MACULA's morphology rather than the pointing alone.
