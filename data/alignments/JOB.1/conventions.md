# Job 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The apodosis waw of a וַיְהִי frame may open the following verse. In JOB.1.13–14 the frame begins at וַיְהִי הַיּוֹם (13) and its apodosis is וּמַלְאָךְ בָּא (14), so JOB.1.13's verb takes only *it fell* with no complementizer to place, and JOB.1.14's opening וּ takes *that* rather than *and* under rules 45 and 14. The chapter's other three frames (vv. 5, 6 and the same 13) keep the *that* on the apodosis waw within their own verse. Do not give a frame's *that* to הָיָה anywhere in this chapter.
- The messenger's refrain of vv. 15, 16, 17 and 19 is aligned identically in all four: the waw of וָאִמָּלְטָה takes *and*; the Niphal takes *am escaped*, headed on the participle; its paragogic he is its own `unrepresented` unit (rule 32); רַק takes *only* and the explicit אֲנִי takes *I*; לְבַדִּי is one three-morpheme unit rendered *alone* (rule 30); and in לְהַגִּיד לָךְ the first לְ takes *to*, the infinitive *tell*, the second לְ is `unrepresented` because the ASV writes a bare English object (rule 40), and its suffix takes *thee*. Align any further occurrence the same way rather than re-deriving it.
- Verses 16, 17 and 18 all read *While he was yet speaking* but do not carry the same source. Verses 16 and 17 write the adverb עוֹד, which takes *yet*, and the supplied subordinator *While* is `added` under rule 55, the two English words being separated by *he was* and so failing rule 55's contiguity test. Verse 18 writes the preposition עַד instead, which takes *While* under rule 52's temporal case, and there the intensifier *yet* is the word no morpheme answers to and is `added`. The differing `added` token across these three verses is deliberate; read which of עוֹד or עַד is written before placing either word.
- Multi-morpheme expressions in this chapter were fused or split by rule 30's one-English-rendering subtraction test, not by lemma. Fused as single units: מֵאַיִן → *Whence*, בְּתוֹךְ → *among*, שִׂים לֵב → *considered*, וְאוּלָם → *But*, אִם־לֹא → *and*, מֵעִם → *from*, עַל־יְדֵי → *beside*, מֵעֵבֶר → *from*, לְבַדִּי → *alone*, שָׁמָּה → *thither*, כָּל־הַיָּמִים → *continually*, וְלֹא → *nor*. Split under rule 2 because each morpheme keeps an English word of its own: מִסָּבִיב → *on* + *every side*, and לְפִי־חָרֶב → *with* + *the edge* + *of the sword*. A compound preposition whose second morpheme has no separate English word fuses here; one whose object is rendered by a content noun does not.
