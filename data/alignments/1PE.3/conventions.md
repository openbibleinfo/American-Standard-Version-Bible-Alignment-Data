# 1 Peter 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **1PE.3.6** — Where a Greek compound word is rendered by a multiword English expression, the target head goes to the word glossing the element that carries the lexeme's sense: agathopoieō -> *if ye do well* heads on *do* and -> *for well doing* on *doing* (3:6, 3:17), the poieō element, as 1PE.2.14, 1PE.2.15 and 1PE.2.20 have it; philadelphos -> *loving as brethren* heads on *loving*, the phil- element, as 2PE.1.7 and TIT.1.8 have it. This is not a morphological rule about which element stands second — PHP.2.3 heads tapeinophrosynē on *lowliness* and TIT.1.7 heads aischrokerdēs on *greedy* — and 3JN.1.11 dissents unhedged for the -poieō shape, heading *that doeth good* on *good*, which is why 3:6 is recorded medium.
- **1PE.3.5** — An adverb the ASV expands into a supplied *manner* frame heads on the word carrying the adverb's own sense rather than on the supplied generic noun: houtōs -> *after this manner* heads on *this* (3:5, as REV.11.5 does for the same frame), homoiōs -> *in like manner* on *like* (3:1, 3:7, as REV.2.15 and REV.8.12 do). anastrophē -> *manner of life* heads on *life* (3:16, as 1PE.1.18 and EPH.4.22 do), *manner of* being the frame. The corpus is divided and the dissent is unhedged in each arm — JHN.21.1 heads *on this wise* on *wise*, JUD.1.8 heads *in like manner* on *manner* with no note, GAL.1.13 heads anastrophē on *manner* before rule 66 entered the contract — so these units stay medium and this convention is deliberately not promoted.
- **1PE.3.7** — A Greek article governing an expressed vocative noun takes the ASV's supplied address pronoun, headed on it (3:7 Hoi -> *Ye*, husbands standing bare); where the vocative is anarthrous the pronoun is an ordinary rule 7 helper on the noun (3:1 gynaikes -> *ye wives*). EPH.6.9, REV.18.20 and REV.19.5 are the precedent, and REV.18.20 writes both halves in one verse.
- **1PE.3.16** — A substantival article before a participle takes the single English relativizer where the ASV writes only one word for the pair (3:16 hoi -> *who*, the participle keeping *revile*); where the ASV writes two words the ordinary rule 32 split applies (3:10 *He* / *that would*, 3:13 *he* / *that will harm*, 3:15 *man* / *that asketh*). EPH.2.11 states the principle; an article governing an expressed head noun is rule 31's case and stays unrepresented (3:5).
- **1PE.3.4** — Where the ASV repeats a jussive the Greek writes once, the repetition is added rather than a helper: estō is spent on *let it be* in 3:3, so 3:4's second *let it be* is its own added unit and anthrōpos keeps only *man*. EPH.5.22 and EPH.5.24 read the same ellipsis the same way.
