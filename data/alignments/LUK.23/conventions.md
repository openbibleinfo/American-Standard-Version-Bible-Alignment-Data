# Luke 23 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied English nominals fold onto the source token whose own English expression they complete, and 'added' is reserved for supplied lexical content that completes no token's expression. This chapter folds 'this place' onto hode (23.5), 'the third time' onto triton (23.22), 'the right hand' onto dexion (23.33), 'the sun's light' onto heliou (23.45, rule 52's shape, no Greek token answering to 'light'), and 'man' or 'things' onto the demonstratives at 23.2, 23.18, 23.22, 23.31, 23.41, 23.49 and 23.52. The one 'added' unit is 23.51's italicised 'a man': aner stands in 23.50 and is spent twice there, so rule 37 is not the case and rule 103's discontinuous-second-realization concession cannot be taken, rule 18 keeping the verse the whole unit of work.
- A supplied juncture 'and' between two Greek constituents rendered unlike is rule 88's conversion juncture and rides with the constituent it introduces, whether that constituent is finite (23.1 brought, 23.3 said, 23.14 said, 23.26 laid, 23.52 asked, 23.53 wrapped, 23.55 beheld, 23.56 prepared, and the 'and release him' inside the apolyso unit at 23.16 and 23.22) or is itself a participle (23.11 'and arraying', 23.40 'and rebuking'). Rule 98's 'added' seam is reached nowhere in this chapter, because no two coordinated constituents here are both rendered alike: a participle turned finite always stands beside a participle left participial or beside an expressed finite verb.
- In a periphrastic einai plus participle the copula token keeps only the finite auxiliary and the participle keeps the passive auxiliary and the lexical verb: 23.15 'hath' on estin and 'been done' on pepragmenon, 23.19 'was' / 'cast', 23.51 'had' / 'consented', 23.53 'had' / 'lain', 23.55 'had' / 'come'. Keeping 'hath been' whole on the copula at 23.15 is the competing reading and is named in that verse's note.
- Rule 38 empties an article only while the demonstrative actually occupies the English determiner slot. 23.4, 23.14 and 23.48 keep the attributive frame and leave the article unrepresented; 23.47's recast, 'Certainly this was a righteous man', makes houtos the standalone subject and writes 'a' for the article's own noun, so the article is aligned to that determiner under rules 51 and 92 and rule 92's bar on handing a supplied determiner to a noun standing behind its own article applies. The Greek is identical in all four places; the ASV's recast is what differs.
