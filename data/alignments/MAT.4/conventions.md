# Matthew 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- κύριον τὸν θεόν σου (4:7, 4:10): the supplied *the* belongs to the anarthrous κύριον as a rule 7 helper (rule 51's anarthrous clause), σου takes the determiner slot of θεόν with *thy*, and the article τόν is `unrepresented` under rule 20. Both verses read the collocation this way. Do not hand the *the* to the article standing before θεόν: the English determiner stands in front of κύριον, which has no article of its own.
- The chapter marks nothing `added`; every ASV-supplied word was traced to a token that licenses it. The two asterisked supplies are the decisive ones. At 4:21 *son* goes into the governing article's own phrase under rule 102, so τὸν τοῦ Ζεβεδαίου gives *the son* to τόν headed on *son*, leaves the inner τοῦ `unrepresented` under rule 21's determinerless clause, and gives *of Zebedee* to the name; reading *the son of* whole on the outer article under rule 52's genealogical arm remains defensible. At 4:25 the repeated *from* is a rule 7 helper on πέραν under rule 103's repeated-function-word clause, the single ἀπό governing both coordinate members.
- A particle standing with a compound verb rides inside that verb's own unit wherever no expressed preposition governs the phrase — 4:1 *was led up*, 4:12 *was delivered up*, 4:16 *did spring up*, 4:21 *going on*, 4:23 *went about*, 4:24 *went forth* — and is headed on the verb word (rule 106). At 4:4 an expressed διά does govern the phrase, so the whole English prepositional expression *out of* is the preposition's under rule 36 and ἐκπορευομένῳ keeps a bare *proceedeth*; leaving διά a bare *of* while the verb took *out* is what rule 106 forbids. The two treatments are one analysis and not a split.
- τότε is *Then* on its own at 4:5, 4:10 and 4:11, and splits at 4:17, where the expressed ἀπό takes *From* and τότε takes *that time*: the ASV wrote separate English words, so rule 94's count test forbids a compound unit. The head is *time*, the word answering to the adverb's temporal core under rule 66; heading it on *that* as the deictic element remains defensible. The difference between the two renderings is licensed by the Greek and is not a cross-verse split.
