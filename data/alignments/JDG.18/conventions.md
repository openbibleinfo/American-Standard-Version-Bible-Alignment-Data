# Judges 18 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Rule 31's subtraction test was run on this chapter's recurring nominal renderings and is settled: `פֶּסֶל` → *graven image* heads on *graven* (JDG.18.14, 17, 18, 20, 30, 31), `מַסֵּכָה` → *molten image* on *molten* (14, 17, 18), `נַעַר` → *young man* on *young* (3, 15), and `טַף` → *little ones* on *little* (21). The ASV nowhere writes *image*, *man* or *ones* alone for these words, so the generic noun is the supplied completion in each. The competing head is that generic noun in every case; do not re-open it verse by verse.
- A supplied English subject *it* with its copula joins the predicate's unit where the clause has a subject the *it* denotes — `טוֹבָה` → *it is very good* (JDG.18.9) and `אַחֲרֵי` → *it is behind*, on the preposition under rule 49 (JDG.18.12) — while the anticipatory *it* of *is it better ... to be priest* (JDG.18.19) is its own `added` unit under rule 25, because the ASV renders the extraposed infinitival subject in full. Read whether the clause's real subject stands elsewhere in the English before placing such an *it*.
- In `מַה־לְּ` with a suffix, the verb the ASV writes belongs to the preposition and the pronoun to the suffix, whichever verb it is: *hast thou* (JDG.18.3) and *have I* (JDG.18.24) under rule 43's possessive clause, *aileth thee* (JDG.18.23, 24) under rule 49's relational-verb sentence. The interrogative keeps only its own *What*, and the preposition is never left `unrepresented` here.
- A possessive the ASV supplies on a bare noun is marked `added` in this chapter even where a co-referent pronominal suffix stands in the same clause and has already been rendered: the italic *their* of *their inheritance ... had not fallen unto them* (JDG.18.1) and the *his* of *asked him of his welfare* (JDG.18.15). The competing reading is rule 18's twice-realized suffix, which would fold each word into that suffix's discontinuous unit headed on *them* and *him* respectively; both verses take the `added` reading, and the ASV's italic corroborates it at JDG.18.1 only.
- `מִשָּׁם` is divided by counting the ASV's words: *from thence* (JDG.18.11) spends a word on each morpheme, so rule 2 splits it and `מִן` takes *from*; the bare *thence* (JDG.18.13) meets rule 30's one-rendering condition, and the preposition and adverb share one unit headed on `שָׁם`, which carries the lexeme. The difference between the two verses is the ASV's and not an oversight.
