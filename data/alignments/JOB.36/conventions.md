# Job 36 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- `מָלֵא` with a bare accusative of material: the supplied *of* is the predicate's under rule 44, never the noun's under rule 33. `דִין־רָשָׁע מָלֵאתָ` (36.17) → *thou art full of the judgment* and `מָלֵא דָשֶׁן` (36.16) → *would be full of fatness* both give *of* to `מָלֵא`, headed on *full*, and leave the noun its own word; 36.16 was patched to match. Word order does not change the reading. An adjunct accusative in the same chapter keeps its own supplied preposition, as `כַּבִּיר כֹּחַ` → *mighty in strength* (36.5) and `יָזֹקּוּ מָטָר` → *distil in rain* (36.27) do, because there the ASV had a relation to choose and *full* does not.
- A subject pronoun the ASV supplies for a clause that writes no subject morpheme joins the predicate word whose agreement it realizes, and is `added` only where no morpheme in the clause carries person, gender or number for it. The predicate adjective `כַּבִּיר` takes *He is* (36.5, the subject gapped from the first line's `אֵל`) and the passive participle `אֲסוּרִים` takes *they be* (36.8), while the *thou* of `לֹא בְצָר` → *that thou be not in distress* (36.19) is `added`, rule 49 having given that clause's copula to the `בְ`, which agrees with nothing. Rule 20's copula goes to the predicate in all three, and the competing readings are named in each verse's notes.
