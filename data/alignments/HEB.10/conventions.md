# Hebrews 10 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- eis to dienekes occurs three times (10.1, 10.12, 10.14) and is analysed by the ASV's word count, not by the Greek. Where the ASV writes the single word 'continually' (10.1) the preposition is pleonastic and unrepresented and the adjective takes the word alone, following 2CO.13.2's eis to palin -> 'again' under rules 34, 93 and 94; where it writes 'for ever' (10.12, 10.14) the ordinary split applies, eis taking 'for'. The nominalizing article is unrepresented in all three. This looks like a split in the candidate table and is not one.
- A distributive kata plus noun that the ASV renders by a reduplicated English idiom - kat' eniauton -> 'year by year' (10.1, 10.3), kath' hemeran -> 'day by day' (10.11) - gives the medial 'by' to the preposition and both English occurrences of the noun to the single Greek noun as one discontinuous unit under rule 15. The repetition is obligatory English idiom for the distributive, so rule 103 does not reach it and the second occurrence is not 'added'. 10.1's note records the competing reading.
- Rule 67's divided case for a complex supplied frame carried by an expressed proper preposition is resolved one way for the whole chapter: the head goes on the prepositional word. peri -> 'sacrifices for' at 10.6 and 10.8, epi -> 'on the word of' at 10.28. The noun-headed reading (EPH.5.32, PHP.1.5, PHP.2.22, EPH.3.1) stands in the corpus and each unit's note says so; the 2 Corinthians run is the reading followed here.
- English repeated in a later verse for a source token that is already realized in an earlier one is 'added': 10.21's supplied 'having' answers to Echontes at 10.19, which is realized there as 'Having'. Rule 37 does not cover it, that rule's 'added' arm presupposing the neighbouring token is unrepresented, and rule 103 scopes its already-spent test to a single verse. The same shape inside one verse is at 10.20, where hodon is spent on 'a ... way' and the supplied antecedent 'by the way' is added.
- The English phrase 'draw nigh' takes two different heads in this chapter because two lexemes stand behind it, not because the analysis differs: prosercho(mai) heads on 'draw' at 10.1 and 10.22, its sense resting on erchomai (rule 104's contrast, 'cometh forth' on 'cometh'), while engizo heads on 'nigh' at 10.25, the engus family heading its own word throughout rule 71's list. Decided by rule 91's query on the lexeme, not by the English surface.
- Versification: the projection opens 10.17 with hysteron legei, which the ASV translates at the end of 10.16 as 'then saith he'. Both tokens are unrepresented at 10.17 and the English is added at 10.16, each side noting the other under rule 37. A reference comparison will report both tokens as disagreements; that report is not a correction, and the English must not be stretched back across the verse boundary.
