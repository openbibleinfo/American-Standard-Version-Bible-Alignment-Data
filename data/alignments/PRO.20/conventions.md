# Proverbs 20 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Distributive repetition in this chapter (`אֶבֶן וָאֶבֶן`, `אֵיפָה וְאֵיפָה`, PRO.20.10 and PRO.20.23) is read under rule 22's distributive paragraph: the first occurrence takes the noun's own English together with the ASV's supplied *Diverse* under rule 15, and the repetition's waw and its second noun are each their own `unrepresented` unit. The chapter's written *and* is then placed by the pair of items it stands between, as rule 42 directs, and not by preferring a position: PRO.20.23 writes a waw at the boundary between the two coordinated nominals and that waw takes it, while PRO.20.10 sets the weights pair beside the measures pair with no conjunction at that boundary, so its *and* is `added` under rule 14's closing sentence and both of its waws are unrepresented. The two verses look inconsistent in English and are not.
- In a verbless comparison the construction-supplied copula goes to the predicate nominal and never to the comparative, whether the comparison is carried by a written `כְ` — PRO.20.2 `נַהַם כַּכְּפִיר אֵימַת מֶלֶךְ` → *is as the roaring*, with *is the roaring* on `נַהַם` and the bare *as* on the `כְ` — or by a supplied *like*, as PRO.20.5 `מַיִם עֲמֻקִּים` → *is like deep water* puts both words on `מַיִם` under rules 20 and 33. The Hebrew, not the English word order, says which side is the predicate: the comparative phrase is an adjunct inside the predicate, which is the carve-out rule 49's closing paragraph makes. Where the ASV then writes a further English relation before the compared noun and the `כְ` has already been spent on *as*, that word joins the compared noun's unit rather than returning to the preposition, which is not a home for a second word — PRO.20.2 `כְּפִיר` → *of a lion* (rules 33, 52).
