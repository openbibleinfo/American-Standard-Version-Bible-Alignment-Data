# Job 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied English relatives are triaged by what stands in the Hebrew clause, not by whether the antecedent is written: a relative the ASV supplies for a finite clause juxtaposed to a written antecedent with no relative and no pronominal morpheme in it is `added` (3.3 *the night which said*, 3.16 *infants that never saw light*, 3.25 *the thing which I fear*); a written כִּי or אֲשֶׁר takes it (3.12, 3.25b); a pronominal suffix the recasting makes the relative clause's subject or object takes it (3.15 *that had gold*, 3.23 *whom ... hedged in*); and an anarthrous participle or adjective takes it into its own unit under rule 46 (3.5, 3.8, 3.17, 3.19, 3.20, 3.22).
- In the demonstrative phrase הַ + noun + הַ + הוּא (3.4, 3.6, 3.7) the pronoun takes the ASV's *that* under rule 41's limit, since a written morpheme licenses the determiner, and both articles are `unrepresented`, the ASV writing no *the* for the phrase. This is the source of the six Td disagreements with BSB, which attaches *that* to the article; the corpus also records a competing noncompositional fusion of הַ+הוּא, so the reading was taken deliberately rather than by default.
- English the ASV supplies by ellipsis from a neighbouring verse's parallel line is `added`: the *There* of 3.18, carried from the two שָׁם of 3.17, and the italic *Why is light given* of 3.23, carried from 3.20. Rule 10 confines the verse, so no morpheme in the verse licenses such a word; this is not rule 34's gapping, which supplies a copula inside the verse being aligned.
- Rule 44's bare-object clause is applied to a pronominal object suffix as to a bare object noun: where the English verb reaches a suffix through a preposition the ASV has written and no marker or preposition of the object's own stands, the preposition joins the verb's unit — *seek for it* (3.4), *seize upon it* (3.6), *dig for it* (3.21), *cometh upon me* (3.25) — the suffix keeping only its pronoun.
