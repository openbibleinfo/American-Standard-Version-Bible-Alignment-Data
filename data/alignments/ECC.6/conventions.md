# Ecclesiastes 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- `מַה־יּוֹתֵר לְ` + noun is written twice in this chapter and is analysed two ways, because the ASV renders it two ways. At ECC.6.8, *what advantage hath the wise* makes the clause possessive, so the `לְ` is what makes it so and carries the *hath* under rule 43. At ECC.6.11, *what is man the better* makes `אָדָם` the English subject and puts the construction-supplied copula on the predicate `יֹתֵר` under rule 20, leaving the `לְ` `unrepresented` under rule 40; giving that `is` to the preposition under rule 49 would leave it holding a word for a relation the English never states. The idiom does not fix the analysis: read the verb the ASV wrote.
- The ASV writes *and yet* at ECC.6.6 and ECC.6.7 and the two are aligned differently, because the Hebrew differs: at 6.7 a `גַם` stands in the clause and takes the *yet*, while at 6.6 only the waw stands, it already holds the *and* written for it, and the *yet* is its own `added` unit under rule 51. Reading *though … yet* as a correlative pair on the `אִלּוּ` of 6.6 under rule 14 was considered and set aside, since the ASV's *yet* stands inside the protasis rather than at the head of the main clause, which is *do not all go to one place*. The cross-verse candidate table is keyed by source lemma and so cannot show this pair at all.
