# Deuteronomy 16 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A quantifying construct noun keeps only the English written for it, and the relational *of* standing between it and its rectum belongs to the rectum under rule 19: `אַחַד שְׁעָרֶיךָ` → *any* … *of thy gates* (16.5) and `כָּל־עֵץ` → *any kind* … *of tree* (16.21). A generic noun the ASV supplies to expand the quantifier — the *kind* of *any kind of* — joins the quantifier's own unit under rule 31 and does not move the *of*; the unit heads on the word carrying the lexeme. A partitive `מִן` is not this case: `מִן־הַבָּשָׂר` → *any of the flesh* (16.4) keeps the whole *any of* on the preposition, headed on *any* under rule 24, because no construct relation stands in the phrase.
- `אֶת־פְּנֵי` before the divine name (16.16, twice) is read as the preposition H854 fused with `פָּנֶה` into the one word *before* under rule 30, headed on the construct noun, and not as an unrepresented object marker beside a noun holding the whole word. MACULA prints the class `To` for both, but glosses them *with*, where every H853 elsewhere in the chapter is glossed *(et)*; rule 16's warning against the homograph is what the division follows. Every other `אֵת` in the chapter is its own `unrepresented` unit.
- `לְשַׁכֵּן שְׁמוֹ` (16.2, 16.6, 16.11) divides as the written *to* on the `לְ` under rule 52 and the whole causative frame *cause … to dwell* on the Piel infinitive, headed on the complement verb *dwell* under rule 44, the unit discontinuous around the object noun. The `שָׁם` closing the clause is its own unit; at 16.6, where the ASV writes *to cause his name to dwell in, there thou shalt sacrifice*, both the stranded *in* and the fronted *there* are that one `שָׁם` realized twice under rule 25.
- `עֶבֶד` → *man servant* and `אָמָה` → *maid servant* (16.11, 16.14) both head on *servant*, reading *man* and *maid* as the ASV's specifiers under rule 31's tie-break, since *servant* is a noun the ASV writes alone for a word of that class. Heading each on the specifier is the defensible alternative and is named in both verses' notes. The chapter's other supplied-generic phrases go the other way on the same test, because the ASV never writes the generic alone for those words: `חָמֵץ` → *leavened bread*, `מַצּוֹת` → *unleavened bread*, `קָמָה` → *standing grain*, `גֹּרֶן` → *threshing floor*, `נְדָבָה` → *freewill offering* all head on the modifier.
