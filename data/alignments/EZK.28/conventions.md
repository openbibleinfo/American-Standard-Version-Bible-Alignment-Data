# Ezekiel 28 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה (7×: vv.2, 6, 10, 12, 22, 24, 25). The noun of אֲדֹנָי takes 'the Lord' with a supplied 'the' under rule 15, its 1cs suffix is unrepresented throughout because the ASV writes no possessive for it, and the name takes 'Jehovah'. Where the formula is the predicate of אֲנִי אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה (v.24), the construction-supplied copula joins the noun's unit under rule 20, giving 'am the Lord'. BSB represents the suffix; that is its documented convention and not evidence against this reading.
- שׁוּט with the object marker (vv.24, 26). The same Hebrew is rendered two ways and the marker follows the ASV, not the lemma: 'that did despite unto them' gives 'unto' to the marker under rule 16, because the English verb reaches its object only through that word; 'those that do them despite' writes the object bare, so the marker is unrepresented and the suffix keeps 'them'. Do not level these two.
- סָבִיב with מִן (vv.23, 24, 26). The adverbial expression — 'every side', 'round about' — is always סָבִיב's own rendering, headed on the word carrying its lexeme (rule 31); the מִן takes only whatever separate word the ASV writes for it ('on' v.23, 'of' v.24) and is unrepresented where the ASV writes none (v.26). The pair is never fused, because the ASV spends a word on each wherever it spends one at all, so rule 30's one-English-word condition is unmet.
- Supplied English relatives. Where the ASV supplies a relative that makes an articleless participle, adjective or noun into an English noun phrase or relative clause, it joins that word's own unit and is not added (vv.7, 8, 9, 18, 19 under rule 46, and v.24's מִכֹּל סְבִיבֹתָם → 'of any that are round about them', where 'that are' stays with the noun even though the antecedent 'any' is written for כֹּל). Where the Hebrew writes its own finite clause beside a written antecedent and only the subordinator is supplied, that word is added (v.3 'that', v.14 'so that'). The v.24 fold is the chapter's one debatable case: rule 46's recast-noun rider is stated for a headless clause, so the competing reading marking 'that are' added under rule 55 is defensible and was not taken.
- Surplus English 'and'. A coordinator standing between two phrases the Hebrew merely juxtaposes is added (v.5's italic 'and' between the two בְּ phrases, no waw being present). But an 'and' internal to a fixed English rendering of a single Hebrew word stays inside that word's unit: הִתְהַלָּכְתָּ → 'walked up and down' (v.14) is one rendering of the Hithpael's iterative sense, so its 'and' is written inside a rendering rather than beside one and rule 14's closing sentence does not reach it. Count the clause's waws against the ASV's connectives before applying this, as v.5 does.
