# Proverbs 15 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied determiner in an anarthrous construct chain: it belongs to the construct noun in both English orders. Where the construct is rendered attributively and stands first (`טוֹב־לֵב` → *a cheerful heart* 15.15, `מַרְפֵּא לָשׁוֹן` → *A gentle tongue* 15.4) and where the rectum is rendered attributively and the construct is the English head noun (`דְּבַר עֶצֶב` → *a grievous word* 15.1, `אִישׁ חֵמָה` → *A wrathful man* 15.18), the single written *a* or *the* is the construct noun's, and the rectum keeps only its own word together with whatever relational expression rule 19 gives it. The word standing next after the determiner is not what decides it. Rule 15's direction to take the noun of the expression governs a noun with an absolute modifier — `לֵב שָׂמֵחַ` → *A glad heart* (15.13), `כְּסִיל אָדָם` → *a foolish man* (15.20), `שׁוֹר אָבוּס` → *a stalled ox* (15.17) — where MACULA tags no construct, and read the morphology to tell the two apart.
- The chapter's four ASV italics are disposed three ways, each by a named rule, and the table of candidates cannot show it because `added` units carry no source token. *doeth* (15.7) joins `זָרָה` as rule 34's pro-verb, because the Hebrew writes the verb it stands for in the first colon. *goeth* (15.24, 15.33) joins the fused directional `לְמַעְלָה` and the fused `לִפְנֵי` as the linking verb rule 49 puts on a prepositional or directional predicate, which rule 30 already licenses in naming `לִפְנֵי` → *was before*; the head stays on the word carrying the relation. *hath* (15.15) is `added`, because rule 43's count finds no `הָיָה`, no possessive preposition, no suffix and no verb of possession in that colon. *And* (15.10, 15.30) is `added`, because neither verse writes a conjunction morpheme at all. An italic corroborates and decides nothing in every one of these, by rule 7.
