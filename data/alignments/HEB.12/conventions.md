# Hebrews 12 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied existential *there be* in a `μὴ τις` clause rides with the head of the nominal it predicates: with the expressed noun where one stands — 12:16 `πόρνος` → *there be … fornicator*, `τις` keeping a bare *any* — and with the substantival indefinite where none does — 12:15 `τις` → *there be any man*, the *man* being the generic completion and the recast participle `ὑστερῶν` keeping its own *that falleth short*. Rule 68 decides it by what is left in the clause to carry the supplied words, and whether a head noun is expressed is the same test rules 31, 47, 58 and 102 use. The two verses are one analysis and not two; do not read the difference as a split, and do not attach the frame to `μή`.
- Where the ASV realizes an intensifying prepositional prefix by a separate English adverb, the adverb is a rule 7 helper on its own word and rule 17's head stays on the stem word, never the adverb: `ἐπισκοποῦντες` → *looking carefully* on *looking* (12:15), `ἐκζητήσας` → *sought diligently* on *sought* (12:17), `προστεθῆναι` → *that more should be spoken* on *spoken* (12:19), `ἔκφοβος` → *exceedingly fear* on *fear* (12:21), `εὐαρέστως` → *well pleasing* on *pleasing* (12:28), `εὐπερίστατον` → *which doth so easily beset us* on *beset* (12:1). This is rule 104's `εὐ-` clause held for the prefix family. It does not reach a support-verb rendering, where the English verb carries no part of the lexeme and rule 17 heads the complement instead: `κάμητε` → *wax weary* on *weary* (12:3), `ἐνετρεπόμεθα` → *gave them reverence* on *reverence* and `ὑποταγησόμεθα` → *be in subjection* on *subjection* (12:9), `λατρεύωμεν` → *offer service* on *service* (12:28). Nor does it reach a two-root compound where the first element carries the distinctive sense, which is why `ὀλιγώρει` → *regard not lightly* heads on *lightly* (12:5) with its competing reading named in the unit.
- `Ἔτι ἅπαξ` (Hag. 2:6) at 12:26 and 12:27 is analysed alike under rule 24: `ἔτι` → *Yet*, and `ἅπαξ` → *once more* headed on *once*, the *more* staying with `ἅπαξ` as the completion of a single English adverbial idiom. Reading *more* as a discontinuous second word of `ἔτι` (*Yet … more*) is defensible and both verses concede it; if a later reader takes that branch, take it in both verses or in neither.
- `πανηγύρει` (12:22) is `unrepresented` and the English answering to it, *to the general assembly*, opens 12:23 as an `added` unit — rule 37's versification pair, each side naming the other verse. Do not stretch 12:22's *innumerable hosts of angels* over it, that English belonging to `μυριάσιν` and `ἀγγέλων`, and do not reach back from 12:23. Note also that 12:23's `θεῷ` takes the shared *to* because the ASV puts it first, leaving `κριτῇ` a bare *the Judge*; the two units cross under rule 15 and must not be merged.
- No matrix verb in this chapter carries a supplied complementizer. 12:19 gives *that* to the expanded infinitive `προστεθῆναι` under rule 85 and leaves `παρῃτήσαντο` a bare *entreated*; 12:25 gives the discontinuous *that … not* to the subordinating `μή` under rule 40 and leaves `Βλέπετε` a bare *See*. A clause-introducing `μή` takes whatever word introduces its English clause together with the negation, whether the ASV fuses the two as *lest* (12:15, 12:16) or spells them apart as *that … not* (12:25) — one analysis under rule 24, not two.
