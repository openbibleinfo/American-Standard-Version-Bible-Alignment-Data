# 1 Peter 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **1PE.4.2** — Supplied possessive: it goes to the first still-available token in the phrase. A case-marked or lexical possessor takes it and the article is unrepresented (4.19 tas psuchas auton -> 'their souls', rule 20 proper); where no possessor is free the article takes it (4.8 ten -> 'in your', the reflexive already spent on 'among yourselves', rule 20's second arm via rule 76); where the article is itself spent on 'the', the noun takes it (4.2 chronon -> 'of your time'). The third arm has no other corpus instance, so 4.2 is recorded medium and concedes EPH.6.5, which marked a same-shaped supplied 'your' added.
- **1PE.4.11** — One source token realized twice in the ASV is folded into that token as one discontinuous unit, not split with the supplied copy marked added: 4.11 lalei -> 'speaketh *speaking*' and diakonei -> 'ministereth *ministering*', 4.14 pneuma -> '*Spirit* Spirit'. The test is 1PE.1.11's: a token scoping a coordination or resuming before its own complements is folded, whereas a supplied phrase following a completed phrase whose every token is spent is added. 4.16's '*a man suffer*' is added because its licensing verb paschetō is spent in 4.15 — the chapter's only added unit.
- **1PE.4.7** — Where one Greek word is rendered by an English phrase, the head goes on the word the corpus uses to gloss that lexeme, never on the phrase's formal English noun (rule 17 forbids deciding from English syntax). Decided against the ASV's surface here: 4.7 sōphronēsate -> 'be ye of sound mind' heads on 'sound', the corpus glossing sōphr- 'sober' and never 'mind' (TIT.2.6); 4.4 blasphēmountes -> 'speaking evil of you' heads on 'speaking', 'speak' glossing -phēm- (TIT.3.2). The contrast is a genuinely empty support verb: 4.4/4.12 xenizō -> 'think it strange' heads on 'strange', since 'think' glosses five other lexemes and never xen-.
