# Mark 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A nominative πᾶς that the ASV expands with a supplied subject pronoun keeps both words as one unit headed on the quantifier: πάντες → *they all*, as πάντα → *all things* and πᾶσιν → *among them all* are. The pronoun is not the finite verb's rule 9 subject, because rule 9 covers a subject Greek leaves unexpressed and here a nominative token stands unspent; rule 76's availability test decides it and rule 17 holds the head. MRK.6.30, 6.41, 6.42, 6.50.
- Distributive reduplication of a noun or numeral is decided by the count of English words the ASV wrote, which is rule 94's test carried over from a compound prepositional unit. Where the ASV compresses the pair into one English phrase, the first token takes it whole and the second is its own `unrepresented` unit — συμπόσια συμπόσια → *by companies* at 6:39 and πρασιαὶ πρασιαὶ → *in ranks* at 6:40. Where the ASV writes the repetition out, the pair splits like any other and the obligatory juncture attaches to the token it introduces under rule 29 — δύο δύο → *by two* and *and two* at 6:7. Do not join the two tokens into one unit and do not stretch the single phrase across both. A reference comparison will report the compressed second token as a disagreement, and that report is not a correction.
- κατ᾽ ἰδίαν → *apart* is one `noncompositional` unit under rule 45, the single English word answering to neither the preposition nor the adjective separately, exactly as καθ᾿ ἕνα → *severally* does. The phrase is unattested in the corpus, so the reading rests on that analogy. MRK.6.31, 6.32.
- ἄν following a conjunction or quantifier that the ASV renders by an expression carrying no separate word for the particle is joined to that word as one `aligned` unit rather than left `unrepresented`: ἕως ἂν → *till* headed on the conjunction, ὅσοι ἂν → *as many as* headed on the quantitative word. Rule 50 forbids an empty ἄν and names only `-soever` compressions and counterfactual modals, neither of which reaches these. Rule 72's contrast is untouched — the same English over ἐάν leaves that particle empty. MRK.6.10, 6.56.
- Where the ASV carries a marginal *Greek …* gloss for a word it has rendered by an adjective-plus-noun phrase, that gloss is rule 91's evidence for which English word glosses the lexeme rather than completes it. δύναμις → *mighty work* and *mighty works* therefore head on *mighty*, the ASV's own note reading *power* and *powers*, and the bare *powers* of 6:14 confirms it from inside the chapter. Heading on *work* remains defensible and each unit says so. MRK.6.2, 6.5, 6.14.
- διά carrying a contiguous *for the sake of* frame heads on *for*, the prepositional word. Rule 67 leaves the head genuinely open where a contiguous complex frame rides on an expressed proper preposition, and this chapter took the prepositional-word reading throughout, following rule 46's test that the identical English heads on the noun only for the postposition χάριν and on the prepositional word wherever a Greek preposition is expressed. The noun-headed reading stands in the corpus at EPH.5.32, PHP.1.5, PHP.2.22 and EPH.3.1. MRK.6.17, 6.26.
