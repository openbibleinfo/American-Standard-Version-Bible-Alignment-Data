# Luke 19 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- engizo and eggys are analysed apart. The verb keeps 'nigh' inside its own unit headed on the verb -- 'he drew nigh' (19.29, 19.41), 'as he was now drawing nigh' (19.37) -- while the adverb takes the whole 'nigh to' headed on 'nigh' and leaves the governed name bare (19.11, rules 42, 71, 101). The two are one lexeme family and two source classes, so this is not a split.
- An English particle or adverb that realizes a Greek preverb rides inside the compound verb's own unit and is never `added`: 'thereon' for the epi- of epebibasan (19.35) and 'more' for the pros- of prosergasato (19.16), as 'up', 'down', 'in', 'out', 'round', 'through' and 'back again' do at 19.1, 19.4, 19.7, 19.15, 19.21, 19.22, 19.24, 19.26, 19.28, 19.43 and 19.45. At 19.35 'thereon' resumes the epi ton polon already spent on 'upon the colt', so reading it `added` under rule 103 was the competing analysis; it was rejected because the preverb is a source element with no other English realization.
- Where the ASV converts a circumstantial participle into a coordinate *infinitive* rather than a finite clause, the supplied juncture is rule 88's helper on the verb it introduces, not rule 98's asyndetic seam: 19.37 chairontes ainein to 'to rejoice and praise' gives 'and praise' to the infinitive. Rule 98 asks for two *like* constituents both rendered alike, and a converted participle beside the infinitive that governs it is not that pair.
- Supplied English is folded onto the token that licenses it throughout, and the chapter marks nothing `added`. The non-obvious licensers, all of which a later reader would otherwise have to rediscover: idou carries 'behold here is' (19.20), the improper preposition katenanti carries its supplied complement 'you' (19.30), an imperative carries the object English requires -- 'give it', 'bring him', 'do ye loose him', 'Trade ye herewith' (19.13, 19.24, 19.30, 19.31, rule 74's object clause) -- and the second infinitive of 19.11 carries the repeated 'because', the one expressed dia having been repeated in the coordinated clause (rule 86).
- Heads on one-word-to-phrase renderings follow rules 66, 91 and 104 rather than English syntax, and the chapter takes them all the same way: the meaning-bearing complement in a light-verb or denominative rendering -- 'haste' for speusas (19.5, 19.6), 'peace' for siopesousin (19.40), 'ground' for edaphiousin (19.44), 'Well' for Euge (19.17), 'side' for pantothen (19.43) -- and the lexeme-glossing word where the English noun glosses it, 'priests' for archiereis (19.47) and 'publican' for architelones (19.2), against 'sycomore' for sykomorean (19.4) and 'mighty' for dynameon (19.37), where the English noun is the generic completion. The last two name their competitors in their notes.
- Where pas plus a substantival article splits under rule 80's second arm and the phrase is dative, the English case preposition was kept contiguous with the quantifier: 19.26 panti to echonti to 'unto every' and 'one'. 19.24 tois parestosin to 'unto them' is not a counter-treatment, the article being the phrase's first token there. Attaching 'unto' to the article under rule 21 remains defensible and is named in the unit note.
