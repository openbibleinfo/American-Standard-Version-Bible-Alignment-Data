# Mark 10 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- An English preposition or particle that realizes a Greek verbal prefix stays inside the verb's own unit, and the verb's object keeps only its own English word: ἐμβλέψας αὐτῷ -> 'looking upon' + 'him' (10:21, 10:27), ἐμπτύσουσιν αὐτῷ -> 'shall spit upon' + 'him' (10:34), κατακυριεύουσιν αὐτῶν -> 'lord it over' + 'them' and ἄρχειν τῶν ἐθνῶν -> 'to rule over' (10:42), with the same for 'come together' (10:1), 'joined together' (10:9), 'round about' (10:23), 'going up' and 'going before' (10:32), 'come near' (10:35), 'went out' (10:46), 'casting away' and 'sprang up' (10:50), and 'rise again' (10:34). The contrast is a verb with no such prefix, where the supplied English preposition answers to the governed case alone and rides with the pronoun: γονυπετήσας αὐτόν -> 'kneeled' + 'to him' (10:17) and αἰτήσωμέν σε -> 'we shall ask' + 'of thee' (10:35). Rule 36 does not decide this, being scoped to an expressed Greek preposition; the test here is whether the verb carries a prefix for the English word to answer to.
- ἕνεκεν is treated as an improper preposition — genitive only, no verbal compound — so rule 95's classification applies and rule 71 heads its supplied complex frame on the frame's lexical noun: Ἕνεκεν τούτου -> 'For this cause' heads on 'cause' at 10:7, and both ἕνεκεν frames at 10:29 -> 'for my sake' and 'for the gospel's sake' head on 'sake'. Rule 78's eleven-to-none precedent for heading a discontinuous 'for … sake' wrap on the prepositional word is real but carries a proper διά in every one of its units, so it does not reach this lexeme. The chapter follows rule 46's own test — the head goes to whichever word carries the postposition's or improper preposition's sense — and a reader who prefers rule 78 should change all three units together.
- Rule 43's English-word test is applied to both arms in this chapter, and the ASV's supplied word counts as an English word: at 10:33 ὅτι introducing direct speech is aligned to the italicized 'saying' the ASV supplies for it rather than left unrepresented with 'saying' marked added, while at 10:2 the interrogative εἰ is unrepresented because the ASV converts the indirect question into direct speech and writes nothing for it. Both readings turn on what the ASV wrote, not on whether the Greek clause is recitative; the BSB comparison reports the 10:2 εἰ as a disagreement and that report is not a correction.
