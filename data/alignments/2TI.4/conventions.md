# 2 Timothy 4 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- σπουδάζω → *Give diligence* is one unit headed on *diligence* (4:9, 4:21). *Give* is an empty support verb carrying no part of the lexeme, and rule 91's operational test puts the head on the word the corpus spends on σπουδ-, which rule 66 records in terms when it glosses that lexeme *diligence* and never *care*.
- A supplied English object pronoun folds onto the verb that requires it in English and is never `added`: *thee* on Διαμαρτύρομαι (4:1), *him* on ἄγε (4:11), *me* on σώσει (4:18). This is rule 74's object contrast, and rule 103 is not the case, because no source token has already been spent on these words — rule 7 names pronouns as helpers outright.
- τὴν ἀκοήν is analysed two ways because the ASV is: 4:3 writes a bare *ears* and the article is `unrepresented` under rule 23; 4:4 writes *their ears* with no genitive pronoun in the clause, so the article takes *their* under rule 20's second arm. Checked in reconciliation against the identical Greek and recorded as licensed, not as a split.
- An adverb the ASV renders by preposition plus noun is one `aligned` unit headed on the noun: εὐκαίρως → *in season* and ἀκαίρως → *out of season* (4:2), analysed alike. The preposition spells the adverb's prefix — the alpha-privative in the second, nothing lexical in the first — and the noun glosses the καιρ- root, which is rule 84's reasoning applied to a positive adverb as well as a privative one.
- A verbless clause's supplied copula goes to the predicate, including a predicate expressed by a bare Greek case: 4:18's ᾧ ἡ δόξα → *to whom be the glory* puts *be* on the dative relative with its case preposition under rules 53 and 21, not on the noun-phrase subject *the glory*. Rule 53's list names only prepositional, adjectival and adverbial predicates, but its own reasoning about what is left to carry the copula decides this — the subject spells its article and noun out in words already answering to those tokens, and the dative is the one token whose English the copula predicates. 4:22 puts both supplied copulas on μετά the same way.
