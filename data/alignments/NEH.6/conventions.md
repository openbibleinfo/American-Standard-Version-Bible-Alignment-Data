# Nehemiah 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **NEH.6.1** — The vayehi frame of 6.1 has its apodosis at the head of 6.2. The frame's complementizer 'that' (ASV 6.2 t001) is aligned in 6.2 to that verse's opening waw under rule 45; 6.1's hayah takes only 'it came to pass'. 6.16 is the ordinary in-verse case and divides the same way.
- **NEH.6.4** — ka-davar ha-zeh / ka-devarim ha-elleh (6.4, 6.5, 6.6, 6.7, 6.8): the ke takes the ASV's own preposition (after / in / according to) in the separately rendered relations listed here. At 6.8, כְּ and אֵלֶּה instead jointly render *such* as one discontinuous aligned unit, headed on the governing comparative כְּ under rule 23; the assimilated article and the agreement article are both unrepresented unless the ASV writes a 'the' before the noun (6.4 'the same manner'); the noun takes sort / manner / words / things; apart from the specified 6.8 fusion, the demonstrative takes whatever stands in the determiner slot — this, same, like, these, such — under rule 41. In 6.8 the correlative 'as' goes to asher, not to the ke. The 6.8 fusion and comparative head follow the original independent confirmation and the fresh scoped adjudication; the first assessor proposed the same fusion with the demonstrative as head. This supersedes only the former 6.8 unrepresented-ke and demonstrative-only-such allocation. The independently rendered noun, the relative and its as, and the other listed constructions remain unchanged; the original convention and all three judgments are preserved in the deferred-investigation audit.
- **NEH.6.14** — Periphrastic hayu + participle (6.14, 6.19 twice): hayu takes the auxiliary or subject pronoun the ASV writes for the finite verb ('would have', 'they') and is its own unrepresented unit where it writes neither (6.19 'and reported'); the participle keeps the lexical verb phrase with any supplied preposition or complement ('put ... in fear', 'spake of', 'reported'). Where no hayah stands (6.9) the bare participle takes the whole 'would have made afraid'.
