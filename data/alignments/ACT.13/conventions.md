# Acts 13 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Two rule 37 versification seams. The duration phrase ὡς ἔτεσι τετρακοσίοις καὶ πεντήκοντα opens ACT.13.20 in the projection and is translated at the end of ACT.13.19, and the clause καὶ ἀπὸ πάντων ὧν οὐκ ἠδυνήθητε ἐν νόμῳ Μωϋσέως δικαιωθῆναι closes ACT.13.38 and is translated in ACT.13.39. Each side stays inside its own verse: the orphaned source tokens are unrepresented with a note naming the other verse, and the English is `added` there with the same note. A reference comparison reports every one of these tokens; that is not a correction.
- The vocative formula Ἄνδρες ἀδελφοί is collapsed by the ASV into the single word *Brethren*, so ἀδελφοί takes it and Ἄνδρες is its own unrepresented unit (13.15, 13.26, 13.38). Ἄνδρες Ἰσραηλῖται is the contrast and is licensed by the ASV, which writes *Men of Israel*: there Ἄνδρες takes *Men* and the gentilic takes *of Israel* (13.16).
- A supplied English preposition governing a bare accusative object rides with the verb, not the noun: no Greek preposition stands and the accusative is not an oblique whose case rule 21 would render, so the preposition is English verb government and a rule 7 helper on the verb. ᾐτήσαντο βασιλέα -> *they asked for a king* (13.21) and ᾐτήσαντο Πιλᾶτον -> *asked they of Pilate* (13.28) are analysed alike. The chapter's 'case preposition stays with the name/noun' notes are scoped to genitives and datives (13.14, 13.23, 13.25, 13.26, 13.36, 13.45, 13.46, 13.52).
- Where an article governs a substantival adjective and the ASV completes it with a nominal, the nominal is the article's and the adjective stands bare, headed on the nominal: τὰ ὅσια -> *the … blessings* (13.34), τοὺς πρώτους -> *the … men* (13.50), τὸν ὅσιόν σου -> *thy Holy One* with *One* on the article (13.35), a genitive pronoun in the determiner slot changing nothing under rule 54. Anarthrous Greek is the contrast and the adjective keeps both the supplied determiner and the supplied noun: ἑτέρῳ -> *another psalm* (13.35), ἐσχάτου -> *the uttermost part* (13.47).
- τε is decided on the English alone. In the correlative τε … καί of 13.1 the ASV writes one *and*, which is καί's, so τε is its own unrepresented unit; a τε the ASV realizes with a connective — verse-initially at 13.46 and 13.52, and clause-internally at 13.1 and 13.4 — is aligned to that word.
- Prepositions whose phrase the ASV recasts. Where the recast clause has an English introducer, the preposition takes it: ἐν τῇ παροικίᾳ -> *when they sojourned* gives *when* to ἐν and leaves the article unrepresented (13.17). Where the recasting or the bare temporal phrase leaves no English word, the preposition is unrepresented: ἐν ὑμῖν recast as the subject of *if ye have* (13.15), κατὰ πᾶν σάββατον -> *every sabbath* (13.27), εἰς τὸ μεταξὺ σάββατον -> *the next sabbath* (13.42).
