# Luke 13 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- ἀφ' οὗ is decided on the English by rule 45's test, not on the Greek phrase: at 13:7 the ASV writes *these*, which answers to neither token, so the pair is one `noncompositional` unit; at 13:25 it writes *When*, which answers to the relative, so the same pair is one `aligned` unit. Both are headed on the governed word. This is rule 45's own ἐν ᾧ → *wherewith* against *while*, and a chapter writing both is following rule 24's habit.
- ἕως with a following relative compressed into a single English word is one `aligned` unit headed on ἕως, by rule 94's count test and rule 39's status test: ἕως ὅτου → *till* at 13:8 and ἕως οὗ → *till* at 13:21. A bare ἕως keeps its own word in the ordinary way, as ἕως → *until* does at 13:35.
- εἰ δὲ μήγε at 13:9 is split token by token — δέ → *but*, εἰ → *if*, μήγε → *not* — because the ASV wrote three English words. Rule 39's cluster is scoped to a rendering by one English word, which is what the corpus's *else* and *or else* units (LUK.5.36, LUK.5.37, JHN.14.11, REV.2.5) all carry; do not join the cluster mechanically from those precedents where the ASV has spelled it out.
- συκῆ → *fig tree* is headed on *tree* at 13:6 and 13:7, *tree* being what glosses the lexeme and *fig* the specifying modifier (rule 66's first arm). A head on *fig*, on the analogy of ἀγάπαις → *love feasts*, remains defensible and is what the 13:6 note names.
- A compound agent noun rendered by two words each glossing an element is headed by rule 66's morphological core and never by the English phrase's formal head: ἀρχισυνάγωγος → *ruler of the synagogue* heads on *synagogue* at 13:14, as πιθανολογίᾳ → *persuasiveness of speech* heads on *speech*, and οἰκοδεσπότης → *master of the house* heads on *master* at 13:25, δεσπότης being that compound's core. The two land on opposite English positions under one test. A head on *ruler* at 13:14 remains the competing reading and would rest on English syntax alone.
- Where the Greek verb has no expressed object and the ASV writes a single negative word carrying both the clause's negation and the object English requires, that word is the negator's own `aligned` unit and the verb stands on its own word: οὐχ εὗρεν → *found* and *none* at 13:6, οὐχ εὑρίσκω → *find* and *none* at 13:7. Rules 41 and 59 do not empty the negator here, no second negative token standing; the supplied pronominal rides inside the negator's unit as a rule 7 helper.
