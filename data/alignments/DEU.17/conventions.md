# Deuteronomy 17 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The idiom `al pi` is split in all four of its occurrences (17.6 twice, 17.10, 17.11) rather than fused under rule 30: the preposition takes whatever English the ASV writes for its own relation, whether *At*, *at*, or the compound *according to* headed on *according* under rule 24, and the construct noun takes *the mouth* or *the tenor* with the supplied *the* rule 15 gives it. Rule 30's one-rendering condition is never met here, because subtracting the preposition's word leaves the construct noun holding a plain English rendering of its own.
- In a construct chain whose rectum is a counted expression, the chain's *of* goes to the morpheme the construct actually governs, which the Hebrew word order decides and the English word order does not. `pi shenayim edim` -> *the mouth of two witnesses* makes the numeral the immediate rectum, so it takes *of two* (17.6 s003), while `pi ed echad` -> *the mouth of one witness* makes the noun the rectum, so it takes *of ... witness* discontinuous around the numeral (17.6 s015-s016). Rule 15's sentence giving a supplied determiner to the counted noun rather than to the numeral reaches supplied determiners only and does not move this *of*.
- A determiner outside rule 41's named list of *this*, *that*, *these*, *those*, a possessive, *any* and *every* is still the demonstrative pronoun's where it stands in the phrase's determiner slot and no other morpheme licenses it: `ha-toevah ha-zot` -> *such abomination* gives *such* to `zot` and leaves both the noun's article and the agreement article unrepresented (17.4 s017-s020), on the same terms as the eight phrases in this chapter whose written word is *this*, *that* or *those*.
- Where the ASV supplies an English nominal head for a bare prepositional phrase that contains no participle, adjective, clause or other substantive, no morpheme licenses it and it is `added`: *one* before *from among thy brethren* (17.15) and the italic *that which is* before *before the priests* (17.18). Rule 46 reaches a headless relative clause or a bare participle standing in a nominal slot, not a prepositional phrase, and the prepositions in both places already hold their own English (*from among*, *out of ... before*), so rule 52's count leaves them no room for a second word.
