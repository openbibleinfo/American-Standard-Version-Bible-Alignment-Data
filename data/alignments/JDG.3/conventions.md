# Judges 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where the ASV renders a Hebrew noun by a generic English noun with a specifier, this chapter heads on the specifier wherever that word carries the Hebrew lexeme, by rule 31's subtraction: aliyyah to 'upper room' and 'upper chamber' heads on 'upper' at 3.20, 3.23, 3.24 and 3.25, and har to 'the hill country' heads on 'hill' at 3.27 twice. 'room', 'chamber' and 'country' are the supplied generics and are never the head. JDG.3.20 was corrected from 'room' to 'upper' to bring it into line with the other three. The competing reading, that 'room' is a class noun the ASV writes alone for words of its class, was weighed and set aside: the ASV writes no bare 'room' or 'country' anywhere in this chapter.
- Where MACULA segments a compound proper name into two tokens, the ASV's own word count decides, on rule 30's one-rendering condition and not on anything peculiar to names. Cushan-rishathaim (3.8 twice, 3.10 twice) and Baal-hermon (3.3) are written in two English words, so each morpheme takes the word standing for it and the chain's relational 'of' goes to the first under rule 19; Aram-naharaim to 'Mesopotamia' (3.8, 3.10) is written in one, so the two morphemes share a unit headed on the opening morpheme under rule 23, with the 'of' inside that unit. Do not read a two-token name as automatically fused or automatically split, and do not let the edition's marginal note ('Hebrew Aram-naharaim' at 3.8, 'Hebrew Aram' at 3.10) move the analysis; it corroborates only, by rule 38.
- The object marker takes the preposition through which the English verb reaches its marked object wherever the ASV writes one, and is unrepresented otherwise: 'hearken unto' at 3.4, 'smote of' at 3.29 and 3.31, 'passed beyond' at 3.26, against 32 bare occurrences. The 'smote of' reading is settled for this chapter: the partitive look of the English does not make the word the object noun's, since Moab and the Philistines each keep their own rendering and nothing but the object relation goes when 'of' is dropped. 'passed beyond' is the widest application here, and the competing reading, taking 'beyond' as the verb's own particle and leaving the marker unrepresented, is recorded at 3.26; a later chapter meeting the same verb should read that note before following this one.
