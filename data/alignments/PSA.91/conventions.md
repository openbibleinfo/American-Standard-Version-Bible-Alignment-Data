# Psalms 91 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- **PSA.91.7** — Verbs of approaching rendered 'come nigh' (נָגַשׁ in v7, קָרַב in v10) keep 'nigh' inside the verb's unit as the complement carrying the lexeme, headed on 'nigh' under rule 44; the preposition on the goal (אֶל in v7, בְּ in v10) is its own unrepresented unit under rule 40, since the ASV writes no word for its relation apart from the verb's own rendering. The competing reading gives 'nigh' to the preposition; it was rejected in both verses so the chapter is uniform.
- **PSA.91.5** — The one לֹא of v5 scopes over both cola and takes both 'not' and the 'Nor' opening the second colon (rule 25 distributed negative; no waw stands there to fuse with under rule 35). v6 continues the same negation in English ('Nor for the destruction') but carries no negative and no conjunction of its own, so its 'Nor' is added under rules 10 and 14. The two verses are not a split: the difference is whether a negative morpheme stands in the verse.
- **PSA.91.14** — חָשַׁק → 'he hath set his love upon' is read as one light-verb rendering headed on 'love' (rule 44) with the 'his' inside it, because the possessive is what the English idiom requires and it realizes the verb's own subject rather than a possessor the Hebrew leaves unwritten. This is distinguished from v12's 'their hands', where the possessive stands on a written noun (כַּפַּיִם) with no suffix and is added under rule 18's closing sentence. The added reading for v14's 'his' is named in the unit's note.
