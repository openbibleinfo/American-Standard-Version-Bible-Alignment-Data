# Matthew 16 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The two verbless weather clauses are analysed alike (rule 24): at 16:2 Eudia and at 16:3 cheimon each carry the whole supplied 'It will be' frame under rule 68, no subject being expressed, and each heads on 'weather' rather than on 'fair' or 'foul' - the noun glossing the lexeme for cheimon, and the compound's core under rule 66 for eudia.
- At 16:3 the supplied 'and' of 'the heaven is red and lowering' is folded onto stugnazon, the participle it introduces. Rule 88's 'never on the participle it follows' addresses the reverse word order, and its principle - an obligatory juncture is a helper on what it introduces - puts the word here. Marking it added under rules 5 and 98 remains the competing reading; rule 98 is scoped to an asyndetic seam between two like constituents both rendered alike, and a finite verb with a circumstantial participle converted to a coordinate predicate is not that seam. Every other juncture in the chapter (16:1, 16:2, 16:4, 16:5, 16:16, 16:17, 16:22, 16:23) sits on a finite verb, as rule 88's majority requires.
- The chapter's only two added units are repeated lexical verbs whose licensing token is already spent - 'discern' at 16:3 for diakrinein and 'say' at 16:14 for eipan - marked added under rule 103 rather than folded under rule 7 or stretched under rule 15, each conceding the rule 15 reading in its note. Every supplied word that is a pronoun or an auxiliary is folded onto the token that licenses it instead (16:1, 16:8, 16:12, 16:17, 16:23, 16:24). The test applied throughout is rule 103's own - rule 7's list, not recurrence.
