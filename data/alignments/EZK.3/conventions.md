# Ezekiel 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- בֵּית מְרִי as a predicate (3.9, 3.26, 3.27): the construct בַּיִת takes the construction-supplied copula *are* together with the indefinite article of its phrase (rules 20, 15), מְרִי takes the attributive *rebellious* (rule 28), and the explicit הֵמָּה takes only *they* (rule 20). Where an explicit subject noun stands in the clause instead, as at 3.7 (*all the house of Israel are of a hard forehead*), the resumptive הֵמָּה is unrepresented under rule 22 and the copula still goes to the predicate.
- הַמְּגִלָּה הַזֹּאת (3.1, 3.2, 3.3): the ASV writes exactly one determiner for the phrase. Where that word is *this*, the demonstrative pronoun licenses it and both articles go unrepresented (3.1, 3.3); where it is *the*, the first article takes it and the demonstrative goes unrepresented (3.2). The agreement article standing on the demonstrative is never the morpheme that takes a written determiner.
- The זָהַר family (3.17-3.21): where the ASV writes a light verb plus complement — *give warning*, *givest warning*, *hast given warning*, *took warning* — the whole rendering is that verb's one unit headed on *warning* (rule 44), discontinuous around an object pronoun, which belongs to the object suffix under rule 18. Where the ASV writes a single *warn*, the verb takes that word and nothing more.
- The object marker אֵת is unrepresented on every bare object in this chapter (rule 16) and takes English only where the ASV's verb of speaking reaches its object through *with*: 3.22 *talk with thee*, 3.24 *spake with me*, 3.27 *speak with thee* (rule 16, first family). The אֵת of 3.3 is not the marker at all: MACULA tags it R, so it is the homograph preposition H854 aligning as an ordinary preposition. Read the morphology, not the '(et)' gloss.
- אָבָה before לְ + infinitive (3.7, twice): the ASV's *will not hearken* puts *will* on אָבָה — the finite verb in the first clause, the participle under אֵין in the second — and the lexical verb on the infinitive, while the לְ is unrepresented for want of a written complementizer (rules 40, 52). Do not read *will* as the infinitive's own auxiliary under rule 17; the verb of willing is what it renders, as rule 35 reads a modal onto יָכֹל where that verb stands in the clause.
