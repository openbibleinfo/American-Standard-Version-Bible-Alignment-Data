# Acts 22 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where the ASV compresses an appositional ἀνήρ into the single English word rendering the noun standing with it, ἀνήρ is its own `unrepresented` unit under rules 23 and 39's test: Ἄνδρες ἀδελφοί → *Brethren* at 22:1 and ἀνὴρ Ἰουδαῖος → *a Jew* at 22:3 give the whole word to ἀδελφοί and to Ἰουδαῖος. Where the ASV writes *man* for it the ordinary alignment holds, as ἀνὴρ εὐλαβής → *a devout man* does at 22:12 with the supplied determiner on the noun under rule 58. Joining the two tokens on the single word remains defensible and is named in each unit's note; a reference comparison reports the empty ἀνήρ as a disagreement.
- A periphrastic εἰμί is decided by the English the ASV wrote. Where the ASV keeps the periphrasis, the copula or auxiliary is the verb's own `aligned` unit — ἤμην ἐφεστώς → *was standing by* at 22:20, ἦν δεδεκώς → *he had bound* at 22:29, where *had* is the only word answering to it. Where the ASV collapses the periphrasis into a simple past, nothing is left for the copula and it is its own `unrepresented` unit: ἤμην φυλακίζων καὶ δέρων → *I imprisoned and beat* at 22:19, the copula there governing two participles at once so that no one of them can carry it. Folding it onto the first participle remains defensible and is named in that unit's note; a reference comparison reports the empty ἤμην as a disagreement.
