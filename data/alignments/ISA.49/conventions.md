# Isaiah 49 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A supplied English preposition by which the verb reaches a bare Hebrew object joins the verb's unit: made mention of at 49.1, clothe ... with them all and gird ... with them at 49.18, hath mercy on at 49.10, have compassion upon and on at 49.13 and 49.15, wait for at 49.23, contendeth with at 49.25. A supplied preposition licensed instead by an adverbial accusative's own case-function stays with its noun: with ... faces and to the earth at 49.23, with ... blood at 49.26, into singing at 49.13. Rule 44's closing limit separates them; this log records which nominals the chapter read each way, since the English looks identical.
- The comparative preposition before the second member of an instrumental comparison takes the whole as with, headed on as, and the compared noun keeps only its own English: ka-adi to as with an ornament at 49.18 and ke-asis to as with sweet wine at 49.26. Subtracting with leaves both morphemes their own rendering, so it is not a word the noun owns.
- A possessive lamed with a suffix that the ASV renders by a bare English possessive leaves the preposition unrepresented and gives the possessive to the suffix: lo to his at 49.5 and li to my at 49.6. The competing reading, fusing the pair under rule 49's genitive sentence as it fuses la-YHWH to Jehovah's, was not taken, because the suffix holds a possessive pronoun of its own and the fusion sentence governs a preposition holding none.
- In the disjunctive question at 49.24 the single English or goes to im, which is what marks the second question, and the waw is unrepresented. This is not read as rule 35 fusion, though the waw plus lo pairs at 49.10 are: im alone carries the disjunction the ASV wrote, whereas lo alone does not carry the coordination of nor, so there one word answers to two morphemes and here it does not.
