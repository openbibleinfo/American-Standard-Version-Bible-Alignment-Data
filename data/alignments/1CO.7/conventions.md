# 1 Corinthians 7 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A substantival article's complement decides where a supplied relativizer sits, and the ASV's identical *he that is X* frame across 7:32-34 is not evidence that the three phrases are one construction. Before a participle the article takes only its pronoun and the participle carries *that* with any copula: `ὁ κληθεὶς` → *he* / *that was called* (7:22), `ὁ γαμήσας` → *he* / *that is married* (7:33), `ἡ γαμήσασα` (7:34), `ὁ γαμίζων` (7:38), and the three members of 7:29-31, all under rule 32. Before an adjective the article keeps the pronoun and the relativizer together and the adjective takes only the copula and its own word: `ὁ ἄγαμος` → *He that* / *is unmarried* (7:32) and `τὸ εὔσχημον` → *that which* / *is seemly* (7:35), rule 22 being unnarrowed there, since rule 32 narrows it for a participle and rule 92 for an expressed noun and neither reaches an adjective. Where a head noun is expressed the second article is `unrepresented` and the predicate word carries relativizer and copula under rule 31: `ἡ γυνὴ ἡ ἄγαμος` → *the woman that is unmarried* (7:34).
- `ἴδιος` and a reflexive `ἑαυτοῦ` both empty the article they follow, under rules 47 and 20, but they head differently and the chapter keeps that difference throughout. `ἴδιος` heads on *own*, the word glossing the lexeme: `τὸν ἴδιον` → *her own* (7:2), `τοῦ ἰδίου` → *her own* and *his own* (7:4), `τοῦ ἰδίου` and `τῇ ἰδίᾳ` → *his own* (7:37). `ἑαυτοῦ` heads on *his*, the word realizing the pronoun, with *own* its emphatic helper: `τὴν ἑαυτοῦ` → *his own* (7:2, 7:37, 7:38).
- Rule 67 leaves the head of a complex prepositional frame open where the carrier is an expressed proper preposition, and this chapter takes it on the prepositional word throughout: `κατὰ συγγνώμην` → *by way of* headed on *by* (7:6), `διὰ τὴν ἐνεστῶσαν ἀνάγκην` → *by reason of* headed on *by* (7:26), and `περὶ τοῦ ἰδίου θελήματος` → *as touching* headed on *touching* (7:37). The frame's-noun reading stands in the corpus and each unit's note says so.
- `μεριμνάω` rendered *is careful for* keeps the governing *for* inside the verb's own unit at 7:32, 7:33 and 7:34, no Greek preposition being expressed there. Rule 36 moves such a preposition onto a source token only where the Greek writes one, as `τὰ περὶ ὑμῶν … μεριμνήσει` → *will care truly for your state* does.
- An asterisked ASV supply is folded onto the source token whose English expression it completes, and is not `added` merely for being italicized (rule 11): *cases* on `τοιούτοις` (7:15), *it* on `χρῆσαι` (7:21), *namely* on `ὅτι` (7:26), *So* on the adjunctive `καί` (7:34), and *daughter* on `παρθένον` (7:36, 7:37, 7:38), each headed on the word glossing the lexeme rather than on the supply. The chapter's one `added` unit is the supplied *yea* at 7:10, where `οὐκ`, `ἐγώ` and `ἀλλά` have each already spent their own English and no token licenses it (rule 86).
- `οὕτως` keeps whatever frame the ASV builds around it, headed on the word carrying the adverb's own deictic or comparative sense and never on the frame's supplied noun or verb: *after this manner* headed on *this* and the elliptical *after that* on *that* (7:7), *as he is* and *as she is* headed on *as* (7:26 and 7:40, the ASV's own gloss at 7:26 reading *Greek so to be*), and a bare *so* where the ASV writes one word (7:17, 7:36).
