# Psalms 132 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The chapter's three lə-X lə-Y possessive phrases — לְכִסֵּא־לָךְ (v11, v12) and לְמוֹשָׁב לוֹ (v13) — are read alike: the first לְ takes the preposition the ASV writes (upon, for), the second לְ is its own unrepresented unit under rule 40 because the ASV recasts it as a possessive determiner on the noun standing beside it, and that suffix takes the determiner (thy, his). BSB's contrary treatment at v12 is the documented convention difference and not an error.
- The supplied with of clothe X with Y (v9, v16, v18) and satisfy X with Y (v15) renders a Hebrew double accusative: the second noun is the verb's own object, so the preposition the English verb needs to reach it joins the verb's unit under rule 44 and the noun keeps only its own word. אֱמֶת in v11 is the counter-case: an adverbial accusative of manner, whose supplied in is the noun's under rule 33. Run the direct-object question first; the availability of another English preposition decides nothing here.
- The only added units in the chapter are the And of v2 and the Or of v4, each a coordinator the ASV writes at the head of a colon the Hebrew juxtaposes with no conjunction in it at all (rule 14). Every other connective answers to a written morpheme, including the postposed also of v16 (rule 51) and the Nor of v3, which is the second oath אִם. That אִם of vv. 3–4 is the negative oath particle throughout and takes whatever negative the ASV writes for it — Surely ... not (v3, discontinuous under rule 25), Nor (v3), not (v4); it is never unrepresented and the negation is never handed to the verb.
- Both construct chains fused into a single English word under rule 30 are headed by rule 23, but on opposite sides, and the test is which lexemes the English word writes. הֲדֹם רַגְלָיו → footstool (v7) is a compound writing both lexemes, so no morpheme is uniquely defensible and the head goes to the governing morpheme, the construct הֲדֹם. עֶרֶשׂ יְצוּעָי → bed (v3) writes one lexeme, and the ASV's own literal margin distributes couch to עֶרֶשׂ and bed to יְצוּעַי, corroborating without deciding under rule 38, so the head goes to יָצוּעַ. The competing head on the construct עֶרֶשׂ remains defensible, both lemmas being ordinary ASV bed words; it is recorded here because the v3 note names only the competing unrepresented reading.
- יְרַנֵּנוּ is rendered shout for joy in both v9 and v16 and heads on shout as the lexical predicate; the competing head on joy under rule 44's light-verb clause is named in both notes. The two verses are the chapter's own subtraction test for rule 56: they render the same verb identically and differ only by the aloud of v16, which is what the infinitive absolute רַנֵּן standing there is rendered by, as בָּרֵךְ takes abundantly in v15.
