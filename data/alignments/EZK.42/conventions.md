# Ezekiel 42 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A doubled סָבִיב סָבִיב that the ASV renders once by the compound adverb *round about* takes both English words on the first occurrence, and the second occurrence is `unrepresented` under rule 22. So EZK.42.15 and EZK.42.20, which agree. Rule 22 says which occurrence is the rendered one only for a counted noun distributed across a numeral, so it does not settle this; the compound adverb is one rendering of one adverb and is not divided across the two tokens, which is the competing reading each unit's note names.
- In a verbless clause opening with a locative prepositional phrase, read whether the clause predicates a location of a definite subject or asserts existence, and place the construction-supplied copula accordingly. EZK.42.9, whose subject הַמֵּבִיא is articular and for which the ASV writes a bare *was*, is a locative predication, so the copula joins the fronted preposition under rule 49. EZK.42.10, whose subject לְשָׁכוֹת is anarthrous and for which the ASV writes the presentative *there were*, is existential, so the whole frame joins the nominal anchor under rule 20. Rule 20 allows the frame on either a nominal anchor or a prepositional predicate and gives no test for choosing, so this is the chapter's; EZK.40.44 (`לִשְׁכוֹת` → *were chambers* after a fronted מִחוּצָה phrase) reads an indefinite subject the same way.
- Where coordinated comparanda each carry their own מִן and the ASV writes the comparison once — מֵהַתַּחְתֹּנוֹת וּמֵהַתִּיכֹנוֹת → *more than from the lower and the middlemost* — the מִן standing on the phrase the English *more than* or *from* introduces takes that English and the later one is `unrepresented` under rule 22, exactly as rule 47 reads which word a single written *the* stands before. So EZK.42.5 (three מִן, two renderings) and EZK.42.6 (three מִן, two renderings). This is what the two BSB מֵ disagreements are; BSB attaches each preposition to its own phrase by documented convention, and neither is an error.
