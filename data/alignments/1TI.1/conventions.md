# 1 Timothy 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Indefinite τις expanded with a generic English noun heads on the word the ASV chose to render the lexeme, not on the noun: 'certain men' (1:3) heads on certain and 'any … thing' (1:10) on any, under rule 17's generic-noun clause as rule 69 applies it. Where the ASV supplies only the default article, that article is a rule 7/51 helper on the word it determines and cannot be the head, so 'a man' (1:8) heads on man. Heading 1:8 on 'a' is the competing reading; corpus τις heads 'any man' on 'any' in 35 units and shows no head on 'man' in the rows supplied, but no τις → 'a man' row was supplied either.
- One Greek word rendered by an English phrase heads on the word glossing the Greek base morpheme, the prefix or first element riding along inside the unit: ματαιολογίαν → 'vain talking' (talking, 1:6), νομοδιδάσκαλοι → 'teachers of the law' (teachers, 1:7), πατρολῴαις and μητρολῴαις → 'murderers of fathers/mothers' (murderers, 1:9), ἐπιόρκοις → 'false swearers' (swearers, 1:10), ἀρσενοκοίταις → 'abusers of themselves with men' (abusers, 1:10), ἑτεροδιδασκαλεῖν → 'to teach a different doctrine' (teach, 1:3), ὑπερεπλεόνασεν → 'abounded exceedingly' (abounded, 1:14). Where the English verb is instead an empty support verb, rule 91's test puts the head on the complement: προσέχειν → 'give heed' (heed, 1:4), ἠλεήθην → 'obtained mercy' (mercy, 1:13 and 1:16), ἐναυάγησαν → 'made shipwreck' (shipwreck, 1:19). The two arms are the same test and never cross: 'teach' glosses διδασκαλ-, 'made' and 'obtained' gloss nothing.
- μή standing before the correlative μήτε … μήτε is unrepresented (1:7). The ASV writes the negation once, in 'neither', and rule 59's licensing condition — a following word that is itself a negative able to carry the whole English negation alone — is met by a negative conjunction as well as by a negative indefinite, rule 59 already naming a preceding οὐδέ emptying a following μή. Giving 'neither' to μή and emptying the first μήτε was the competing reading and was rejected because μήτε is the coordinating pair. A reference comparison reports this token as represented, and that report is not a correction.
