# Deuteronomy 12 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Resumption in relative clauses. Where the ASV writes a single pronominal adverb for the relative and its resumptive, they share one unit headed on the relative: asher + sham -> wherein (12.2), asher + sham + directive he -> whither (12.29, three morphemes, one word). Where the clause carries a further resumptive the ASV does not write - b-o at 12.11 and 12.18, where sham has already taken the there, and otam at 12.2, where the antecedent is the object - that morpheme is its own unrepresented unit under rule 22's first sentence. Both shapes occur in this chapter and the BSB disagreements at 12.11, 12.18 are this convention, not errors.
- pen is rendered by the discontinuous that ... not (12.13, 12.19, 12.30 twice) and kept in one unit headed on not, since the negation is what distinguishes the pair from a bare complementizer, as rule 35 heads a Nor on its negative. Heading on that is the competing reading and is named in each verse note; the chapter was not checked against corpus precedent for this lemma.
- mishlach yad recast as a relative clause (12.7, 12.18). The whole recast frame - that ye put ... unto, that thou puttest ... unto, including the supplied subject pronoun and the stranded unto - belongs to mishlach and heads on the lexical verb, discontinuous under rule 25; yad keeps hand and its suffix the possessive. Nothing else in the clause answers for the unto.
- kol with a relative is fused only where the ASV spends one word on the pair: 12.32's What ... soever is one unit on kol plus asher, with davar keeping thing and its article unrepresented because the determination is inside that word. Where the ASV writes a word for each, rule 2 splits them and kol keeps whatever generic the ASV supplies to complete it: 12.8's all the things goes to kol, headed on all under rule 31, and that goes to asher.
- Comparative particles are counted against the morphemes present. 12.15 writes ke twice and each takes its own as of, with the assimilated article taking the written the; 12.16 and 12.24 write no the, so the assimilated article is unrepresented. 12.22 writes kaasher once and the ASV rewrites as over the coordinated member, so both occurrences belong to that one morpheme's discontinuous unit under rule 25 and the second is not added.
- Rule 31 heads in this chapter's offering and servant vocabulary. olah, terumah and nedavah head on the modifier (burnt, heave, freewill), as do pasil (graven images) and qodesh (holy things), because the ASV never writes the generic noun alone for those words. eved, amah and tirosh head on the class noun (servant, servant, wine), because the ASV writes that noun alone for words of the same class, which is rule 31's tie-break rather than a different convention.
