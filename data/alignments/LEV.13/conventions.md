# Leviticus 13 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The ASV supplies a conditional *if* inside the וְהִנֵּה protasis of the examination formula — *and, behold,* ***if*** *the plague be spread* — where the Hebrew writes no conjunction at all. That *if* is its own `added` unit in all sixteen places (13.3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 13, 17, 20, 25, 30, 32, 34, 36, 39, 43, 55) and is never folded into the waw that opens the clause, into הִנֵּה, or into the verb. Where the clause does write אִם (13.21, 22, 23, 26, 27, 28, 35, 37, 53, 56, 57), כִּי (13.16, 31, 40, 42, 51) or a waw (13.49, 58), that morpheme takes the *if* and nothing is added. The italicized *yet* of 13.40 and 13.41 and the surplus *whether* of 13.55 are the same case.
- רָאָה + אֵת and רָאָה + verbal suffix are aligned differently in this chapter, and the source licenses the difference. Where the object marker stands, it takes the *on* or *upon* by which *look* reaches its object under rule 16 (13.3, 6, 15, 25, 30, 31, 32, 34, 43, 50, 51, and the *unto* of 13.49). Where the object is instead a pronominal suffix on the verb itself and no marker is present, the particle stays inside the verb's unit and the suffix takes only the pronoun (13.5, 17, 21, 26, 27, 36). Every other אֵת in the chapter is `unrepresented`, the ASV writing its object bare.
- The metonymic הַנֶּגַע, 'the plagued person', is rendered *him that hath the plague* (once *that which*, 13.50). Where an article morpheme stands on the noun, the article takes *him that* / *that which* and heads on the pronoun under rule 41, while the noun keeps *hath the plague* (13.4, 12, 13, 17, 33, 50). At 13.31 the same metonym is the anarthrous construct of נֶגַע הַנָּתֶק — the phrase's only article stands on הַנָּתֶק — so no article is present to take that English and the whole supplied *him that hath the* joins the substantive's own unit under rule 46, headed on *plague*. Read which word the article morpheme sits on before treating the two as a split.
- The intensifying infinitive absolute takes the adverb the ASV writes for it and is not folded into the finite verb: פָּשֹׂה תִפְשֶׂה → *spread*(finite) + *abroad*(infinitive absolute) at 13.7, 22, 27 and 35, פָּרוֹחַ תִּפְרַח the same at 13.12, and טַמֵּא יְטַמְּאֶנּוּ → *surely* at 13.44. *Abroad* is read as the infinitive absolute's own rendering rather than as a phrasal particle of *spread* under rule 44, because the ASV writes it only where the infinitive absolute stands: פָּשָׂה alone is *be spread* throughout (13.5, 6, 8, 20, 23, 28, 32, 34, 36, 53, 55).
- מִחְיָה is the ASV's *quick* and חַי its *raw* throughout the chapter: בָּשָׂר חַי → *raw flesh* (13.14, 13.15 twice, 13.16), מִחְיַת הַמִּכְוָה → *the quick flesh** with *flesh* italicized (13.24), and מִחְיַת בָּשָׂר חַי → *quick*(מִחְיָה) *raw*(חַי) *flesh*(בָּשָׂר) at 13.10, corrected by this reconciliation pass from an earlier reading that had the two reversed.
