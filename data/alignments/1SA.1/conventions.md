# 1 Samuel 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The phrase 'be-eynei' plus suffix is read two ways in this chapter, and the difference is the ASV's word count rather than a split. At 1SA.1.18 the ASV writes 'in thy sight' - a word for the preposition, a word for the noun, a word for the suffix - so rule 30's one-rendering condition is unmet and rule 2 splits all three. At 1SA.1.23 the ASV writes 'what seemeth thee good', spending one English word on the preposition and its construct noun together, so those two share one unit headed on the verb, on the model of rule 40's 'im-ra be-eyneykha' to 'if it displease thee'. The predicate adjective tov is not drawn into that fusion, because it holds its own 'good' and rule 30's subtraction test finds a word spent on it in particular; the suffix keeps the 'thee' rule 18 writes for it. The competing reading, which takes 'seemeth' as rule 20's linking copula joining tov and marks both preposition and noun unrepresented, is named in the 1SA.1.23 unit note.
- **1SA.1.14** — 'ad matay' rendered 'How long' with no matrix frame written is split morpheme by morpheme, as rule 17's closing sentence directs: the temporal interrogative matay takes 'How', which writes its wh-lexeme, and 'ad takes 'long', which is the extent the preposition states. This is not rule 17's indivisible 'how long will it be ere' unit, which that rule reserves for a clause carrying a separate English matrix frame; here the ASV writes only the question itself, so nothing is fused.
- **1SA.1.28** — 'kol ha-yamim asher hayah' rendered 'as long as he liveth' divides as follows: kol, the article and yamim share the aligned temporal unit 'as long', headed on yamim and long; asher retains the second 'as', and hayah retains 'he liveth'. Both independent scoped judgments include the article under current rule 30 because the temporal rendering retains the definite duration specified by the following life-clause. This supersedes only this occurrence's former article exclusion and its distinction between aligned and noncompositional article inclusion; the original convention is preserved in the deferred-investigation audit.
