# Matthew 15 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where the ASV writes a single determiner for a coordinated list of anarthrous substantival adjectives, the determiner is folded onto the first member and the remaining members stand bare: 15.30's 'the lame, blind, dumb, maimed' gives 'the' to cholous and leaves typhlous, kophous and kyllous their own bare words. Where the ASV instead writes a determiner before each member it is folded onto the member it determines, as 15.31's four 'the's and 15.14's two are. Rule 51's anarthrous clause decides both; the count of English determiners the ASV wrote is what distributes them.
- For a pros-compound verb of motion or calling, the English 'to him' or 'unto him' goes to a dative pronoun where one stands in the Greek and rides inside the verb's own unit where none does. 15.30 prosēlthon autoi gives a bare 'there came' to the verb and 'unto him' to the pronoun under rule 106's oblique-case arm, while 15.10 and 15.32 proskalesamenos keep 'called to him' and 'called unto him' whole, no source token standing for the supplied pronoun. Read the source for a dative before assigning the phrase.
- 15.36's verse-opening 'and' is folded onto elaben as rule 88's juncture even though the circumstantial participle it converts, parangeilas, stands in 15.35. Rule 86 already names a conjunction or licensing token expressed elsewhere in the sentence, and the Greek sentence runs across the ASV verse boundary here; the juncture belongs to the finite verb it introduces, which is in this verse. Marking it 'added' under rule 5 would have read the verse boundary as a sentence boundary.
