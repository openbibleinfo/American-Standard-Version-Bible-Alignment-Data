# Joshua 16 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Boundary-itinerary toponyms: a place name or place noun standing in the itinerary with no preposition and no object marker on it takes the English *to*, *at* or *unto* the ASV writes before it into its own unit as a directional or locative accusative (rule 33), and that word is never `added`. This chapter reads it so seven times: `בֵּית־אֵל` → *to Beth el* (16.1), `עֲטָרוֹת` → *to Ataroth* (16.2, 16.7), `הַמִּכְמְתָת` → *at Michmethath* and `תַּאֲנַת שִׁלֹה` → *unto Taanath shiloh* (16.6), `הַיַּרְדֵּן` → *at the Jordan* (16.7), `נַחַל קָנָה` → *to the brook of Kanah* (16.8). Where a preposition or a construct relation is present it takes the word first, as `יַרְדֵּן יְרִיחוֹ` → *the Jordan at Jericho* does in 16.1 under rule 19.
- `בְּתוֹךְ` (16.9) and `בְּקֶרֶב` (16.10) are each rendered *in the midst of* and are split, the `בְּ` taking *in* and the construct noun taking *the midst* with its supplied *the*. Rule 30 lists `בְּקֶרֶב` → *among* among its fused examples, but what that list names is the one-word rendering and not the lemma: here the ASV spends a word on each morpheme, so the one-rendering condition is unmet and rule 2 splits the pair. Do not read the example list as fusing these prepositions wherever they occur.
