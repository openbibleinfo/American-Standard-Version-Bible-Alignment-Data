# 1 Corinthians 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Where an expressed Greek pronoun stands in apposition to an articular substantive and the ASV recasts the phrase as a finite relative clause, the pronoun's own English is the antecedent, so the article has no pronoun left to supply and is its own unrepresented unit; the complement carries the supplied relativizer, any copula and its own word. τοῖς δὲ σῳζομένοις ἡμῖν -> 'unto us who are saved' gives 'unto us' to ἡμῖν, nothing to the article and 'who are saved' to the participle at 1.18, exactly as αὐτοῖς δὲ τοῖς κλητοῖς -> 'unto them that are called' does at 1.24. The complement's class does not change it: a participle and a verbal adjective are treated alike here. The contrast within the chapter is an articular participle with no pronoun in apposition, where the article takes the pronoun in the ordinary way (1.18 s007, 1.21, 1.31) or the single relativizer under rule 90 (1.2).
- Rule 70's counting test, stated there for τε ... καί, is carried in this chapter to a correlative καί ... καί and to a three-member τε ... καί ... καί series, the number of English conjunctions the ASV wrote deciding it. At 1.22 the ASV writes one 'and' for two correlative καί, so the second takes it and the first is unrepresented; at 1.30 it writes three conjunctions for τε, καί and καί, so each particle keeps one; at 1.28 it writes three connectives for three καί, so each keeps one. Count the English conjunctions before asking which particle is which.
