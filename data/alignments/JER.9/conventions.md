# Jeremiah 9 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The formula ne'um-YHWH is aligned throughout as ne'um -> 'saith' and YHWH -> 'Jehovah' (9.3, 9.6, 9.9, 9.22, 9.24, 9.25). The ASV renders the construct noun by a finite verb, so it writes no 'of' and rule 19 has nothing to place on the divine name; the formula is not read as a construct chain in this chapter.
- mi yitten is one noncompositional optative unit -> 'Oh that' in both places it stands (9.1, 9.2), headed on mi as the deterministic display anchor under rule 6 and on 'Oh' as the lexical English anchor. Where the verb carries an object suffix (9.2 yittenani), the suffix is split off and takes the ASV's recast 'I had' under rule 43's third case, so the idiom's own rendering stays identical across the two verses instead of absorbing the possessive recasting in one of them.
- mibbeli is read three ways in this chapter and none of them is a split; each is what the ASV writes. 9.11 mibbeli yoshev -> 'without inhabitant' fuses the two morphemes into one English word under rule 30. 9.12 mibbeli 'over and 9.10 mibbeli ish 'over are recast as negative result clauses, so the preposition takes 'so that' under rule 48 and beli takes 'none'. In 9.10 alone, where ish is also present, beli and ish fuse into that single 'none' under rule 30, which is why 9.12's beli carries 'none' by itself.
- The italic simile frame 'as it were' at 9.3 (qashtam -> 'as it were their bow') is aligned to the bare accusative of comparison rather than marked added: rule 33 gives such a noun the function word its case-function licenses, and the frame's dummy 'it were' is construction-supplied on rule 20's terms. The ASV italics corroborate only (rule 7). This is the chapter's one italic that is not `added`; 'else' (9.7), 'who is' (9.12), 'and' (9.21), 'them' (9.22), 'their' (9.25) and 'of their hair' (9.26) all are, because no morpheme licenses them.
