# Psalms 149 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A volitive 'Let' the ASV writes for a Hebrew jussive or for a verbless clause with jussive force is never added in this chapter, and where it goes depends on whether a verb stands. In PSA.149.2, .3 and .5 a jussive verb stands and the frame joins that verb under rule 17, discontinuous around the subject under rule 25. In PSA.149.6 the clause is verbless with a prepositional predicate and no verb stands, so the whole supplied 'Let ... be' is the construction-supplied copula and joins the preposition under rule 49, headed on the preposition; the fronted construct nominal is the clause's subject and takes none of it, and the ASV's italics on 'Let' and 'be' corroborate the supply without deciding it, by rule 7.
- The chapter supplies a definite article twice inside one anarthrous construct chain. In PSA.149.1 'in the assembly of the saints' no article morpheme stands anywhere in the phrase: the construct noun takes the first supplied 'the' under rule 29 and the substantival adjective serving as rectum takes the second under rule 46, alongside the relational 'of' rule 19 gives it. The bare substantival 'the' stands alone in PSA.149.4 'the meek' and PSA.149.5 'the saints', and is supplied nowhere in PSA.149.9, where the written suffix's possessive already determines the phrase. Two supplied 'the's in one anarthrous chain are not evidence of a fronted article.
- Verbs of singing head on 'sing', not on the noun or adverbial beside it: zamar 'Let them sing praises' (PSA.149.3) and ranan 'Let them sing for joy' (PSA.149.5) both head on the verb, because rule 31's subtraction leaves 'praises' and 'for joy' naming no singing while 'sing' still writes the making of music and the vocal cry. Verbs whose English is a true light verb with a complement head the other way under rule 44 - gil 'be joyful' on 'joyful' (PSA.149.2), ratsah 'taketh pleasure' on 'pleasure' (PSA.149.4). Each verse note names the competing reading.
- PSA.149.7 bal is a divergence the pipeline has recorded no textual decision for. MACULA segments the word as the negative particle bal followed by ummim, while the ASV read balumim as a pointed preposition with its article on leom and wrote 'upon the'. The packet marks no variant unit in that slot, so rule 53's second paragraph governs: the English is aligned as printed to the token the projection carries, on the terms the construction would otherwise take, headed on the relational word under rule 24. Do not mark the token unrepresented and the English added, and do not take a new textual decision in the alignment file, which rule 9 forbids.
