# Mark 14 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Compound verb, no expressed Greek preposition: where the verb governs a bare dative or genitive and the ASV writes an English preposition governing that word, the preposition realizes the case government and rides with the governed word under rules 21, 36 and 69 — ἐμπτύειν αὐτῷ → 'spit' / 'on him' (14:65), ἐμβλέψασα αὐτῷ → 'looked' / 'upon him' (14:67), συνηκολούθει αὐτῷ → 'followed' / 'with him' (14:51), συναποθανεῖν σοι → 'die' / 'with thee' (14:31), καταμαρτυροῦσιν σου → 'witness' / 'against thee' (14:60), ἐνεβριμῶντο αὐτῇ → 'murmured' / 'against her' (14:5), ἐπέβαλαν … αὐτῷ → 'laid hands' / 'on him' (14:46), συνέρχονται αὐτῷ → 'come together' / 'with him' (14:53). The prefix keeps only an English particle that governs nothing: away, forth, out, up, off, together, forward, in, abroad. An accusative object is the contrast, since English needs no case preposition for a direct object, so the phrasal particle stays on the verb — κρατοῦσιν αὐτόν → 'lay hold on' / 'him' (14:51) and προάξω ὑμᾶς → 'go before' / 'you' (14:28). 14:51 writes both halves and 14:54's plain ἠκολούθησεν αὐτῷ → 'followed him' shows the ASV rendering the same dative bare, which is why 'with' at 14:51 is not read off the verb.
- A Greek predicate adjective with an expressed copula that the ASV collapses into a single English verb is one `aligned` unit, headed on that verb and on the adjective: ἴσαι ἦσαν → 'agreed together' (14:56) and ἴση ἦν → 'did agree together' (14:59), the inversion auxiliary 'did' riding inside the unit under rule 15. Rule 53 does not reach these, the copula being expressed rather than supplied; this is rule 73's collapse test applied to a copular clause. Splitting 'agree' onto the copula and 'together' onto the adjective remains defensible and both verses say so.
- The ASV's supplied appositional 'even' is `added` throughout this chapter, no expressed preposition introducing any of the three appositives: a substantival article plus participle at 14:18, an article before a name at 14:67, and a demonstrative phrase at 14:30. Rule 105 names the first two carriers; 14:30 falls under neither of its named carriers and was decided by the same reasoning — nothing is present for the word to answer to, since a case preposition rides with an article only because it realizes the article's own case and 'even' realizes nothing.
- ἐξ αὐτῶν with the ASV's italicized supplied 'one' — 'This is one of them' (14:69) and 'thou art one of them' (14:70) — puts the supplied nominal on the preposition and heads the unit on the prepositional word 'of', following rule 67's 2 Corinthians reading rather than its Ephesians/Philippians one. Both verses read alike, which is rule 24.
- A single Greek noun the ASV expands into an English phrase keeps that whole phrase, italics included, headed on the word glossing the lexeme: οἰκοδεσπότῃ → 'master of the house' (14:14), φῶς → 'light of the fire' (14:54), ῥαπίσμασιν → 'blows of their hands' (14:65), ἀλάβαστρον → 'alabaster cruse' (14:3), κατάλυμα → 'guest chamber' (14:14). Rule 103 does not reach these and they are not `added` under rule 11: no other source token has spent that English, so the expansion belongs to the noun that licenses it.
