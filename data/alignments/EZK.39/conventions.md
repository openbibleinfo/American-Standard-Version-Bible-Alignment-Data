# Ezekiel 39 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The suffix of 'adonay is unrepresented throughout the chapter (39.1, 5, 8, 10, 13, 17, 20, 25, 29): the ASV writes 'the Lord Jehovah' with no separate possessive, and the supplied 'the' goes to 'adonay under rule 15 while the suffix is left unrendered under rule 22. This is the chapter's largest BSB disagreement class (9 tokens) and is a convention difference, not an error.
- hayyom hahu' is rendered 'that day' (39.11, 39.22): the demonstrative hu' takes the written 'that' and every article in the phrase is unrepresented - the assimilated [ha] of ba and the attributive ha alike - because the ASV writes no 'the' for the phrase at all (rules 29, 41). BSB represents all three of those articles; that is convention, not error.
- 'avar is the ASV's 'pass through' throughout this chapter, including the four occurrences where no preposition stands (39.11 twice, 39.14, 39.15). The chapter therefore reads the 'through' of 'pass through the land' as the verb's own phrasal particle under rule 44 and marks the be of ba'arets unrepresented under rule 40 (39.14, 39.15). The competing reading gives 'through' to the be and leaves the verb 'pass'; it is what BSB's two disagreements here amount to, and it was rejected because it would render one verb two ways inside 39.15, where the same participle is written once with the preposition and once without. The judgment rests on chapter-wide evidence a verse pass cannot see, which is why it is recorded here rather than promoted.
- ba'ar is rendered by the light verb 'make fires'. Where the Hebrew writes 'esh (39.9b, 39.10) the noun takes 'fires' and the verb keeps only 'make'; where it does not (39.9a) the verb takes the whole phrase and heads on 'fires' under rule 44. Read the clause for a written 'esh before placing the noun: 39.9 contains both cases.
- 39.9's 'both the shields and the bucklers' renders two waws, not one. The waw standing where 'both' stands takes it and the waw between the two coordinated members takes 'and'. Rule 14's correlative clause hands a pair to a single waw only where one waw is what stands there; where the phrase writes a conjunction morpheme in each position, count them against the ASV's connectives as rule 42 directs.
- 39.14: the ASV reads the 'et before ha'ovrim as the preposition 'with' (H854) although MACULA tags it To, so the marker takes 'with' under rule 16's subtraction test, as the corpus already does at EZK.2.6. That clause writes no waw, so the 'and' the ASV puts before the phrase answers to no morpheme and is its own added unit under rule 14's last sentence rather than being folded into the marker.
