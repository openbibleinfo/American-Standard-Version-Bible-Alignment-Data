# Matthew 12 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Genitive of comparison: the supplied *than* is a case marker and is placed by rule 21, not kept with the comparative that licenses it. Where the ASV gives the compared noun a determiner, *than* rides with the article and the unit heads on *the* (12:6 `τοῦ ἱεροῦ` -> *than the* + *temple*; 12:45 `τῶν πρώτων` -> *than the* + *first*). Where it gives none, the marker stays whole on the noun or name (12:12 *than a sheep*, 12:41 *than Jonah*, 12:42 *than Solomon*, 12:45 *than himself*). The comparative itself keeps only its own English word, headed on the adjective against any supplied generic completion (12:6 *one greater*, 12:41 and 12:42 *a greater*).
- `σάββατον` rendered *sabbath day*: the supplied *day* completes the noun and is neither `added` nor the article's (12:1, 12:5, 12:10, 12:11, 12:12), and the article beside it keeps only *on the* under rule 21. Where the ASV writes a bare *sabbath* the noun stands alone (12:5 second occurrence, 12:8), and where the Greek is anarthrous the supplied determiner is an ordinary helper on the noun (12:2 *the sabbath*).
- An English verbal particle with no Greek preposition expressed for it belongs to the English verb: *out* (12:11, 12:14, 12:24, 12:26, 12:27, 12:28, 12:43, 12:44), *forth* (12:13 twice, 12:20, 12:35 twice, 12:49), *up* (12:41, 12:42), *in* (12:45), *after* (12:39), *aloud* (12:19), and the *on* of *lay hold on* (12:11), which is the light verb's own particle and not the object pronoun's case preposition. The contrast is an expressed `ἐκ` rendered by the two-word *out of*, which takes both words as one unit headed on *out* (12:34, 12:35).
- Supplied possessives: where a genitive pronoun stands it takes every English possessive the ASV writes for the phrase it governs, even two from one token, and the articles are `unrepresented` under rule 20 — 12:46 `ἡ μήτηρ καὶ οἱ ἀδελφοὶ αὐτοῦ` -> *his mother and his brethren* gives both *his* to the single `αὐτοῦ` discontinuously under rule 15, one word's English split across a phrase still being that word's (rule 76). Where no pronoun stands at all, the article takes the supplied possessive under rule 20's second arm (12:33 `τοῦ καρποῦ` -> *its*; 12:35 `τοῦ … θησαυροῦ` -> *his*, twice), and an expressed `ἐκ` keeps its own English rather than riding with that article.
- Interrogative `εἰ` introducing a direct question is decided by rule 64's English-side test like any other question particle: 12:10 `Εἰ ἔξεστι …;` -> *Is it lawful …?* leaves it `unrepresented`, the ASV marking the question by inversion alone and *Is* being the copula the clause required anyway, while 12:23 `Μήτι` -> *Can* is aligned because the ASV supplies an interrogative word. Rule 64's list of lemmas is not exhaustive; the particle's work decides.
