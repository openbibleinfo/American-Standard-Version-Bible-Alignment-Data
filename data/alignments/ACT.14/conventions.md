# Acts 14 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Pronoun arguments of an infinitive clause: an expressed accusative subject takes the English subject word of the clause the ASV writes for it — αὐτούς → they at 14.1, ἡμᾶς → we at 14.22, αὐτόν → he at 14.19 — and an English object the ASV supplies for a Greek verb whose own object is unexpressed is a rule 7 helper on that verb, not a second realization of a nearby pronoun: of it on συνιδόντες at 14.6 and on ἀκούσαντες at 14.14, them on παρακαλοῦντες at 14.22, him on ἔσυρον at 14.19. Where the Greek pronoun really is the argument of two coordinate verbs and the ASV writes it twice, the repetition stays with that pronoun as rule 103's resumptive pronoun, as αὐτούς → them … them does at 14.5.
- Two-element verb lexemes are headed by asking whether the English verb word glosses a root of the Greek. Where it does, the head is the verbal core: ἀγαθουργῶν → he did good on did, παρρησιαζόμενοι → speaking boldly on speaking, and the noun compounds περίχωρον → region round about on region and ὁμοιοπαθεῖς → of like passions on passions. Where the English verb word glosses no root and is only the verbalizing support word of a denominal, the head is the noun: εὐαγγελίζω → preached the gospel at 14.7 and 14.21 and → bring good tidings at 14.15, both on the noun, and μαθητεύσαντες → had made disciples at 14.21 on disciples. The ASV's own gloss note at 14.21, brought the good tidings, corroborates the εὐαγγελίζω reading.
- The chapter's one added unit is the italicized locative there of 14.3, which διατρίβω does not carry and no other token licenses. It is not a precedent against the folded there of 14.8 and 14.19, which is the expletive subject of an English existential riding with its verb under rule 96, nor against the thither of 14.19, which realizes the preverb ἐπ- under rule 106. Folding 14.3's there onto διέτριψαν remains defensible and is named in that unit's note.
