# Luke 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Versification: the ASV translates LUK.6.18's οἳ ἦλθον ἀκοῦσαι αὐτοῦ καὶ ἰαθῆναι ἀπὸ τῶν νόσων αὐτῶν at the end of LUK.6.17. Under rule 37 the ten tokens are their own `unrepresented` units in 6.18 and the English is one `added` unit in 6.17, each side naming the other. A reference comparison reports all ten as disagreements; that report is not a correction.
- ἀγαθοποιέω and κακοποιέω rendered by *do* plus a complement are one `aligned` unit headed on the complement and not on *do*: *to do good* and *to do harm* at 6.9, *ye do good* and *that do good* at 6.33, *do them good* at 6.35. Rule 17's meaning-bearing-complement clause decides it, and 3JN.1.11 (*that doeth good* → *good*, *that doeth evil* → *evil*) is the precedent; the corpus units heading on the verb word all render the lexeme *do well* or *well doing*, where the second English word is an adverb. Where both Greek words are expressed the pair splits like any other, as καλῶς ποιεῖτε → *do good* does at 6.27.
- κατὰ τὰ αὐτά → *in the same manner* at 6.23 and 6.26 is analysed compositionally and not as a rule 67 supplied frame: κατά takes *in*, the article takes *the … manner* headed on *manner* under rule 102, and αὐτά takes *same*. Rule 45's closing clause licenses this, the ASV having spread the phrase over words that each answer to a token.
- Where the ASV supplies a nominal inside an article's own phrase, the article takes it under rule 102 and the unit heads on the nominal; it is not `added` under rule 103, because the token licensing it is the article rather than a spent noun. So τὰ σά → *thy goods* gives *goods* to the article and *thy* to the possessive adjective at 6.30, and 6.45's ὁ πονηρός → *the evil man* and τοῦ πονηροῦ → *the evil treasure* are analysed alike, each headed on the supplied noun. 6.34's τὰ ἴσα → *as much* is the contrast and leaves the article `unrepresented`, neither *as* nor *much* being a determiner or a nominal it could take.
- An English verb particle realizing a Greek preverb rides inside the verb's own unit and never stands alone: *stand forth* (6.8 στῆθι), *came forth* (6.19 ἐξήρχετο), *ask … again* (6.30 ἀπαίτει), *receive again* (6.34 ἀπολάβωσιν), *measured … again* (6.38 ἀντιμετρηθήσεται), *bringeth forth* (6.45 προφέρει twice), *fell in* (6.49 συνέπεσεν). The head stays on the verb word, as rule 104's ἐξέρχεται → *cometh forth* has it.
- A substantival article in apposition to an expressed dative pronoun the ASV has already realized: where the ASV writes a second pronoun word the article takes it and the participle keeps *that* with its verb (6.25 ὑμῖν οἱ ἐμπεπλησμένοι → *unto you ye that are full*); where it writes only the relativizer, the article takes that word and the participle or adjective keeps its own (6.24 τοῖς πλουσίοις → *that* plus *are rich*, 6.27 τοῖς ἀκούουσιν → *that* plus *hear*). The ASV's word count decides it, which is rule 90's own test.
- οὐαί: where a dative pronoun is expressed it takes the supplied *unto you* (6.24, and 6.25 on the selected reading r01); where none is expressed the supplied *unto you* is a rule 7 helper on the interjection itself (6.26). It is not `added` in either case.
- 6.4 τοὺς ἄρτους τῆς προθέσεως → *the showbread*: the single English compound spells both lexemes (*-bread* for ἄρτους, *show-* for προθέσεως), so the two nouns are one `aligned` unit under rule 35's test, headed on the noun the English head element answers to, while the outer article stands alone and takes *the*. The intervening τῆς contributes no part of the compound and is its own `unrepresented` unit, which is rule 93's reasoning. Giving *showbread* to ἄρτους alone and leaving προθέσεως empty remains defensible; the unit note records it.
