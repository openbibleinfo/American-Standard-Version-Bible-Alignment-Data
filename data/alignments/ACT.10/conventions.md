# Acts 10 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Supplied determiners and supplied generic nominals: the presence of a Greek article decides who takes them, and ACT.10 applies rule 102's test to two shapes. Where an article governs a substantival adjective or ordinal, the article takes the determiner and the supplied noun and the modifier stands bare — τὴν ἐνάτην to 'the … hour' (10:30), τὰ τετράποδα to 'beasts' (10:12, corrected from unrepresented). Where the Greek is anarthrous the modifier keeps them — ὥραν … ἐνάτην (10:3) and ὥραν … ἕκτην (10:9) under rule 58, ἑρπετά to 'creeping things' (10:12) and δευτέρου to 'the second time' (10:15) and εὐσεβής to 'a devout man' (10:2) and ἀθέμιτον to 'an unlawful thing' (10:28) under rules 17 and 104. 10:12 writes both in one verse, so the identical English 'the ninth hour' at 10:3 and 10:30 is one analysis and not two.
- Supplied English lexical content is decided by asking whether some source token's own lexeme carries it, not merely whether a same-lexeme English word stands elsewhere in the verse. 10:22 folds the italicized 'of God' into ἐχρηματίσθη, the lexeme meaning to be divinely instructed and no other token licensing the agent, even though θεόν is spent on 'God' earlier in the verse; 10:1 marks the second 'band' added under rule 103, σπείρης having already spent its English on the head of that same noun phrase. Both units name the competing reading.
- The ASV's supplied appositional 'even' with a carrier rule 105 does not name — a bare accusative noun (10:38, before Ἰησοῦν) or a dative pronoun whose only preposition is itself supplied (10:41, before 'to us') — is its own `added` unit, as rule 105's substantival-article carrier is. Rule 105 licenses the fold only where an expressed preposition introduces the appositive, its stated reason being that a case preposition rides with its token because it realizes that token's own case while 'even' realizes nothing; a bare nominal offers even less to ride on.
- ἐνώπιον is rendered two ways and the ASV's phrasing decides. Where it becomes the complex frame 'in the sight of' (10:31, 10:33) the frame is taken whole on the adverb including its trailing 'of' and headed on 'sight' under rule 71, and the article standing after it is `unrepresented` under rules 23 and 95, having no determiner left to anchor — REV.14.10's shape. Where the ASV renders it by the single word 'before' (10:30, and ἔμπροσθεν at 10:4) no frame question arises and the adverb takes that word alone.
