# 2 Timothy 3 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- προκόπτω with ἐπί and a comparative leaves ἐπί its own unrepresented unit throughout the chapter: 3.9 ἐπὶ πλεῖον → 'no further' under rule 34, the comparative's own gloss already spelling 'further'; 3.13 ἐπὶ τὸ χεῖρον → 'worse and worse' under rule 36, the ASV writing no English preposition anywhere and rule 94 forbidding a compound unit where more than one English word stands. Joining 3.9's pair as one aligned unit on 'further' under rules 35 and 94 remains defensible; the chapter took the split for both so that the same construction reads one way. Both tokens are BSB disagreements and neither report is a correction.
- A φιλ- compound adjective rendered by an English phrase heads on 'lovers', the word glossing the φιλ- element, in all five instances (3.2 ×2, 3.3, 3.4 ×2). This holds at 3.3 ἀφιλάγαθοι → 'no lovers of good' even though a privative negator stands: the head is neither the negator nor the completing 'good'. The two plain privatives of the same verse still head on their rule 84 nouns — ἄστοργοι → 'without natural affection' on affection, ἀκρατεῖς → 'without self control' on control — so the chapter is not reading 'without'/'no' as the meaning-bearing word in either family.
- In the καὶ … δέ idiom at 3.12 the assignment is functional, not positional: δέ is the sentence connective and takes the plain English 'and', while adjunctive καί emphasizing πάντες takes the emphatic 'Yea'. This follows 1JN.1.3, which carries the same idiom with the same pairing (both units hedged there). The competing reading — 'and' to καί as its ordinary gloss, 'Yea' to δέ — is defensible but crosses the two tokens for no gain.
- Where an English phrasal verb carries a trailing or leading particle and no Greek preposition stands for it to answer to, the particle rides inside the verb's own unit as a rule 7 helper rather than being marked added: 3.5 ἀποτρέπου → 'from … turn away' (discontinuous under rule 15) and 3.14 ἐπιστώθης → 'hast been assured of'. Where a Greek preposition is expressed it keeps its own English word, as 3.14 παρά → 'of' does in the same verse.
