# Judges 11 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Predicative LAMED before a predicate noun: where a written verb already carries the predication - HAYAH taking the English copula (11.6 *be our chief*, 11.8 *thou shalt be our head*, 11.9 *shall I be your head*) or SIM writing it as a bare object complement (11.11 *made him head and chief*, both LAMEDs) - the resultative LAMED is its own `unrepresented` unit and the predicate noun keeps only its own word. Rule 49's resultative frame reaches a LAMED only where no verb is written to carry the copula, and rule 21's ruling covers the passive recasting; this chapter's shape is neither. A dative LAMED standing beside it whose suffix the ASV recasts as a possessive determiner is unrepresented on the same terms, the suffix taking the determiner under rules 18 and 40.
- A particle standing inside an English verb phrase was tested by rule 44's subtraction against the bare English verb the ASV chose, not against the Hebrew stem. *turned again* (11.8), *bring home again* (11.9), *go back* (11.35), *took away* (11.13, 11.15), *drive out* (11.7), *went around* (11.18), *gathered together* (11.20), *passed over* (11.29, 11.32), *came out* (11.34), *offer it up* (11.31), *go down* (11.37) and *sent away* (11.38) all keep their particles, the bare verb not writing the whole lexeme. The *again* of *restore ... again* (11.13) is `added`: *restore* is *cause to return* entire, so nothing is left for the word to complete, exactly as rule 44 leaves the *back* of hiphil BO -> *bring him back*.
- Bare nominals after verbs of motion. BO takes no direct object in this chapter, so the supplied *to* before MINNIT (11.33) and HA-MITSPAH (11.34) is rule 33's word for a terminative place accusative and belongs to the name, headed there under rules 24 and 31. AVAR is the other case: 11.29 writes it twice with ET-marked objects and once with a bare one, so the *unto* before *the children of Ammon* is the preposition the English verb's valency needs to reach its own object and belongs to the verb under rule 44. Read which the Hebrew noun is, as rule 44's own closing limit directs.
- An appositional *even* was placed by what stands immediately after it. Before a bare nominal that no object marker and no conjunction stands on it is `added` (11.33, before *twenty cities*). Before a written preposition it is that preposition's expansion and the pair goes whole to it, headed on the relational word (11.36, MIN -> *even on*); rule 16 names *even unto*, *even to*, *even till*, *even for* and *even as*, and this chapter reads *even on* on the same terms, since the rule states the condition rather than a list. Where a conjunction morpheme stands before the preposition, rule 16's count gives the waw the bare *even* and the preposition the relational word alone (11.13, 11.22).
