# Leviticus 22 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A generic aish standing before a relative, a subordinator, or a second aish is fused or split by rule 30's one-English-word condition alone, and this chapter settles both sides of it. LEV.22.3 (kol aish), .5 (aish asher), .18 (aish aish) and .21 (aish ki) each go into one unit for a single Whosoever, headed on the morpheme carrying the lexeme; LEV.22.4 (What man soever) and .14 (a man ... if) split, because the ASV spends a word on each morpheme. Where the distributive doubling is split, the first aish carries the noun and the second carries the discontinuous What ... soever.
- In the formula Whosoever he be of X, the construction-supplied copular frame he be belongs to the min heading the prepositional predicate and not to the aish or kol unit that holds Whosoever (LEV.22.3, LEV.22.18); the same reading gives it be to the first lamed of the whether it be any of their vows phrase at LEV.22.18, while a clause with no preposition gives the frame to the predicate noun instead (LEV.22.28, it be cow). The competing reading attaches the frame to the subject expression.
- Every supplied have, hath or having in this chapter's blemish and uncleanness lists is added, because the Hebrew there writes bare nominals and passive participles and nothing makes the clause possessive: LEV.22.3 (where 'al has already taken upon), .22, .23 and .24. It goes to a morpheme only where one stands to carry it - the be of LEV.22.20, the existential ayin of LEV.22.13, the possessive suffix of LEV.22.5. LEV.22.23 was corrected to this reading, its hath having earlier been folded into the participle's rendering.
