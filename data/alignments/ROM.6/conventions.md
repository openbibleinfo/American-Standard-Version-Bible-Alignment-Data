# Romans 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A σύν-compound's associative English phrase goes to the expressed dative where the Greek writes one, and rides inside the compound's own unit where it does not. συνετάφημεν … αὐτῷ → We were buried … with him (6:4) and συζήσομεν … αὐτῷ → we shall … live with him (6:8) give with him to αὐτῷ under rule 106's oblique arm, the ASV writing buried and live for the verbs themselves; συνεσταυρώθη → was crucified with him* (6:6) keeps the whole supplied phrase inside the verb's unit headed on crucified, no dative standing to answer to the preverb. The same holds for a compound that is not a verb: the adjective σύμφυτοι → united with him* (6:5) keeps its supplied pronoun as a rule 7 helper headed on united, rule 106's test being whether another source token stands to answer to the word, not whether the compound is verbal.
- In the formula χάρις … τῷ θεῷ → thanks be to God (6:17) the supplied copula goes to the dative and not to the subject. χάρις is a lexical noun, so rule 53's subject-carrier exception is switched off by rule 63, and rule 56 does not reach the clause because τῷ θεῷ stands in it. θεῷ takes be to God headed on God, its article unrepresented and the case preposition riding with the noun under rule 21's determinerless clause, as 2CO.8.16 and 2CO.9.15 read the identical formula. Reading the dative as a benefactive adjunct, so that the clause has no predicate at all and thanks be forms one unit, remains defensible; that was the earlier reading here.
- Where the ASV writes a preposition's English twice — once as a separate word before the antecedent and once fused into a where- relative — the pair does not join under rule 35, rule 94's single-English-word precondition failing. The preposition takes its own word, the portmanteau is the relative's own unit whole, and the repeated element inside it is a rule 7 helper on the relative under rule 103's function-word arm rather than added. εἰς … ὃν … παρεδόθητε → to that form of teaching whereunto ye were delivered (6:17) gives to to εἰς and whereunto to ὅν, as ἐφ᾽ οἷς → in the things whereof (6:21) gives in to ἐπί and the things whereof to οἷς, headed on the nominal under rule 77.
- κυριεύω → have dominion over heads on dominion under rule 91, the corpus spending the noun and not the light verb have on that lexeme, and the over goes to the governed genitive as the case government the verb expresses: κυριεύσει … ὑμῶν → shall not have dominion over you (6:14) and κυριεύει … αὐτοῦ → hath dominion over him (6:9) give over you and over him to the pronoun, as the corpus's five over you units head on the pronoun. Reading over as licensed by the frame's own noun dominion, so that it stays inside the verb's unit under rule 21's support-frame clause and the genitive keeps a bare pronoun, remains defensible; that clause's own examples all govern accusatives, where no case stands for an English preposition to realize.
