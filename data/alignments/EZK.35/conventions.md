# Ezekiel 35 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה → *the Lord Jehovah* (35.3, 35.6, 35.11, 35.14). The noun אֲדֹנָי takes *the Lord*, its *the* being supplied with the noun under rule 29 since no article morpheme is present; MACULA's separately segmented 1cs suffix is `unrepresented`, the ASV writing nothing for it; and יְהוִה takes *Jehovah*. The four suffixes are BSB disagreements and are a documented convention difference, not errors.
- After the oath formula חַי־אָנִי נְאֻם אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה, the particle that opens the apodosis is `unrepresented`: the asseverative כִּי of 35.6 and the waw of וְעָשִׂיתִי in 35.11. The ASV writes no connective at that clause's head, and the *therefore* it does write answers to the לָכֵן standing at the head of the verse (35.6 writes a second *therefore* for the waw of וְדָם יִרְדֲּפֶךָ, so the connectives and the morphemes count out even). Both particles are BSB disagreements and neither is an error.
- Where a construct chain is rendered attributively under rule 28 and the ASV writes an indefinite article for the phrase — אֵיבַת עוֹלָם → *a perpetual enmity* (35.5), שִׁמְמוֹת עוֹלָם → *a perpetual desolation* (35.9) — the *a* joins the construct noun's unit under rule 15, which is therefore discontinuous around the attributive, and the nomen rectum takes only the attributive word (*perpetual*).
- כְּשִׂמְחָתְךָ → *As thou didst rejoice* (35.15) is read as rule 52's preposition-plus-recast-clause with the parts divided as rule 18 divides an infinitive construct and its suffix: כְּ takes the subordinator *As*, the verbal noun שִׂמְחָה carries the lexeme with the supplied auxiliary (*didst rejoice*, headed on *rejoice*), and its suffix takes the subject pronoun *thou*. Rule 52 already licenses the preposition's half for a nominal object; the division of the noun and its suffix is recorded here rather than promoted, because the chapter has one instance of it and the corpus evidence before this pass did not reach the construction.
