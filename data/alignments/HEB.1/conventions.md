# Hebrews 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A supplied English subject pronoun belongs to an expressed Greek subject token, and a finite verb takes one only where the Greek expresses no subject. 1:5 αὐτός → 'he' beside ἔσται → 'shall be', 1:10 Σύ beside ἐθεμελίωσας, 1:11 αὐτοί/σύ and πάντες → 'they all', 1:12 σύ → 'thou' beside εἶ → 'art', against 1:2 ἔθηκεν → 'he appointed', 1:5 εἶπεν → 'said he', 1:6 εἰσαγάγῃ → 'he bringeth in', 1:9 ἠγάπησας → 'Thou hast loved', 1:13 εἴρηκεν → 'hath he said'. English interrogative inversion does not license the other reading: 1:14's 'Are they not all' gives 'Are' to εἰσίν and the discontinuous 'they … all' to πάντες, rules 53 and 63 having refused word order as a test.
- πρός + accusative in the formula introducing a quotation is rendered 'of' by the ASV and takes that word itself under rule 36: 1:7, 1:8 and 1:13 all give the first 'of' to the preposition. It is not the partitive of the following genitive article, which keeps its own 'of the' headed on 'the' at 1:5 and 1:13 — 'of which of the angels' spells both.
- Predicative εἰς — the Septuagintal εἰς + predicate accusative of the 2 Sam. 7:14 citation — is its own unrepresented unit, twice in 1:5. The ASV writes no preposition for it anywhere: the dative αὐτῷ/μοι takes 'to him'/'to me' and the anarthrous predicate noun its own supplied 'a' under rule 51's anarthrous clause, so rule 36's absorbed-into-a-predicate clause leaves the preposition empty. A reference comparison will report both tokens as disagreements, and that report is not a correction.
- ποτέ → 'at any time' is one unit headed on 'time', at 1:5 and 1:13 alike. 'any' is the competing head on the analogy of rule 69's οὐδένα → 'any man', and was rejected because ποτέ is itself temporal, so 'time' glosses the lexeme rather than completing it (rule 66). This does not carry to πάλαι → 'of old time' at 1:1, which heads on 'old' because that is the word glossing that lexeme; the test is lexeme-by-lexeme.
- The supplied 'he saith' at 1:8 is the chapter's one added unit. The Greek of 1:8 gaps its verb of speaking from 1:7's λέγει, which is already spent on that verse's own 'he saith', so no token of 1:8 licenses it. Rule 37 is not the case — the Greek verb is translated in its own verse, and this is ellipsis rather than a versification difference — and rule 103's refusal to fold repeated lexical content onto a token whose English is spent is what decides it.
