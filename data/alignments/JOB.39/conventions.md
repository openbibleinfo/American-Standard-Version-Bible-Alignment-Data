# Job 39 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Clefted questions (JOB.39.26, JOB.39.27). Where the ASV clefts a question, the interrogative particle takes the single fronted 'Is' as both question auxiliary and copula (rules 17, 20); the dummy 'it' is its own added unit, headed on itself, because rule 20's cleft sentence heads the dummy on a copula that here belongs to the particle; and the cleft relative 'that' is added under rule 55. Read the clause the relative opens before placing it: a kiy or other subordinator standing at the head of a further coordinated clause is not what it answers to, and where the ASV writes no word at that clause's own head the subordinator is unrepresented. The corpus aligns an interrogative particle to 'is it' as one unit at EZK.20.3 and HAB.2.13; that competing reading was named and rule 20's added-dummy sentence followed.
- A supplied 'when' before an infinitive construct the ASV recasts as a finite clause stays inside that infinitive's unit and is never added, whichever morpheme licenses it. Where the infinitive is the nomen rectum of 'et, the construct relation licenses it (rules 19, 52) - JOB.39.1 s004, JOB.39.2 s007. Where no 'et and no preposition stands, the word is part of the infinitive's own recasting, as rule 46 keeps a supplied relative with a recast nominal - JOB.39.1 s007, 'canst thou mark when the hinds do calve'. Rule 55's added subordinator governs a clause the Hebrew writes, and an infinitive construct is not one.
- Rule 31's tie-reading for this chapter's animal names: the ASV's class noun carries the lexeme and the modifier beside it is the ASV's specifier, so 'the wild ox' heads on ox (39.9, 39.10), 'the wild ass' and 'the swift ass' on ass (39.5), 'the wild goats' on goats (39.1) and 'the quivering mane' on mane (39.19). Where the English noun is instead a generic the ASV supplied to complete the phrase, the same subtraction moves the head the other way: 'young ones', 'the open field', 'the salt land', 'dwelling place', 'the armed men' and 'threshing floor' all head on the modifier.
- A preposition the ASV supplies before a second bare noun is placed by rule 33's closing test, and the chapter falls on both sides of it. Where the English predicate admits no other word, the preposition is the verb's under rule 44: 'hast thou clothed his neck with the quivering mane' (39.19) and 'hath deprived her of wisdom' (39.17). Where the word states what the noun contributes and another would have served, it is the noun's under rule 33: 'with his band' (39.10) and 'On the cliff' (39.28), where sela' carries no preposition in the Hebrew at all.
- Supplied possessives are split by what they stand on. On a noun the Hebrew writes, a supplied possessive is added under rule 18's closing sentence: 'his might' (39.19, italic) and 'his strength' (39.21). Inside a light verb's supplied complement that English cannot write without one, it belongs to the verb's rendering as rule 30 keeps the pronouns of a particle's idiomatic formula: 'maketh her home' for yitlonan (39.28), headed on home under rule 44. The competing reading - 'her' added under rule 18's closing sentence - is named in that unit.
- The chapter's three renderings of shuv are divided by rule 44's subtraction rather than by the shape of the English verb phrase, and one of them departs from that rule's own example. 'They go forth and return not again' (39.4) leaves 'again' added, because 'return' has already written the returning; 'neither turneth he back' (39.22) and 'he will bring home thy seed' (39.12) keep their particles, because 'turneth' and 'bring' alone do not.
