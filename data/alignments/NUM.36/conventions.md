# Numbers 36 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The copular marriage formula הָיָה (+ subject) … לְנָשִׁים / לְאִשָּׁה → *be married*, *were married*, *shall be wife* is divided the same way throughout: הָיָה takes the copula together with whatever subject pronoun, modal or jussive frame the ASV writes (*they be*, *Let them be*, *shall they be*, *were*, *They were*, *shall be*), נָשִׁים or אִשָּׁה takes the lexical *married* or *wife*, and the לְ is its own unrepresented unit under rules 21 and 40, the recasting leaving nothing for its relation. Verses 3, 6 (twice), 8, 11 and 12. BSB represents that לְ; the difference is convention, not error.
- אִישׁ rendered by a distributive English phrase heads on the nominal word and not on *every*: *every one* heads on *one* in vv. 7 and 9 and *every man* heads on *man* in v.8, since *every* is the distributive the phrase adds and the other word is what stands for the noun (rules 24, 31). The competing reading heads all three on *every*; it was declined so that the three units head alike whichever English noun the ASV chose. A written כֹּל is a different case and takes the *every* itself, as in v.8's כָל־בַּת.
- The ASV italic *houses* in *the fathers houses* (v.1, both occurrences) is aligned into its own אָבוֹת unit as the generic supplied to complete that substantive's sense under rule 31, headed on *fathers*, rather than marked added on the strength of the italic (rule 7). The competing reading treats it as a supplied head noun under rule 20's supplied-lexical boundary and marks it added; it was declined because the phrase renders one Hebrew substantive and no event is predicated by the supplied word. Both occurrences are read the same way, and the chapter's one added unit is the italic *other* of v.3, where no morpheme stands for the word at all.
