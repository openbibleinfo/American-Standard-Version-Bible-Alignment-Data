# Luke 22 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- The fixed temporal phrase 'heos hotou' / 'heos hou' rendered by a bare 'until': the conjunction takes 'until' and the relative pronoun is its own unrepresented unit (22.16 s009/s010, 22.18 s015/s016). 'until' is already the conjunction's own direct gloss, so rule 34's shape applies rather than rule 45's, and the pair is not joined into one noncompositional unit. Reading the pair as one noncompositional 'until' remains defensible; a reference comparison reports the relative as a disagreement, and that report is not a correction. The demonstrative construction is not this case: 22.51 'heos toutou' -> 'thus far' splits under rule 94, 'far' to the adverb and 'thus' to the demonstrative.
- 'apo tou nyn' -> 'from henceforth' is three units, not one (22.18 s007-s009, 22.69 s001-s003): the ASV writes two English words, so rule 94's precondition for a compound prepositional unit fails, 'apo' keeps 'from', 'nyn' takes 'henceforth', and the article contributes no part of the phrase and is unrepresented under rule 93. The corpus's single-word 'henceforth' precedent for 'apo tou nyn' does not reach a two-word rendering.
- The adverb 'semeron' rendered by the two-word 'this day' is one aligned unit headed on 'day' (22.34 s009, 22.61 s023), the noun carrying the lexeme's temporal sense and 'this' being the supplied deictic completion. Do not head it on 'this' by analogy with a demonstrative: no demonstrative token stands in the Greek.
- A Greek article before an attributive participle is decided by whether the ASV supplies a separate antecedent pronoun for it, not by the Greek shape. The chapter's parallel institution clauses settle it: 22.19 's015 'to ... didomenon' -> 'which is given for you' gives the relativizer and copula to the participle and leaves the article unrepresented under rule 31, while 22.20 s019 'to ... ekchynnomenon' -> 'that which is poured out for you' gives the supplied 'that' to the article under rule 32 and 'which is poured out' to the participle. Same construction, one analysis: the article recovers a word only where the ASV writes one for it.
- Where a verbless or inverted clause's predicate is a substantival article plus a participle or adjective, the chapter puts the supplied copula on the article rather than on the participle: 22.27 s010 'ouchi ho anakeimenos' -> 'is ... he' with 'that sitteth at meat' left to the participle, and 22.26 s006 'ho meizon' -> 'he that is the' with a bare 'greater' left to the adjective. Rule 96 permits it and rule 32's 'and nothing else' is the competing reading. Where the predicate is a preposition, an adverb or an anarthrous adjective the ordinary rule 53 analysis stands and that word takes the copula (22.21, 22.26 s004, 22.27 s003, 22.38, 22.49).
- A particle whose only work is to mark a direct question, and which the ASV realizes with no word because it marks the question by inversion alone, is its own unrepresented unit throughout the chapter: 'me' at 22.35, 'ara' at 22.23, and the conditional 'ei' at 22.49 ('ei pataxomen en machaire;' -> 'shall we smite with the sword?'). Rule 64's test is the particle's work and never its morph tag, so a token tagged COND falls under it exactly as a PRT-I does. An ordinary conditional 'ei' or 'ean' is untouched and is aligned to 'if' (22.42, 22.67, 22.68).
