# Matthew 10 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- `ἕως ἄν` is one `aligned` unit taking the ASV's single *till*, headed on *till* (10.11, 10.23). Rule 50 forbids an `unrepresented` `ἄν` and rule 39's test makes the unit `aligned` because *till* answers to `ἕως`; the absence of a `-soever` is not licence to empty the particle. Every `ἄν` in the chapter is represented — compressed into *whatsoever* or *whosoever* at 10.11, 10.14, 10.33 and 10.42, and into *till* here.
- An article before a noun the ASV determines with a demonstrative is its own `unrepresented` unit under rule 38, and the case preposition then falls to the noun under rule 21's determinerless clause: 10.15 `τῇ πόλει ἐκείνῃ` → *for that city*, 10.19 `ἐν ἐκείνῃ τῇ ὥρᾳ`, 10.23 `τῇ πόλει ταύτῃ`, 10.42 `τῶν μικρῶν τούτων`. 10.14 is the sole exception and the reason is countable rather than a different analysis: `ἔξω τῆς οἰκίας ἢ τῆς πόλεως ἐκείνης` → *out of that house or that city* writes two English *that*s for one Greek demonstrative, so the first is the article's own under rules 60 and 76 and carries *of* with it under rule 21, which leaves the improper preposition `ἔξω` a bare *out* as rule 101 requires. The second article is emptied by rule 38 as everywhere else. The competing reading — one discontinuous `ἐκείνης` over both English *that*s, emptying both articles and restoring *out of* whole to `ἔξω` — is named in the verse's own note.
- The Semitic `ἐν` of `ὁμολογήσει ἐν ἐμοί` and `ὁμολογήσω ἐν αὐτῷ` (10.32) is its own `unrepresented` unit. The ASV renders the construction as a plain transitive *shall confess me* / *will I also confess him* and says so in its own margin (*Greek in me*, *Greek in him*), so no English word answers to the preposition and rule 34's pleonastic case governs. Rule 94's single-English-word precondition is satisfied, but rules 35 and 45 are not the model: *me* and *him* answer wholly to the pronoun and no part of either to the preposition. A reference comparison reports both tokens as disagreements and that report is not a correction.
- Where one Greek verb is rendered by an English frame in which no single word glosses the lexeme, the head goes on the meaning-bearing complement and never on the copula, auxiliary or light verb: `ἐγγίζω` → *is at hand* heads on *hand* (10.7, following rule 71's settlement of the cognate `ἐγγύς` → *is at hand* on *hand*), `διχάζω` → *to set at variance* on *variance* (10.35), `διαφέρω` → *are of more value* on *value* (10.31), `ποτίζω` → *give to drink* on *drink* (10.42), `θανατόω` → *cause … to be put to death* on *death* (10.21). None of these lexemes has a corpus entry for rule 91's query to return, so each was decided from the root gloss or the cognate's settled head, and each verse's note names the competing head.
- `τῶν μικρῶν τούτων` → *these little ones* (10.42) leaves the article `unrepresented`. Rule 38's demonstrative takes the determiner, and with no determiner left for the article to anchor, rule 102 does not reach the supplied nominal *ones*, which stays inside the substantival adjective's unit headed on *little* under rule 17. The same verse's anarthrous `ψυχροῦ` → *of cold water* is analysed identically, which is rule 24. Reading rule 102's *determiner plus supplied nominal* as reaching an article rule 38 has emptied remains defensible and would give `τῶν` a discontinuous *of … ones*.
