# Luke 14 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- μήποτε: a bare μήποτε takes the ASV's whole supplied frame *lest haply* as one aligned unit headed on *lest* (14:8, 14:12). Where ἵνα stands in front of it, rule 40 gives ἵνα the word that introduces the English clause and μήποτε keeps *haply* alone (14:29). The competing reading — ἵνα unrepresented and *lest haply* whole on the particle — is recorded in that verse's note and was refused because rule 40 empties a subordinating conjunction only under rule 37.
- συν- compounds: where the Greek expresses the dative complement, the English preposition realizes the case government and rides with that dative under rule 106's dative arm — συνανακειμένων σοι → *sit at meat* and *with thee* (14:10), Συνεπορεύοντο … αὐτῷ → *there went* and *with him* (14:25). Where the Greek expresses no dative, no token stands to carry it and the whole *with him* rides inside the participle's own unit: τῶν συνανακειμένων → *that sat at meat with him* (14:15).
- Supplied verbal arguments are folded onto the verb that licenses them rather than marked added, rule 11 making the ASV's italics evidence only: *wherewith* onto ἔχω in both places it stands (14:14, 14:28), following rule 74's object contrast, which the contract itself records only as medium; *them* onto ἀνάγκασον (14:23); *men* onto βάλλουσιν (14:35), whose person and number license the supplied subject.
