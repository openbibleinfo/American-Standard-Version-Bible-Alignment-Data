# 1 Timothy 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- Finite-clause recasts: this chapter assigns the supplied relativizer and the supplied copula separately, and the test is whether any token in the phrase has verb-bearing English. Where none does, the relativizer stays on the article and the copula still splits onto the predicate under rule 53: 6:3 tei ... kat' eusebeian -> to the ... which on the article with is according to on kata; 6:19 tes ontos -> on the ... which on the article with is indeed on the adverb; 6:17 Tois plousiois -> them that on the article with are rich on the predicate adjective. Where a token's own English carries a verb form, that token takes relativizer and copula together and the article keeps only its determiner or its antecedent pronoun: 6:15 ho ... dynastes -> the on the article and who is ... Potentate on the expressed predicate noun (rule 92); 6:20 ten paratheken -> that on the article and which is committed unto thee on the noun, whose own English is the relative clause.
- A Greek compound rendered by an English phrase heads on the word realizing the compound's own head element, applied uniformly: 6:3 heterodidaskalei -> teacheth, 6:4 logomachias -> disputes, 6:10 philargyria -> love, 6:13 zoiogonountos -> giveth, 6:18 agathoergein -> do, 6:20 pseudonymou -> called. Where the content element is the complement rather than the verb word, the same test moves the head there: 6:10 apeplanethesan -> astray, 6:12 and 6:19 epilabou/epilabontai -> hold. 6:13 and 6:18 name the competing reading in their notes; 6:3 and 6:19 do the same for their own.
- An English particle in a phrasal rendering stays inside the verb's unit wherever no Greek preposition is expressed for it: 6:7 carry ... out, 6:10 have pierced ... through and reaching after, 6:11 follow after, 6:20 turning away from. 6:20's from is the one to note, since the Greek object is a plain accusative and no case preposition is owed. A genuine oblique case preposition still rides with the article that takes the determiner and is not read as part of a phrasal verb: 6:12 and 6:19 tes ... zoes -> on the, against reading lay hold on as one phrasal unit.
