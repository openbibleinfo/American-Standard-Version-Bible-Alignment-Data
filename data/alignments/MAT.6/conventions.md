# Matthew 6 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- A genitive or dative complement keeps the English preposition that realizes its case, even where the ASV strands it and even where an expressed Greek noun standing beside it would license the same English word: ὧν χρείαν ἔχετε → *what things ye have need of* puts *of* on ὧν and leaves χρείαν a bare *need*, as χρῄζετε τούτων ἁπάντων → *of all these things* puts it on ἁπάντων and ἀνθέξεται … ἑνός → *hold to one* puts *to* on ἑνός. Rule 21's support-frame exception is scoped to a supplied frame noun over an accusative and reaches none of these; an accusative object is the contrast and leaves the preposition inside the verb, as ἐπιζητοῦσιν → *seek … after* does.
- A supplied English verb in a clause the Greek left elliptical: where the clause writes a subject and nothing else, the verb folds onto that token — ὥσπερ οἱ ἐθνικοί → *as the Gentiles do* gives *do* to ἐθνικοί (rules 56, 68). Where the ASV instead supplies a whole predicate with its own subject and every standing token has already been given its own English, the supplied words are `added` — 6:30's *shall he clothe* resumes ἀμφιέννυσιν, spent on *doth … clothe* in the protasis, over an οὐ, a πολλῷ, a μᾶλλον and a ὑμᾶς that each keep their own word (rules 62, 103).
- Where the ASV expands a Greek adverb into a multiword frame containing a supplied generic noun, the head is the word that glosses the adverb's own stem, not the frame's noun: Οὕτως → *After this manner* heads on *this*, the οὑτ- stem being what *this* answers to and *manner of* being the same supplied frame rule 99 never heads on. σήμερον → *this day* and *to day* head on *day* instead, because that Greek word carries a day-element for *day* to gloss and the *this* or *to* is the supplied word. Rule 71's πέραν → *on the other side* and ἐγγύς → *at hand* are not the contrast: there no other English word glosses the lexeme, so the noun is what renders it.
- English do-support belongs to the verb whose tense, number and person it carries, never to the negator standing beside it, which keeps only its own *not*: 6:20 *do not break through*, 6:26 *do they reap*, 6:28 *do they spin*, 6:30 *doth … clothe* and 6:32 *do … seek* all keep it inside the verb's unit under rule 9, discontinuously across the negator where the ASV separates them, and rule 17 heads each on the verb word rather than on the auxiliary.
