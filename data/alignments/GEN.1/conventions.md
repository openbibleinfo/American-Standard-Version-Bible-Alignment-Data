# Genesis 1 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in `AGENTS.md` once the chapter is finished.

- **GEN.1.1** — A supplied English article before an anarthrous Hebrew noun belongs to that noun's unit, not to 'added'. In the beginning heads on 'beginning'.
- **GEN.1.1** — The direct-object marker אֵת is its own unrepresented unit throughout, including when it carries a maqqef.
- **GEN.1.2** — In a construct chain, 'of' belongs to the nomen rectum and heads on its noun; the construct noun takes only its own English word plus any article English requires.
- **GEN.1.2** — In a verbless clause the supplied copula joins the predicate that licenses it, not the subject; it is never 'added'.
- **GEN.1.3** — Existential היה takes its dummy subject 'there' and any 'Let' from the jussive into the verb's own unit, headed on the lexical verb.
- **GEN.1.4** — ASV renders the frame bein X u-bein Y (or bein X le-Y) as 'X from Y': the second connective carries English 'from', the first bein and any linking waw are unrepresented.
- **GEN.1.5** — The lamed marking what a thing is called, and any other preposition ASV does not render, is its own unrepresented unit; the article fused into it stays a separate token and takes English 'the'.
- **GEN.1.6** — Periphrastic haya plus participle splits: the finite verb takes the English auxiliary and subject, the participle takes the lexical verb and heads on it.
- **GEN.1.7** — In a compound preposition such as mi-tachat le- or me-al le-, only the head preposition is rendered; the min element and the governing lamed are their own unrepresented units, and a supplied copula joins the rendered preposition.
- **GEN.1.9** — A Hebrew substantive that ASV expands into adjective plus generic noun (yabbashah -> 'dry land') is one unit headed on the word carrying the lexeme, here 'dry', matching the Greek 'all things' -> 'All' rule.
- **GEN.1.11** — Where English fuses several Hebrew morphemes into one word ('wherein' for asher + be- + suffix), they share a single unit headed on the morpheme that carries the clause type; 'added' is reserved for English with no licensing morpheme at all, such as the italicized 'and' coordinating an asyndetic list.
- **GEN.1.14** — A Hebrew article that ASV does not render is its own unrepresented unit, as in 'the firmament of heaven' for ha-shamayim.
- **GEN.1.16** — A repeated attributive article (ha-meorot ha-gedolim) has no English realization and is its own unrepresented unit, as in Greek.
- **GEN.1.21** — An article prefixed to a participle is what makes the clause relative in English ('that moveth'); align it to the relativizer and head the participle's own unit on the verb.
- **GEN.1.22** — In lemor the lamed is unrepresented and the infinitive alone carries 'saying'.
- **GEN.1.27** — A pronominal suffix carries any English intensifier written with it ('his own'), and the object marker that hosts a suffix is unrepresented while the suffix itself is the pronoun.
- **GEN.1.21** — The head of an expanded substantive is whichever English word carries the Hebrew lexeme, which is why yabbashah heads on 'dry' and remes on 'creeping' but tannin heads on 'monsters': 'sea' is a specifier ASV adds, not the lexeme.
