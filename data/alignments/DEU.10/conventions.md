# Deuteronomy 10 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- kĕ's compound renderings are each read as one rendering of the single preposition under rule 30 and headed on the first word: *like unto* (10.1, 10.3), *according to* (10.4), *as at* (10.10, 10.15). The second English word is neither split off onto the following article or noun nor marked added, because dropping it leaves no morpheme short of a rendering of its own. A bare *as* (10.22) is the same preposition where the ASV happened to write one word.
- kaʾăšer is one noncompositional unit headed on the governing preposition whether the ASV writes *as* (10.5) or the two-word *according as* (10.9); the two-word rendering is not split onto kĕ and ʾăšer.
- Determiner counts in demonstrative phrases: where a demonstrative pronoun (hîʾ, zeh, ʾēlleh) stands in the phrase, it takes the one determiner the ASV writes and every article in the phrase — the noun's own and the agreement article alike — is unrepresented (10.1, 10.8, 10.10, 10.15, 10.21). Where no demonstrative morpheme stands, the article itself takes the written *this*: haYYôm → *this day* (10.13). The chapter writes both shapes, so read the phrase rather than the English.
- Possessive and belonging clauses take three shapes here, decided by which morpheme is present. hāyāh present takes *hath* itself and its lĕ goes unrepresented (10.9). A verbless belonging clause gives *unto ... belongeth* whole to the lĕ heading the predicate, headed on *unto* and discontinuous around the subject (10.14). And where the clause's only preposition is a locative bĕ that has already taken its own *in*, the supplied *having* answers to no morpheme and is added, the waw the ASV leaves unrendered going unrepresented beside it (10.3).
- *nor* rests on the waw alone at 10.9, where the clause's single lōʾ is spent on the determiner *no* of *no portion*, and on the fused waw+lōʾ at 10.17, where a second negative is written. Both are correct; the difference is what the Hebrew writes, and neither should be levelled to the other.
