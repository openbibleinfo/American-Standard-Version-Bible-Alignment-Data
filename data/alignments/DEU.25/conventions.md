# Deuteronomy 25 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- לְ before a construct body-part noun is fused or split by what the ASV writes for the pair, and this chapter writes both shapes: `לְעֵינֶיךָ` → *unto thee* (25.3) is one unit on the preposition and עַיִן, headed on the construct noun under rule 23, while `לְפָנָיו` → *before his face* (25.2) and `לְעֵינֵי` → *in the presence of* (25.9) are split under rule 2, each morpheme holding the word written for it. The suffix keeps its own pronoun in all three, and the construct noun is never marked `unrepresented` beside a preposition holding the whole English word.
- The levirate verb יָבַם is rendered by a light verb plus complement — *perform the duty of a husband's brother* — headed on *duty*, which is the word whose loss loses the lexeme; *brother* was the competing head and is named in both verse notes. The supplied *unto* by which that English phrase reaches the verb's object suffix belongs to the verb's unit and the suffix keeps only its pronoun (25.5, 25.7).
- The distributive doubled noun `אֶבֶן וָאֶבֶן` / `אֵיפָה וְאֵיפָה` → *diverse weights* / *diverse measures* is one `noncompositional` unit across both nouns and the waw, headed on the first noun under rule 6's deterministic anchor and on the English noun. The competing split — the first noun taking *weights*, *diverse* going to the waw, the second noun `unrepresented` — was rejected because no morpheme owns *diverse* in particular and assigning it to the conjunction is arbitrary (25.13, 25.14).
- A possessive clause built on the negative existential is rule 43's verbless case, not rule 50's frame: `אֵין־לוֹ` → *have no son* gives *no* to אַיִן, which heads on the negation word, and *have* to the possessive לְ, which is what makes the clause possessive (25.5). Where הָיָה is written instead, the verb takes the *have* itself and the לְ is `unrepresented` (25.13, 25.14, 25.15 twice). The suffix takes the subject pronoun in either shape unless the ASV carries the subject over from a preceding clause, as at 25.5.
