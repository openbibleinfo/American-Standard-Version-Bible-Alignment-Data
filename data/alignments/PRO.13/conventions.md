# Proverbs 13 alignment conventions

Chapter-wide judgments, recorded as they were made. Read this before continuing an interrupted chapter or starting a review range. Conventions that generalize belong in the testament contract under `config/` once the chapter is finished.

- An English *it* in a verbless clause is placed by what it stands for. The anaphoric *it* of `וְעֵץ חַיִּים תַּאֲוָה בָאָה` → *when the desire cometh, it is a tree of life* (13.12) realizes `תַּאֲוָה`, which the verse writes and the ASV has already rendered *the desire*, and joins that noun's discontinuous unit under rule 25, the predicate keeping only *is a tree*. The anticipatory *it* of `תוֹעֲבַת כְּסִילִים סוּר מֵרָע` → *it is an abomination to fools to depart from evil* (13.19) fills a subject slot before an extraposed infinitival subject, denotes nothing the verse names, and is `added` under rule 20, the copula going to the nominal predicate. Read what the pronoun stands for, not the shape of the English clause; the two look alike.
- A supplied possessive is `added` under rule 18's closing sentence even where its referent is written elsewhere in the verse: *his father's instruction* (13.1), *his folly* (13.16), *his children's children* (13.22) all leave the *his* `added`, because no possessive morpheme stands on the noun and rule 25's resumption reaches a pronoun realizing a referent's own syntactic slot, not a determiner supplied on some other noun. Contrast 13.2, where `אִישׁ` is itself the rectum of `פֶּה` and the ASV pronominalises it while promoting the same noun to subject: there *A man ... his* are both realizations of that one written morpheme.
- A preposition the ASV supplies before a bare Hebrew nominal belongs to that nominal under rule 33 wherever the recasting leaves it an adjunct — *shall be to him that refuseth* (13.18), *recompensed with good* (13.21, where the passive makes the person the subject and `טוֹב` an adjunct of means), *is in the tillage* (13.23) — and to the verb under rule 44 only where the English verb reaches its own object through it, as *leaveth an inheritance to his children's children* (13.22). Ask what the nominal is in the English clause after the recasting, not what it was in the Hebrew.
